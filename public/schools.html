Get your number

A fifth absence triggers help, not a letter.

Mycelium gives each family at your school a helper by text, in their language, that works on whatever keeps the child home. You pay a quarter of the attendance funding it recovers, nothing up front.

Today,
a letter.

It is already in your records. With Mycelium it triggers an offer from the school instead: a helper that finds out what is actually in the way, the bus, the Medi-Cal renewal, the shutoff notice, the IEP meeting, and does the thing, with the family’s yes on each action.

The thread stays with the family, under FERPA’s school-official rules. You see attendance and patterns in aggregate, like forty families on one cut bus route; never a reason, and never another institution.

    First we read how you’re funded.

    Your LCAP and your attendance file, line by line: every dollar that depends on a child being in a seat. The number comes first, free.

    Then it rehearses on last year.

    Before it texts anyone, it runs on last year’s records. You see who it would have reached and what it would have said, and you decide when it’s ready.

    You offer it. The family says yes.

    A fifth absence sends an offer from you, in the family’s language. They say yes or they don’t. Your attendance team sees only the cases that need a person.

    You pay when attendance moves.

    Days recovered against each student’s own prior year, rolled out school by school and measured against the schools not yet offered. Our share is a quarter of the funding recovered; the rest stays with you.

    Who was offered a helper, from your own records, and whether attendance improved. Patterns in aggregate. Every rule the helper follows, in writing, before anything goes live. A monthly report your board can read.

    What you see.

    The address, the eviction notice, the immigration question, the sick grandparent. Any message, unless the family chooses to share it. Anything about a family that didn’t say yes.

    What you never see.

    The California Student Data Privacy Agreement before we see a single record. FERPA’s school-official rules and CA-NDPA. Student data is never used to train models and never leaves to another institution.

    What we sign first.

    Measured
    for your board.

    Chronically absent students improve on their own some years. The measurement is built so that doesn’t count as ours.

    Each student against their own prior year, not against a district average.

    Offered school by school, so every month there are schools not yet offered to compare against.

    Reported monthly: families reached, in which language, how many answered, days recovered by school, funding recovered by line.

    If attendance doesn’t move, you pay nothing. There is no platform fee and no minimum.

    Why help works where letters don’t.

    • A brochure about financial aid did nothing. Sitting with the family to finish the form raised college enrollment 8 points.Bettinger, Long, Oreopoulos & Sanbonmatsu, randomized, 26,000 families
    • A letter saying “you qualify” doubled benefit enrollment. A person who filed it tripled it.Finkelstein & Notowidigdo, randomized, 31,000 seniors
    • Personal messages to families recover attendance for $5 to $11 a day, twice as much for families who speak another language.Robinson, Lee, Dearing & Rogers, randomized, California K–5
    • One text helper handled 200,000 messages. Fewer than one in a hundred needed a person.Georgia State University, randomized, 3,100 students

    No one has run the trial where one helper, the family’s own, does all four. Yours would be it: offered school by school, measured against the schools not yet offered, so by June you know.
