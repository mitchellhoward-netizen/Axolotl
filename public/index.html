Your budget lives in a you can't see.

A school is funded by attendance. A benefit fund pays premiums the state would cover. Both are decided in people's homes, where no institution can reach. Mycelium gives each person a helper: an AI that texts with them in their language, paid for by the institution, working for them.

The school sees attendance improve. It never sees the thread.

A child's fifth absence is already in the school's records. Today it triggers a truancy notice. With Mycelium it triggers an offer from the school, in the family's language: a helper that works on whatever is keeping the child home, because the real barrier is usually something the school has never seen.

The school pays only when attendance improves, measured against each student's own prior year and rolled out school by school so the gain is real. Nothing up front.

The helper is theirs.

The institution pays for it because its budget moves. The person uses it because it works for them. Those two things only line up if the helper is honest about whose side it's on, so we built it to be the person's.

It helps with anything.

The bus, the Medi-Cal renewal, the shutoff notice, the IEP meeting, the form the county never explained. Not just the one thing the institution is paid on. The barrier to that one thing is usually something else anyway.

It answers to them.

It never pressures a child to school when she's sick. It never tells the school a family's reasons; the school gets patterns in aggregate, never anyone's story. It says no to the institution before it says no to the family, and that rule is written down where both can read it.

They can say no.

Nothing is filed, called or sent without a yes on that exact action. Stop, and it stops. Decline the offer, and the school hears nothing but that.

It's already in their pocket.

It arrives by text message or WhatsApp, whichever the family already uses. Nothing to download, no portal, no login to forget. Your staff get nothing new to run either.

It stays with them.

A school sponsors it first. When a union fund, a clinic or a college later sponsors the same person, the helper is already there, already trusted, already knows the situation.

We call the gap this closes stranded alignment: outcomes an institution and a person both want, that nobody pursued because helping one person at a time cost more than anyone could capture. The best-run programs give one case manager 110 to 125 students, so a caseworker for every family was never affordable. And help, not information, is what works: in the largest experiment on this, eight minutes of hands-on help with a financial-aid form raised college enrollment by eight percentage points, while a brochure with the same information did nothing. A helper makes that eight minutes affordable for everyone. The institution pays because its number moves; the person gets help they could never have bought.

First we read how you're funded.

Your funding formula and your records, line by line. We find every dollar that depends on something in a person's life: a district's attendance, a fund's Medicare premiums. The number comes first, free.

Then it rehearses on last year.

Before the helper texts anyone, it runs on last year's records. You see who it would have reached and what it would have said. You correct it and decide when it's ready.

You offer it. The family says yes.

A fifth absence, or a member turning 65, sends an offer from you in their language. They say yes or they don't. Your staff see only the cases that need a person.

You pay when the outcome moves.

Attendance days recovered against each student's own prior year, or premiums the state now pays instead of you. Rolled out school by school, so you can see the gain before you scale it. Our share is a quarter of what's recovered; the rest stays with you.

Think of health insurance. Your employer pays for it, the doctor works for you, and your employer benefits when you're well without ever seeing your chart. Same arrangement, now affordable for every family and every retiree.

Works for the family.

The school sees who was offered a helper, from its own records, and whether attendance improved. It sees patterns in aggregate, like forty families on the same cut bus route, which is a policy it can fix, but never anyone's reasons. And it sees every rule the helper follows, in writing, before anything goes live.

The school sees outcomes.

It never sees the address, the eviction notice, the immigration question or the sick grandparent. It never sees a message unless the family chooses to share it, and nothing about a family that didn't say yes. This is enforced in the system, not in a prompt. Student data stays under FERPA's school-official rules and California's CA-NDPA, and is never used to train models.
