When families do better, schools do too.

Every day a student misses, the school loses funding. Every family that never enrolls in food assistance or Medicaid is a student the school isn’t paid for. Mycelium gives every family a helper, sponsored by their school: an AI agent that works for them, by text, in their language. Families get what they qualify for. 8:30 AMFirst bell

Every empty seat is money.

Attendance gets taken, and in California and Texas, schools are paid by the day. A family that never enrolls in the programs it qualifies for is a student the school isn’t paid for. What a school is funded for reaches deep into family life. What its staff can do stops at the classroom door.

10:15 AMThe front office

Everyone wants the kid in school. It’s nobody’s job.

The parent wants it. The teacher wants it. The school is paid for it. We call this stranded alignment: outcomes a school and its families both want, that never happen because reaching them costs more than anyone can spend. Each case is small and sits between offices. A typical school case manager carries 110 to 125 students.

Letters home

Tell families what to do. They reach everyone and change little, because information alone rarely moves anyone.

Coordinators and case managers

Do the work with families, and it works. But one person can only carry so many.

A helper for every family

Does the work with each family, as often as it takes, for every student. That’s new.

What changed: in one experiment, eight minutes of help filling out the federal student aid form raised college enrollment from 34% to 42%. Information alone did nothing. An AI agent can now spend those eight minutes with every family.

Case manager loads: a study funded by the National Institute of Justice. Student aid form: the H&R Block experiment by Bettinger, Long, Oreopoulos and Sanbonmatsu, summarized by J-PAL.

6:30 PMDinner

The helper belongs to the family. The school sponsors it.

It works for the family, on anything making life harder, and on the school’s goals only where the family agrees. Personal AI would otherwise reach families the way financial advisors did: the ones with the most friction last. Schools have what it takes to change that.

  1. A reason to payThe school loses money when these outcomes don’t happen
  2. The recordsIt can see who needs help before they ask
  3. TrustFamilies already read what the school sends
  4. PermissionOffices turn away agents that show up uninvited. The one the school sponsors gets let in
