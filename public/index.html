The school system wasn’t built around the parent.

There are programs to help families. There are people whose job is to help. There are rules about what schools provide.

But knowing something exists isn’t the same as getting it.

Axolotl helps families get from “something’s wrong” to “it’s taken care of.”

Staying at a cousin's house counts

Under McKinney-Vento, that student keeps their school and gets a ride to it. Axolotl makes the claim and chases the route.

An evaluation has a clock on it

Ask under IDEA and the district has a legal deadline. Axolotl puts the request in writing and tracks the date.

An interpreter is not a favor

Title VI requires the school to communicate with you in a language you understand. Axolotl asks for it before the meeting, not during.

Same for meals, accommodations, and school stability. You describe the situation; Axolotl handles which rule applies.

The help exists. Getting to it is the hard part.

School data systems are usually stateless. An absence or a missed meal fires a notification, but nothing connects it to what’s actually going on, and nobody closes the loop to make sure the support reaches the family. When a family tells Axolotl what’s happening, the need becomes clear — and Axolotl does the work to actually resolve it. Many students experiencing housing instability never enter the systems designed to support them.

It runs the other way too

No family says “we need a Tier 2 attendance intervention.” They say their kid hasn't wanted to go to school. Axolotl surfaces the problem before the school has a name for it, which is earlier than any screener can catch.

What districts finally see

Which barriers are most common, which services are hardest to reach, where requests stall, how long need-to-support takes, and how far the support they intend sits from the support families receive. Nobody can currently answer that last one.
