The gap

The help exists.
Getting it is the hard part.

Schools have programs to help families, rules about what they have to provide, and people whose job is to help. Knowing that something exists is not the same as getting it. Picture day, the book fair, the fundraiser, a spirit week, and one thing with a deadline on it. The one that matters is buried under the ones that do not.

Axolotl reads the school email you forward it, works out what applies, and hands you one short list of what actually needs you.

Some of this is not a favor. It is the law.

If your child is staying somewhere temporary, McKinney-Vento keeps them at their school and gets them a ride to it. If you ask for an evaluation, IDEA puts a deadline on the district. An interpreter is not a courtesy: Title VI requires the school to talk to you in a language you understand. The same goes for meals, accommodations, and school stability. You describe the situation; Axolotl works out which rule applies, and puts the request in writing.

What it does

Read it, answer it, file it, chase it.

Reading, answering, paperwork, and follow-through. Most tools pick one. The school work needs all four, in order.

  1. 01

    It reads.

    Forward your school email and it separates the seven messages that are only information from the one that needs something from you, with the deadline attached.

    One list instead of a firehose.
  2. 02

    It answers.

    Ask about your school or your district and it researches the real answer: the bus route, the meal program, what enrollment actually requires, and who to contact.

  3. 03

    It does the paperwork.

    It fills the form, drafts the email to the office, or places the call. You see exactly what it will send, and it waits for your yes before anything goes out.

  4. 04

    It follows through.

    A form that has to be returned, a deadline next week, a reply you are still waiting on. It keeps track, and it tells you when something is genuinely finished.

    Done means the school confirmed it.

Axolotl is new. It works best with a school that communicates by email, and it will tell you when something falls outside what it can do.

What it will not do

The limits, before
you find them yourself.

The honest list. The rest of this page only means something if this part is true, so it is the part we would want you to read first.

It cannot see what your child is already receiving. Meal balances, attendance, what services are in place: that lives in the school's portal, and Axolotl can only read it if you sign in yourself. It will not guess, and it will not tell you something it has not seen.

It will not send anything without your yes.

Every email to your school, every form, every submission waits for an explicit yes from you. A suggestion is not permission, and it is not treated as one.

It will tell you what it cannot open.

A sign-in wall, a bot check, a form that only exists on paper, a district that wants a phone call. It names the wall it hit instead of stalling or guessing.

It will not pretend a form went through.

A submission is only reported as done when the school's own confirmation comes back. If it cannot confirm that, it says so and gives you the link rather than telling you it is handled.

It cannot see your school portal until you sign in.

It does not have your password and it will not ask you for one. If reading the portal matters to you, you sign in yourself, and it only reads.

Where it is strong, and where it is not.

Reading and researching: very good. A clean form it has handled before: reliable. A complicated new form on a district site: it may hand you the link rather than pretend. We would rather do that than tell you something that is not true.

It is not a doctor, a lawyer, or the school.

It helps you navigate the process and get the paperwork done. Decisions about your child stay with you, your school, and your providers.

Your family's information is yours. It is never sold. Nothing goes to your school without your OK. The only system that reads your messages is the AI that writes its replies.
