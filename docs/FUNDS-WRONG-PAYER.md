# Where a union fund pays when someone else should

October 2026. A companion to `FUNDS-MONEY-MAP.md`: every case I could find
where a union health and welfare fund pays a bill, or a benefit, that another
payer owes first.

Each line says:

- who should pay
- the rule
- whether a member has to act to fix it

That last point matters most. Where the member has to apply or enroll, the
work is ours. Where it's a claims recovery, other firms already sell it.

Sources are inline. *Inferred* marks my reading, to check against a fund's
plan document and data.

## A. Medicare should pay first

The Medicare Secondary Payer rules decide who pays first. A fund pays first
for active workers. Medicare pays first for most people whose coverage is not
based on current work.
([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coordination-benefits-recovery/overview/secondary-payer))

**1. Retirees 65+ (and their spouses 65+) not enrolled in Part B**
- **The rule:** Medicare is primary for retirees.
- **When the fund overpays:** if the retiree never enrolled in Part B, a fund
  whose plan doesn't "assume" Medicare enrollment ends up paying primary.
  Whether this costs the fund depends on the plan's wording. *(inferred)*
- **Member action:** enroll in Part B on time.

**2. COBRA and self-pay members who have Medicare**
- **The rule:** COBRA is not current employment, so Medicare pays first for
  an aged or disabled member on COBRA.
  ([Newfront](https://www.newfront.com/blog/how-cobra-and-medicare-interact-for-retirees))
- **When the fund overpays:** when it doesn't know the member has Medicare.
- **Member action:** report Medicare, or enroll in it.

**3. Members who stopped working because of a disability**
- **The rule:** this covers members on a disability pension, an hour-bank
  extension or COBRA.
  - After a Social Security disability (SSDI) award, Medicare starts in month
    24 and pays first for anyone not actively working.
  - **ALS:** Medicare starts the same month SSDI does, with no 24-month wait.
    ([Medicare Interactive](https://www.medicareinteractive.org/?p=4655))
- **One nuance:** for *active* disabled members, a multiemployer plan stays
  primary if any contributing employer has 100+ employees.
  ([HHS MSP model](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/sites/default/files/hhs-guidance-documents/MSP-Model-for-Multi-Employer-Multiple-Employer.pdf))
- **Member action:** apply for SSDI, then Medicare.

**4. Dialysis after month 30**
- **The rule:** the fund is primary for the first 30 months of Medicare
  eligibility based on kidney failure. After that, Medicare pays first if the
  member is enrolled.
  ([Medicare Interactive](https://www.medicareinteractive.org/wp-content/uploads/30-month-coordination-period-factsheet.pdf))
- **The money:** commercial plans spend $238,126 in a patient's first year of
  dialysis. Medicare spends $80,509.
  ([JAMA Netw Open via Fierce](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/238216-80509-157707-thats-how-much-more-commercial-insurers-pay-1st-year-dialysis-medicare))
- **Guardrail:** never steer inside the 30 months.
- **Member action:** have Part B start at month 31.

**5. Kidney transplant drugs**
- **The rule:** Medicare covers immunosuppressive drugs after a
  Medicare-covered transplant, including through Part B-ID once other
  Medicare ends.
- **When the fund overpays:** if the member isn't enrolled. *(inferred,
  check plan)*
- **Member action:** enroll.

## B. The state, or Medicare's subsidies, should pay

**6. Part B premiums the fund reimburses for retirees who qualify for a
Medicare Savings Program**
- **The rule:** the state pays the premium.
  - New York: up to $2,474 a month for one person, no asset test (2026).
    ([NCOA](https://assets.ncoa.org/ffacfe7d-10b6-0083-2632-604077fd4eca/78306116-4908-4802-ae2f-67968427bb55/2026_MSP_Coverage_and_Eligibility.pdf))
  - About 4 in 10 people who qualify aren't enrolled.
- **Member action:** apply.

**7. Part B reimbursed twice**
- **The rule:** a fund should stop reimbursing when the premium is already
  paid by someone else, whether another former employer or the state through
  a Medicare Savings Program.
- **Evidence:** Nassau County found 94 retirees and dependents paid twice,
  $1.6M in total. The rule says reimbursement stops when "the Medicare premium
  is being paid by another entity."
  ([Nassau Comptroller](https://www.nassaucountyny.gov/DocumentCenter/View/53259/Review-of-Medicare-Reimbursements))
- **Why it's ours:** every Medicare Savings Program approval we win has to
  stop the fund's reimbursement, or the fund just pays twice. That's built
  into our ledger.

**8. Retiree drug costs for members who qualify for Extra Help**
- **The rule:** in an employer drug plan (EGWP), CMS pays the plan low-income
  premium and cost-sharing subsidies for those members. Every Medicare
  Savings Program enrollee qualifies automatically.
  ([Milliman](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/Medicare-Advantage-EGWPs-Riding-the-Baby-Boomer-wave))
- **Member action:** apply, or get it through #6.

## C. Another insurer should pay first

**9. Work injuries: workers' comp pays first**
- **The rule:** most plans exclude job injuries. They get paid by the fund
  anyway when the provider bills health insurance.
- **Evidence:** research finds workers' comp costs shifting onto group health.
  ([CDC/NIOSH](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/194065))
- **Where it matters:** in construction trades, this may be the biggest leak
  that isn't Medicare. *(inferred)*
- **Member action:** answer "did this happen at work?" Today that question
  goes out on a paper questionnaire.

**10. Car accidents: no-fault (PIP) pays first**
- **The rule:** in New York, no-fault pays the first $50,000 of medical bills
  from a car accident. Health coverage is secondary.
  ([Albany Med](https://www.albanymed.org/?p=73843))
- **Member action:** answer "was this a car accident?", and the claim has to
  be filed within 30 days.

**11. Other liability: subrogation**
- **The rule:** the fund recovers from whoever caused the injury.
- **Evidence:** plans recover only about 0.2–0.3% of claims this way.
  ([DOL subrogation report](https://dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ebsa/pdf_files/trends-and-practices-in-healthcare-subrogation.pdf))
- **Who does it today:** a crowded vendor market. It's a claims-side job.

**12. A spouse with their own job coverage**
- **The rule:** a spouse covered as a dependent by the fund, who also has
  coverage through their own job, gets that job plan as primary. For children
  covered by both parents, the "birthday rule" decides which plan pays first.
- **When the fund overpays:** when the other coverage is never reported.
- **Member action:** report the other coverage. A text question asked at
  enrollment and at life events does this.

## D. Nobody should pay: the person isn't eligible

**13. Ineligible dependents**
- **The cases:** an ex-spouse after divorce, a child past 26, a child who was
  never eligible.
- **The leak:** 3–8% of covered dependents, at $1,900–3,500 a year each.
  ([Chard Snyder / Mercer](https://www.chard-snyder.com/employers-and-advisors/compliance-watch/does-your-health-plan-need-a-dependent-eligibility-audit))
- **Who does it today:** TPAs run audits.
- **Our version:** catch the life event when it happens.

**14. People who have died**
- **The leak:** coverage, premiums or Part B reimbursements continuing after
  death. Auditors flag plans that don't verify retirees are living.
  ([NY OSC audit](https://osc.state.ny.us/localgov/audits/schools/2018/plainview-old-bethpage.htm),
  [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/mlbenebits/2024/03/dealing-with-the-dearly-departed-in-multiemployer-defined-benefit-plans))
- **Who does it today:** a data-match job, so claims-side.

**15. Eligibility lost, claims still paid**
- **The leak:** an hour bank runs out, or self-pay lapses, and claims keep
  paying until a retro termination.
- **Who does it today:** claims-side.
- **Where we help:** the member side can catch it with a text when hours
  drop: the self-pay offer, or Medicaid / premium help (#17).

## E. A public cash benefit should come first

**16. Disability benefits that wait on, or are offset by, Social Security or
state disability**
- **The rule:** many multiemployer pension plans require an SSDI award before
  paying a disability pension.
  ([NCCMP](https://nccmp.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/NCCMP-Comment-to-DOL-on-Proposed-Rule-For-Claims-Procedures-for-Disability-Benefits-1-19-2016.pdf))
- **The leak:** some fund weekly or long-term disability benefits are reduced
  by SSDI or state disability, such as California SDI or New York DBL. Members
  who never apply cost the fund the offset. *(inferred, check plan
  document)*
- **Member action:** apply.

**17. Medicaid paying the premium (HIPP)**
- **The rule:** states must pay employer or COBRA premiums for
  Medicaid-eligible people when it's cost-effective.
  ([Texas HHS](https://www.hhs.texas.gov/node/94591))
- **Who gains:** this keeps self-pay members covered and paying, rather than
  the fund losing them. A member and retention win more than a direct
  savings.
- **Member action:** apply.

## F. Plan design: the fund's choice, member enrollment is ours

These are trustee decisions made with consultants. The hard part of each is
moving members, which is where we fit.

**18. Pre-65 retirees**
- **The idea:** some sponsors move early retirees to the ACA marketplace with
  an HRA or a stipend.
- **The cost today:** premiums averaged $12,000+ per pre-65 retiree a year.
  ([Business Insurance](https://www.businessinsurance.com/more-employers-move-pre-medicare-retirees-to-exchanges-high-deductible-plans/))
- **The catch:** retirees who keep an HRA can't also take a premium tax
  credit.
- **Where we fit:** enrolling each retiree.

**19. Medicare retirees**
- **The idea:** move from a self-funded plan that pays after Medicare to a
  group Medicare Advantage / EGWP plan, which CMS subsidizes.
  ([Milliman](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/Medicare-Advantage-EGWPs-Riding-the-Baby-Boomer-wave),
  [Amwins](https://www.amwins.com/docs/default-source/taft-hartley/egwp-vs-rds-sell-sheet-2017-union.pdf))
- **Where we fit:** enrollment help and the transition.
