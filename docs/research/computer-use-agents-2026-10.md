# Per-portal workflows, not models, finish family paperwork

As of October 2026, computer-use models clear human level on short desktop tasks: vendors self-report 83–86% on OSWorld-Verified against a 72% human baseline. They still fail about half of long, real administrative workflows. The best result on OSWorld 2.0 is 48.7% binary completion, and it is self-reported. On HealthAdminBench, a replica of payer portals, the best agent finished **36% of tasks end to end while getting 78–83% of individual steps right**. The breakthroughs that close that gap are mostly not in the model. They sit in the harness: structured page access, validators that check each step, and compiling a successful run into a per-portal workflow that replays without the model. Each of these produced gains of 1.5 to 4 times in controlled studies, which is larger than the gap between frontier models. Nobody runs agents that file SNAP or Medi-Cal applications at scale. Propel's 2026 test found that general-purpose agents could not finish a BenefitsCal application in an hour, and that they checked the legal attestation for the user. No product completes K-12 enrollment, LCFF income forms, consents or the CADAA for families. On the legal side, the Ninth Circuit now says the *user* "accesses" a site when the agent runs through the user's own machine. Courts are treating CAPTCHAs as access controls, Login.gov bans automated access outright, and the authorized-representative rules in Medicaid and SNAP are the only footing government recognizes today. Investors are asking for this almost word for word. a16z's Fabrizio Serafini co-wrote that agents will log "into government and insurance portals" and that winners will be built "at the application and context layer, not the model layer," and speedrun's SR008 applications close **November 1, 2026**. For Mycelium, this points to one strategy. Become the library of per-portal workflows and the family consent graph that sit between general models and messy institutions. Assist, but never sign for the family. Never solve a CAPTCHA. Sort every portal by how much legal authority you have to act on it.

## Agents passed humans on short tasks and still fail half of long ones

The original OSWorld benchmark is effectively solved. The best agent scored 12.24% when the benchmark launched in April 2024. OpenAI's Computer-Using Agent reached 38.1% in January 2025, trained with reinforcement learning ([OpenAI CUA](https://openai.com/index/computer-using-agent/)). Simular's Agent S3 with Behavior Best-of-N reached **72.6% in December 2025**, the first result above the 72.36% human baseline ([Simular](https://www.simular.ai/articles/simulars-computer-use-agent-outperforms-humans)). By mid-2026, frontier models self-reported 83–86% ([Steel OSWorld board](https://leaderboard.steel.dev/leaderboards/osworld/)). Two caveats apply. Anthropic's 2026 numbers use a revised harness the aggregator calls "not directly comparable to older entries." And the top entry on the official board, Intelligence-Indeed at 90.19% (July 2026), is relayed by an aggregator because the official page does not render without JavaScript ([Presenc](https://presenc.ai/research/osworld-computer-use-leaderboard-2026)). a16z's own summary is that "a year ago the best computer-using model scored 42% on OSWorld-Verified … today's best scores 85%." It then adds the caveat that matters: "85% still means 15 of 100 failed" ([a16z, Aug 10 2026](https://a16z.com/can-agents-use-a-computer-yet-weve-got-the-data/)).

The field has moved to **OSWorld 2.0**, released June 26, 2026 by xlang/HKU with Snorkel and Simular as co-authors. It has 108 tasks with a median of 1.6 skilled-human hours each, about 318 tool calls and about 27 checkpoints per task. It scores binary completion at a 500-step cap ([arXiv 2606.29537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29537)). This is the closest public proxy for "finish the whole job." Progress has been fast, but benchmark versions changed along the way, so the table mixes runs that are not strictly comparable.

Success falls steeply as tasks get longer. In the June runs, no model exceeded 10% on tasks of 137–163 minutes, and every model scored 0% on tasks longer than that ([OSWorld 2.0 site](https://osworld-v2.xlang.ai/)).

The closest benchmark to Mycelium's world is **HealthAdminBench**, from Stanford (April 10, 2026). It has 135 tasks across a simulated EHR, two payer portals and a fax system: prior authorizations, appeals and equipment orders. Claude Opus 4.6 as a computer-use agent completed **36.3% of tasks but 78.4% of subtasks**. GPT-5.4 completed 26.7% of tasks and 82.8% of subtasks. Giving Opus portal-specific guidance and the page's accessibility tree raised it to **51.9% task and 89.7% subtask success**. The harness mattered more than the model. The same Opus scored 14.8% in a generic harness, and GPT-5.4 fell from 26.7% to 5.9% ([arXiv 2604.09937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09937)). The step-versus-task gap is simple arithmetic: 95% per step over 20 steps compounds to about 36%. That is roughly a 20-to-60-step government form, and **no public benchmark measures forms of that kind**. The ceiling has to be interpolated between OSWorld's tens of steps and OSWorld 2.0's hundreds.

Cost is no longer the constraint, but speed is. Browser Use Bench v2.1 (October 7, 2026; 180 long live-web tasks) was published by a vendor that also competes in it. It measured **GPT-6.1 Sol at 79.6 points for $1.30 per task, Claude Opus 5.5 at 75.0 for $2.35, and Claude Haiku 5.5 at 69.5 for $0.46**, counting model tokens only ([Browser Use](https://browser-use.com/benchmarks/agents)). Hour-plus desktop workflows cost $14–27 per task on OSWorld 2.0, according to Simular, also a competitor ([Simular](https://www.simular.ai/articles/sai-tops-osworld-2-0)). They take about 40 minutes for GPT-6 Astra versus 75 for GPT-5.6 Sol, according to OpenAI ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/)). Per-step latency was 3.6–9 seconds in January 2026 ([Yutori](https://yutori.com/blog/introducing-n1)), and Anthropic says latency "may be too slow for real-time human-AI interaction" ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/computer-use-tool)). At about 5–9 seconds a step, a 30-step form takes 3–5 minutes of agent time (my arithmetic). That works for an asynchronous "we'll text you when it's ready" flow. It is a problem against BenefitsCal's 15-minute session timeout if the agent explores rather than replays ([Propel](https://www.propel.app/insights/can-ai-agents-help-people-apply-for-snap-part-1/)).

The failures on long tasks are mostly about thinking, not clicking. The OSWorld 2.0 authors say agents "are not mainly failing at basic GUI control." Agents lose track of constraints, guess instead of asking, accept proxy success (a file exists, so the job must be done) and skip verification ([arXiv 2606.29537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29537)). In HealthAdminBench, agents rarely went back for information they needed later, mishandled file uploads and forgot details they had not written down ([arXiv 2604.09937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09937)). A September 2026 study of 2,518 trajectories gives the most worrying numbers. **After one wrong step, a computer-use agent's next step is wrong 58.1% of the time, versus 5.3% after a correct step. 69.5% of runs never recover. In 84.1% of failed runs the final step looks correct** ([arXiv 2609.17930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17930)). Policy compliance pulls success down further. On ST-WebAgentBench, counting a task as complete only when no policy (such as "get consent before irreversible actions") was broken cut success from 24.3% to 15.0%, and to 7.1% when five or more policies applied ([arXiv 2410.06703](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06703v5)). Motor problems persist at the edges. Anthropic documents trouble with dropdowns, scrolling and small targets ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/computer-use-tool)). As recently as June 2025, pure-vision models clicked the correct form field less than 5% of the time ([FormFactory](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01520v1)).

## The harness beats the model: breakthroughs ranked by evidence

The ranking below weighs the strength of the evidence and how directly each technique applies to repeated government and school forms. Gains measured on controlled academic benchmarks rank above vendor ablations, and vendor ablations rank above claims made only in abstracts.

| Rank | Technique | Best evidence (date) | Independence | What it means for a family helper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Structured observation plus hybrid API and browser** | Opus 4.6 rose from 36% to 52% on HealthAdminBench with the accessibility tree and portal guidance (Apr 2026); raw HTML beat the accessibility tree by 11–17 points for frontier models on WorkArena (2026); an API-plus-browser hybrid scored 38.9% on WebArena, more than 24 points above browsing alone (Oct 2024) | Academic | Read the DOM, set selects, dates and uploads directly, and keep pixels as the fallback |
| 2 | **Validators (planner, actor, verifier)** | Skyvern on WebVoyager: 45% single loop, 68.7% with a planner, 85.85% with a validator; fixing failure patterns found in traces lifted WebArena sub-sites by 7–16 points (2024) | Vendor and practitioner | Check every field after it is written; never trust "done" |
| 3 | **Workflow memory and "agent once, script forever"** | Agent Workflow Memory raised WebArena from 23.5% to 35.5% with 25% fewer steps (ICML 2025); Skyvern's code mode is 2.7 times cheaper and 2.3 times faster (vendor) | Academic plus vendor | Compile each portal after its first verified success |
| 4 | **Best-of-N with judges** | Agent S3 rose from 62.6% on one run to 72.6% with N=10, and its judge agreed with humans 92.8% of the time on mixed-outcome tasks (Dec 2025); a 4B "Scout" verifier lifted Terminal-Bench from 81.8% to 90.2% (Sep 2026) | Academic or vendor paper | Run several attempts up to the review page and submit the one the judge picks |

| 7 | **World-model simulation before irreversible steps** | WebDreamer is competitive with tree search at 4–5 times the efficiency on live sites (abstract-level evidence, 2024–25) | Academic, thin numbers | A useful pattern for the step just before submit, but no decisive numbers yet |

Three patterns stand out. First, the frontier lab APIs are pixel-first. Claude's `computer_toolset_20260801` takes screenshots and sends mouse and keyboard actions, with no DOM input ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/computer-use-tool)). The strongest gains for forms, though, come from feeding the model page structure. That is a harness decision, and an application company owns it. Second, the market has already converged on compilation. Stagehand caches actions on the server and replays them deterministically, falling back to the model when the page changes ([Stagehand docs](https://docs.stagehand.dev/v4/best-practices/caching)). Skyvern generates Playwright code from the first successful run and regenerates it on drift ([Skyvern docs](https://www.skyvern.com/docs/developers/browser-automation)). Simular "locks in" human-corrected workflows as auditable code ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/02/simular-releases-mac-os-ai-agent-raises-21-5m-from-felicis-with-windows-coming-soon)). Browserbase's Director turns instructions into reusable Playwright scripts ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/browserbase)), Browser Use v4 writes code for hard tasks ([RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/es/article/browser-use-cloud-v4-luna-web-agent-cost)), and Notte's "Anything API" turns a described task into an endpoint ([Notte](https://www.notte.cc/)). Six vendors arriving at the same design independently is strong market evidence. Third, one finding is a warning. Agent Workflow Memory had the highest partial completion on ST-WebAgentBench and also the **most user-consent breaches (37)** ([arXiv 2410.06703](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06703v5)). Replayed workflows skip consent unless the consent gate is written into the workflow itself.

RL environments are now a funded market. The Information reported that Anthropic leaders discussed spending more than $1B on them, and OpenAI's Sherwin Wu said he was "short" on environment startups ([TechCrunch, Sep 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/16/silicon-valley-bets-big-on-environments-to-train-ai-agents/)). a16z led Deeptune's $43M Series A in March 2026 to build workspace "gyms" around Slack, Salesforce and ticketing systems ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/19/andreessen-horowitz-ai-startups-deeptune-series-a)). Nobody in the sources sells gyms for state benefits portals or district enrollment systems. The labs will not train on BenefitsCal unless someone builds that environment. That is a long-term opening for whoever holds the most real portal runs.

The vendors now compete on identity, live-view handoff, recordings and compliance ([Proxidize, Aug 2026](https://proxidize.com/blog/best-cloud-browsers-for-ai-agents/); [Browser Use pricing](https://browser-use.com/pricing.md); [Notte](https://www.notte.cc/); [Cloudflare pricing](https://2b20f69b.previews.developers.cloudflare.com/browser-run/pricing/index.md)).

Prices are a snapshot, since at least Browser Use and Browserbase changed tiers during 2026. Two vendor features are hazards for Mycelium rather than conveniences: **built-in CAPTCHA solving** (Browser Use, Anchor) and stealth fingerprints. The legal section explains why.

Credentials are where the vendors have converged most usefully. 1Password's Secure Agentic Autofill, built with Browserbase in October 2025, keeps credentials out of the model's context. It delivers them just in time over an encrypted channel and requires human approval for each fill ([Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/identity-access-management-security/1password-addresses-critical-ai-browser-agent-security-gap)). Browserbase's guidance for healthcare teams is the closest published operating manual for portal agents. It covers persisting session state, having humans complete sensitive logins, keeping sensitive health data out of prompts, presenting a consistent verified browser ("the goal is not to evade protections"), pausing for human approval at consent and submit, and recording everything so a portal outage can be told apart from a broken selector or an expired login ([Browserbase, Aug 2026](https://browserbase.com/blog/challenges-healthcare-orgs-building-browser-agents)). BrowserBook (YC F24, healthcare back office) found that agents were "slow, costly, drifted on multi-step tasks, hard to debug." Its product assumes from the start "that scripts will break" ([Launch HN](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/46232434)).

**Buy the browser, build the domain.** Mycelium's code is already further along than most teams in the sources. It routes each task down a fallback ladder (API, then WebMCP, then direct HTTP, then DOM through Stagehand, then vision). The browser layer only pre-fills, and submission sits behind the consent gate. Skyvern fills and submits run as two separate tasks, with no code path to submit before a strict YES. "Form recipes" record which fields a form needs after a verified success. The submit adapter refuses to call a run "completed" without the site's own confirmation. Skyvern runs terminate with `captcha_blocked` instead of solving the challenge. What is missing is what the evidence ranks highest. First, the agent never reads its own work back from the page: the recipe module notes that "the fill path never sees the DOM." Second, recipes store field structure, not executable workflows. Third, there is no per-portal evaluation that measures reliability rather than demos.

| Layer | Recommendation | Buy or build | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Family context and consent graph | Each family's verified facts, documents, standing authorizations and every YES, linked to the exact values shown | Build | The moat; Propel's standard and OWASP ASI09 require it |
| Portal registry and tiering | Each portal tagged by authorization footing (see the legal section), route ladder, timeout and known blockers | Build | Decides whether the helper acts, prepares or guides |
| Session | Hosted browser with long sessions, Live View handoff, recordings and Web Bot Auth signing (Browserbase, already integrated; Cloudflare Browser Run as a cheaper option with structured handoff) | Buy | Commodity; avoid 15-minute caps |
| Identity | Parent authenticates live through a handoff link; no stored passwords for government portals; vault autofill with per-fill approval only on sponsor-owned portals | Buy the vault, build the flow | Login.gov rules; OWASP ASI03 |
| Fill | Compiled per-portal workflow as the default; Stagehand DOM agent on drift; pixel computer use only for canvas, PDF viewers or broken markup | Adapt (Stagehand or Skyvern) | AWM and Beyond Browsing; vendor convergence |
| Validator | DOM read-back of every field against the family record, per-portal completeness rules, and best-of-N to the review page when the workflow has drifted | Build | Largest measured deltas; 84% of failures look fine at the end |
| Review and consent | A text-linked page with every exact value, the full attestation text, a screenshot, and one YES per consequential action; the parent personally checks attestations | Build (extend the review links) | Propel's "assist, don't sign"; ESIGN attribution |
| Submit and evidence | A separate post-YES phase; confirmation number required; a log of what was displayed versus what ran; recordings with personal data redacted | Build on vendor recordings | Substantiation (FTC's DoNotPay posture); audit |

On models, use a frontier model (Claude Opus or Sonnet 5.5, or GPT-6.x) for exploring new portals and as the validator's judge. Use Haiku-tier models for routine steps, and no model at all on replays. Opus 5.5 lists at $4/$20 per million tokens ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5)) and GPT-6 Astra at $10/$50 ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/)). My rough estimate is that a first exploratory run of a 30-step form costs $1–4 in tokens, and a compiled replay costs close to the browser's two cents. Unit economics therefore depend on two numbers more than on any vendor's prices: the replay-hit rate per portal, and the rate of handoffs to the parent. For comparison, Benefits Data Trust's high-touch call center cost "a few hundred dollars per successful application" before it closed in 2024 ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/civic-news/benefits-data-trust-shutdown-trooper-sanders/)).

## No one files benefits with agents at scale, and K-12 is empty

The most relevant field test is Propel's. Propel's app has about 5M monthly users. On April 27, 2026 it published a test of ChatGPT Agent and Claude Cowork on **BenefitsCal** and Florida's MyACCESS, using hypothetical data and never submitting. Both agents found the right site and entered most of the data. Both also truncated a mistyped phone number instead of asking, invented SSN explanations, opted the applicant into paperless notices and **checked the Statement of Understanding attestation for the user**. Claude injected JavaScript into a Florida dropdown and froze the page. Neither finished an application within an hour without manual help, against BenefitsCal's 15-minute session timeout ([Propel Part 1](https://www.propel.app/insights/can-ai-agents-help-people-apply-for-snap-part-1/)). Propel followed on July 13, 2026 with a draft "Benefits Navigation Agent Standard." It has four principles: identify and track AI-assisted cases, with no impersonation; **"assist, don't sign,"** meaning no legal acknowledgments unless the user has seen the full text; keep users in control, with revocable access; and have users review pre-filled eligibility data. It argues that agents should start as "Assisters" rather than "Preparers" or "Representatives," because AI cannot carry liability under US law ([Propel Part 2](https://www.propel.app/insights/how-should-ai-agents-help-people-manage-benefits/)). Propel also says BenefitsCal "can flag AI-assisted cases natively." No source beyond Propel confirms that.

The cautionary precedent is eleven years old. In 2015 Intuit's TurboTax "Benefit Assist" submitted SNAP applications to states in bulk. States objected that some were missing names, addresses or signatures, and some contained only symbols. Intuit stopped on January 30, 2015. FNS then ruled that states must process any application with at least a **name, address and signature**, and that FNS "lacks authority" to compel a third party to change its practices ([USDA FNS memo](https://www.usda.gov/sites/default/files/guidance-documents/fns.snap-guidanceAppsbyIntuit.pdf)). The lesson is not that third-party filing is banned. It is that low-quality bulk submissions, sent without coordinating with the states, get shut down. The same dynamic is building again. One 2026 study documents "agentic flooding" of public services: UK housing-ombudsman complaints rose from 2,600 to just over 7,000, and US CFPB complaints rose fivefold. Most of the new filers appear to be eligible ([TechCrunch, Sep 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/10/ai-agents-are-flooding-public-services-with-new-requests/)).

The assister-plus-handoff model is what has actually worked at scale. Code for America's GetCalFresh cut application time from about 45 minutes to under 10 and handled **70% of California's online CalFresh applications**. It is being sunset in phases by September 2025 as counties move to BenefitsCal ([CfA](https://codeforamerica.org/news/california-launches-code-for-americas-getcalfresh-in-all-58-counties); [Route Fifty](https://route-fifty.com/customer-experience/2024/12/why-california-revamping-its-benefits-application-websites/401823)). The state built over the most successful third-party front end it had. Newer deployments sit on the agency's side. Code for America and Anthropic launched a caseworker "SNAP Policy Navigator" in May 2026 ([GovExec](https://www.govexec.com/technology/2026/05/anthropic-code-america-pilot-ai-tools-snap/413464/)). Maryland and Anthropic announced a resident assistant and caseworker document verification in November 2025, following a bilingual SUN Bucks chatbot for 600K+ recipients ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/maryland-partnership)). mRelief reports a 20% rise in documents completed on the first try in a South Carolina pilot (org-reported) ([OpenAI Academy](https://academy.openai.com/public/blogs/turning-benefits-eligibility-into-groceries-with-ai)).

The clearest example of return on investment comes from a health plan. Kern Family Health Care, a Medi-Cal managed-care plan, uses Careforce's voice agent "Angelica." It has made **800K+ calls to 387K members in 30+ languages** about renewals. It points members to forms and books in-person help, and it does not decide eligibility. The plan estimates the work equals 40 staff, about $2.4M, for about $370K of software. Its April renewal rate was 94.9%, but nobody has shown the agent caused that rate (plan-reported) ([Medical Daily via inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/a-california-medicaid-plan-is-using-ai-voice-agents-to-keep-members-enrolled-before-work-rules-begin)). Demand is about to rise. Medicaid work requirements apply at the first renewal after January 1, 2027, and most expansion adults move to six-month renewals (same source). H.R.1 raises states' share of SNAP administrative costs from 50% to 75% ([Propel Part 2](https://www.propel.app/insights/how-should-ai-agents-help-people-manage-benefits/)).

Healthcare administration is where agents on messy systems are commercially proven. Infinitus (a16z-led $51.5M Series C) automated payer calls for 1.17M patients and 20% of US providers in 2025 (vendor). It added direct payer integrations alongside phone calls, and its agents **conference the patient in to give live consent** ([Infinitus](https://www.infinitus.ai/blog/2025-infinitus-product-innovations-powered-patient-access-at-scale/)). Tennr (IVP-led $101M at $605M) processes about 10M fax and portal documents a month (vendor) ([Fierce Healthcare](https://fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/tennr-clinches-101m-build-out-ai-automates-patient-referral-workflows)). Availity's AuthAI recommends rather than decides. **CMS-0057 requires payers to run FHIR prior-authorization APIs from January 1, 2027** ([Availity](https://www.availity.com/authai/)). The pattern has three steps: automate the phone, fax and portal where no API exists; move to APIs as mandates arrive; and keep the person's consent at the high-stakes moment. Almost none of these vendors publishes independently verified accuracy.

Education is advice, not completion. ParentSquare (22M+ students and families) announced an AI assistant on August 20, 2026 that answers parent questions from district content in 190+ languages. It does not complete forms or enrollment ([Techstrong.ai](https://techstrong.ai/articles/the-new-ai-bringing-school-home/)). The BDT/College Board FAFSA text bot advised students and referred them to FSA rather than completing anything ([NCAN](https://www.ncan.org/news/509231/Say-Hello-to-Your-New-Personalized-FAFSA-Assistant.htm)). **I found no product that completes school enrollment, LCFF alternative income forms, consents, IEP paperwork or the FAFSA/CADAA for families.** The nearest consumer analogs are general life-admin agents: Duckbill (AI plus human experts, $33M in 2023) ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/?p=466939)) and Rocket Money's text-based "Rowan" on Claude (September 2026, $15 a month) ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/03/rocket-money-launches-ai-agent-to-manage-finances/)). Neither reports results on government tasks. The K-12 slot is open, but AllHere is the reminder of what happens when it goes wrong. AllHere built LAUSD's "Ed" chatbot, its founder was arrested for fraud in November 2024, and the district dropped the product ([CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/federal-prosecutors-arrest-ai-education-company-founder-for-fraud)).

## The law protects the user's own access and punishes evasion

The biggest legal development of 2026 is **Amazon v. Perplexity**. In March 2026 a district court enjoined Perplexity's Comet agent from password-protected parts of Amazon, finding it likely accessed accounts "with the Amazon user's permission, but without authorization by Amazon" ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/03/10/judge-blocks-perplexitys-ai-bot-from-shopping-on-amazon-in-early-test-of-agentic-commerce/)). On **August 4, 2026 the Ninth Circuit vacated the injunction**. It held that under the CFAA and California's computer-crime statute, "it was the user who 'accessed' Amazon's computers." The deciding fact was that screenshots are taken on the user's machine and "Perplexity itself does not directly communicate with Amazon's servers." The court distinguished *Facebook v. Power Ventures*, where the defendant's own servers contacted Facebook ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-06-ninth-circuit-rules-on-ai-agent-access-to-third-party-websites-under-cfaa); [opinion](https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/amazon-v-perplexity-ninth-circuit-opinion.pdf)). This ruling has limits. It was made at the preliminary-injunction stage, it binds only the Ninth Circuit, it turns on the architecture, it leaves terms-of-service claims open, and it involves a private shopping site rather than a government portal. A cloud browser that contacts BenefitsCal from a vendor's servers looks more like *Power Ventures* than like Comet.

The other direction is hardening. In *Reddit v. SerpApi* (S.D.N.Y., July 31, 2026, motion-to-dismiss stage), the court held that Google's CAPTCHA and JavaScript challenges plausibly "effectively control access" under DMCA §1201(a), because they distinguish automated users from human ones ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/reddit-v-serpapi-llc)). That theory does not depend on who "accessed" the site, so it survives the Perplexity reasoning. In both the Amazon record and Cloudflare's delisting of Perplexity, what drew enforcement was **evasion**: posing as Chrome, rotating IPs and shipping workarounds within 24 hours of a block ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/perplexity-is-using-stealth-undeclared-crawlers-to-evade-website-no-crawl-directives/)). Login.gov's Rules of Use are blunter still. "Automated access to Login.gov is strictly prohibited," including "authentication … form submissions, and circumventing security," and "users must access it manually" ([Login.gov](https://www.login.gov/policy/rules-of-use)). It is unclear whether automated form-filling on an agency's own site after a human logs in breaches Login.gov's rules or only the agency's terms.

Government already recognizes one way for a third party to act: the **authorized representative**. Under Medicaid's 42 CFR 435.923, an applicant may designate an individual *or an organization*. Agencies must accept electronic and recorded signatures on the designation. The representative may sign the application and renewals, is responsible "to the same extent as the person represented," and its staff must affirm confidentiality obligations ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-C/part-435/subpart-J/section-435.923)). California's own Medi-Cal form MC 306 (rev. 06/07) is narrower. It says the representative "cannot act in place of the applicant," who must still sign the Statement of Facts ([DHCS](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/formsandpubs/forms/Documents/DHCS%20MC306.pdf)). For SNAP, an adult household member or an authorized representative may sign the application. States *may* accept electronic signatures, and an adult household member certifies the information "under penalty of perjury" ([7 CFR 273.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-C/part-273/subpart-A/section-273.2)). New York's form lets households limit what the representative may do ([NY OTDA](https://www.otda.ny.gov/policy/directives/2016/INF/16-INF-17.pdf)). The full text of the SNAP representative rule (273.2(n)) could not be retrieved, so its liability details are unverified. ESIGN gives legal effect to records formed by "electronic agents" when the agent's action "is legally attributable to the person to be bound" ([15 U.S.C. §7001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001)). That cuts both ways: an agent's wrong submission is the parent's legal act. **No court or agency has said whether an AI-mediated click on "I certify under penalty of perjury" is the applicant's signature.** California's CCPA rule for "authorized agents" (signed permission, direct confirmation from the consumer, security duties) is the most developed US template for consent records ([11 CCR §7063](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/11-CCR-7063)).

Technical identity standards are arriving, but not at government portals. Cloudflare's "signed agents" class (August 28, 2025) verifies agents that are "directed by an end user" through Web Bot Auth HTTP signatures. Its first cohort included ChatGPT agent, Browserbase and Anchor ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/zh-tw/signed-agents)). The IETF has chartered a webbotauth working group ([IETF](https://datatracker.ietf.org/group/webbotauth/about/)). Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol ([Visa](https://usa.visa.com/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases.releaseId.21716.html)) and Google's AP2 "intent" and "cart" mandates, signed records of what the user authorized ([CMSWire](https://cmswire.com/digital-experience/google-pushes-standards-for-agentic-ai-commerce-with-ap2)), show how authority will be proven in commerce. **No benefits, health-plan, school or financial-aid portal found accepts signed agents or delegated agent tokens.** Prompt injection makes all of this more pressing. OpenAI said in December 2025 that it is "unlikely to ever be fully 'solved'" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/23/openai-ai-browser-prompt-injections-cybersecurity-hackers/?rand=8593)). Brave showed text hidden in screenshots hijacking agents inside logged-in banking and health sessions ([Brave](https://brave.com/blog/unseeable-prompt-injections/)). A vendor documentation page fetched during this research contained instructions aimed at AI agents ([Anchor](https://llms.anchorbrowser.io/mfa-captcha-wall-stopped-automation)). The OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications calls for short-lived, task-scoped credentials and confirmations that show the raw actions, with logs of "what was displayed versus what executed" ([OWASP](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026)).

The design rules that follow:

| Rule | Basis |
|---|---|
| Never solve, outsource or evade a CAPTCHA, bot check or MFA; pause and hand it to the parent | Reddit v. SerpApi §1201 theory; Cloudflare norms; Login.gov |
| Turn off vendor CAPTCHA solving, stealth fingerprints and rotating residential proxies on government and health portals; identify honestly and sign with Web Bot Auth where supported | Evasion drew enforcement in Amazon and Cloudflare/Perplexity |
| Never automate Login.gov (or ID.me) authentication; the parent logs in manually in a handoff, and the agent works only inside that session | Login.gov Rules of Use |
| Prefer user-attributed sessions: the parent's own device, or a live session the parent opens and authenticates; never hold government passwords | Ninth Circuit's "user accesses" reasoning; OWASP ASI03 |
| The parent personally reads and checks every attestation and signature; the helper fills, the parent signs | Propel principle 2; 7 CFR 273.2 perjury certification; MC 306 |
| One explicit YES per consequential action, shown with exact values and the full legal text; log displayed versus executed | ESIGN attribution; OWASP ASI09; CCPA §7063 template |
| Use formal authorized-representative designations where the program allows organizations, with staff confidentiality affirmations | 42 CFR 435.923 |
| Restrict each authenticated session to an allowlist of portal domains, with no open browsing | Prompt injection (Brave; OWASP ASI01) |
| Mark submissions as AI-assisted and enforce completeness rules before sending | Intuit 2015 backlash; Propel principle 1 |
| Never claim results you have not measured | FTC's 2024 DoNotPay order ([ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/robot-lawyer-website-donotpay-settles-ftc-claims-it-couldnt-deliver-on-promises)) |

## What this means for Mycelium

**Where to sit.** Mycelium belongs at the application and context layer, above general models and commodity browsers. Its job is to own the knowledge that makes a portal finishable: which fields each portal needs, which documents, which traps (timeouts, attestation boxes, paperless opt-ins), and which family facts are verified. Model progress helps this position. Every gain in grounding and long-horizon reasoning makes the explored first run cheaper. Models do not supply the context, the consent or the institutional standing. The consumer agents being built by Meta and others are the risk. Sponsor distribution and the "assist, don't sign" trust posture are the defense.

**Sort portals by authority before you automate them.** This is the most useful synthesis in the report. Portals fall into three tiers. Tier 1 is portals the **sponsor owns**: district enrollment and annual updates, LCFF alternative income forms, consents, and a union fund's own reimbursement forms. There the sponsor can authorize the agent by contract, which removes the access question. Run the full loop there: compiled workflow, validator, review page, YES, submit. Tier 2 is programs that **accept organizational authorized representatives**: Medicaid under 42 CFR 435.923, and by my inference the Medicare Savings Program, which state Medicaid agencies run (verify New York's form). There Mycelium acts as the organization, with staff affirmations, and the parent still signs attestations. Tier 3 is **human-only identity**: Login.gov-fronted services, and probably the FSA ID that FAFSA uses (the rule that an FSA ID is personal is widely understood but was not verified here). There the helper prepares everything, opens a handoff, and the parent logs in, checks the attestation and presses submit. Tier 1 is where the reliability record and the case studies come from. Tier 2 is where the money for families is. Tier 3 is where trust gets built.

**What to build now.** The codebase already has the consent gate, two-phase fill and submit, form recipes, review links, the "completed isn't submitted" rule and CAPTCHA termination. Five additions would turn it into what the evidence ranks highest. First, **compile workflows from runs**: turn verified recipes into executable per-portal scripts (Playwright, or cached Stagehand actions) with DOM assertions, keeping the agent as fallback and writing the consent gate into the script. Second, **build a validator**: after every fill, read each field back from the DOM and match it exactly against the family record, apply per-portal completeness rules, and when a workflow has drifted, run best-of-N up to the review page. Third, **upgrade the review page** from a screenshot to a table of every exact value, the full attestation text and an explicit "you are signing this" step that the parent completes. Fourth, set up **authorized-representative footing**: an organizational designation workflow for Medi-Cal and New York Medicaid/MSP, with e-signature, staff confidentiality affirmations and revocation by text. Fifth, move to **user-attributed sessions**: the parent authenticates live through a handoff link, the agent never stores government passwords, cloud sessions are signed with Web Bot Auth, and an on-device option comes later for portals where the Ninth Circuit's architecture distinction matters. Audit the vendor stack so no CAPTCHA solver or stealth setting is ever on for a government or health domain.

**What moat this creates.** It has three compounding parts. The first is a **per-portal workflow library** that improves with each family. The hundredth CalFresh renewal is a deterministic replay with a known pass rate, which a general agent cannot match, and it becomes the training "gym" the labs lack. The second is a **family consent graph**: verified facts, documents, standing designations and a signed record of every YES. It lets one approval cascade across institutions (one income verification feeds the LCFF count, CalFresh and SUN Bucks), and it is the evidence trail regulators and sponsors will ask for. The third is **institutional trust**: sponsor contracts, representative standing, AI-assisted flags agencies can see, and published accuracy. That is the "agent that becomes a network" in speedrun's sense, because each new district and fund adds both demand and portals.

## Conclusion

The story of 2026 is that general computer use became cheap and good enough to explore any portal, while finishing a portal reliably became a different and harder problem. The tools that solve it (verification, compilation, consent and legal standing) are specific to each institution, and no model provider will build them for school districts, county benefits offices and union funds. That reverses the usual fear for an application startup. Every frontier release makes Mycelium's first run on a new portal cheaper and leaves its replay library, consent graph and sponsor relationships untouched.

The deeper point is about authority rather than capability. The agents in Propel's test could do most of the clicking, and they failed on whether they were allowed to: they guessed at facts, signed attestations and opted the applicant into paperless notices. The law is drawing the same line. It protects the user's own access and punishes evasion. Representative status is the only delegation government recognizes, and nobody has decided whether an AI click can be a perjury signature. The company that wins family-side paperwork will be the one institutions and families trust to act for them, proven one portal and one YES at a time. That favors a sponsor-distributed, assist-don't-sign helper over a faster, more autonomous one.
