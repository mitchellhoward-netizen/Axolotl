# Let the school introduce, then finish one task

The research points to one form factor: **a helper on a plain phone number, one number per school, that the school's own channel introduces, that answers the parent's first text within seconds with the child's name and one task the school already knows is open, and that finishes that task in the thread.** Three findings carry most of the weight. First, school-to-family messages move outcomes when they carry **child-specific, actionable facts from a sender the family already trusts, and when every family receives them by default**. In one randomized trial, take-up of academic alerts was about 1% when parents had to opt in and about 95% when they were enrolled automatically, and only the default arm improved grades and course failures ([HKS](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/simplification-and-defaults-affect-adoption-and-impact-technology-decision-makers-do-0)). Second, **doing the task with the family beats telling them about it**. Peer navigators who filled out and submitted applications got 95% of eligible Latino children insured, against 69% under standard outreach ([UConn Today](https://today.uconn.edu/2018/03/parent-mentors-improve-latino-childrens-health-insurance-coverage-rates/)), and simplifying and guiding a SNAP application raised applications from 32% to 59% ([University of Utah](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/mRelief-simplifies-SNAP-applications_University-of-Utah.pdf)). Third, about half of Hispanic adults have never used a chatbot, and most non-users say they are not interested ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/why-dont-people-use-chatbots.md)). So the helper should never present itself as AI to be adopted. It should arrive as the school, about something the parent already owes or is owed. Three conflicts in the evidence have workable answers. To get default-like reach without a vendor cold-texting families, **the district's existing channel sends the first child-specific message and the helper replies once the parent writes back**. AI is disclosed in plain words in the first reply, **after the school's name and the task, not as the headline**. Conversations are task-led, not open chat. No randomized trial has tested an AI helper that completes forms for K-12 families. Mycelium's first district pilot should be built to run that trial.

## Specific facts from a trusted, close sender move attendance and failure, not test scores

About twenty randomized trials from 2012 to 2024 agree on a narrow result. **Child-specific information the parent did not have, sent often, by a sender the parent trusts, cuts absences, missed assignments and course failures by roughly 10–40%.** Effects on grades and test scores are much smaller, about 0.03–0.2 standard deviations, and often zero. Generic one-way nudges from distant senders come out null even at very large scale. The table lists the studies that matter most for a school-sponsored helper, with design strength and dates.

| Study (date) | Design | What was sent | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bergman & Chan, West Virginia ([JHR 2021](https://jhr.uwpress.org/content/56/1/125); [J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/print/pdf/node/2353?lang=en)) | RCT, 22 schools, 1,137 parents, opt-in | Weekly automated texts on missed assignments and absences | **Course failures −27%, class attendance +12%**; no state test effect; messaging cost $63 in total |
| Bergman, Lasky-Fink & Rogers ([OBHDP 2020](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/simplification-and-defaults-affect-adoption-and-impact-technology-decision-makers-do-0)) | RCT, 6,976 parents | Same alerts, three enrollment rules | Take-up **~1% opt-in, ~11% simplified opt-in, ~95% default**; only default moved GPA (+0.06) and failures |
| Bergman, LAUSD school ([dissertation](https://escholarship.org/content/qt9d89d761/qt9d89d761.pdf)) | RCT, 462 students, 2010–11 | Missing assignments with page and problem numbers, ~twice a month | High school GPA **+0.19 SD**; parents chose text 79% of the time |
| Kraft & Rogers ([EER 2015](https://annenberg.brown.edu/publications/underutilized-potential-teacher-parent-communication-evidence-field-experiment)) | RCT, summer credit recovery | One teacher message a week | Failure to earn credit **15.8% → 9.3%**; "what to improve" beat praise |
| Rogers & Feller, Philadelphia ([Nature Human Behaviour 2018](https://toddrogers.scholars.harvard.edu/resource/reducingstudentabsencesatscalepdf)) | RCT, 28,080 households | Up to 5 mailings with the child's total absences | Absences −6% with the child's own total vs −3.5% for a generic reminder; **about $5 per extra day** |
| Lasky-Fink et al., California ([EdWorkingPaper 2021](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai20-271)) | RCT, 131,312 students | Truancy notice rewritten at 5th-grade level, no legal threats | **About 40% larger reduction** in absences than the standard notice |
| IES/NCEE, 4 urban districts ([AIR 2020](https://air.org/news/press-release/new-federal-study-finds-text-messages-parents-can-reduce-chronic-school-absences)) | RCT, 108 schools, 23,000+ students | Weekly texts and same-day absence alerts, then intensified outreach | Chronic absence **−2.4 to −3.6 points**; staff outreach beat automated goal-setting texts; no reading or math effect; $6.90–8.53 per student |
| Berlinski et al., Chile ([NBER w28581](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28581/revisions/w28581.rev1.pdf)) | RCT, 18 months | Weekly attendance texts, monthly grades | Math +0.083 SD, **no fade over 18 months**; only about 60% of texts delivered |
| Bird et al. ([NBER w26158, 2019](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26158.pdf)) | RCT, 800,000+ students | National FAFSA nudges | **Null** on aid and enrollment; authors: "the relationship between the student and the source of the nudge matters" |
| Avery et al. ([NBER w27897, 2020](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27897)) | Concurrent cluster RCTs | Monthly texts from outside counselors vs biweekly texts from students' own counselors | National arm mostly null; **own-counselor arm positive** |
| Kraft & Bolves, NYC ([EFP 2022](https://www.matthewakraft.com/can-technology-transform-communication-between-schools-teachers-and-parents)) | RCT, 132 schools | Free school–parent app with implementation support | Use stayed low, **fell when support ended**, few effects |
| TalkingPoints ([2025 study](https://talkingpts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/TalkingPoints-Attendance-Behavior-Improvement-Study-2025.pdf)) | Vendor, propensity-matched; authors call it correlational | Two-way translated messaging | High school attendance gains reported; **not causal** |

Four design facts come out of these trials, and each one matters for the product. **Personalization drives the effect.** Sending the child's actual absence count roughly doubled the effect of a generic reminder, and in a factorial trial of 5,172 students, child-specific data was the only factor with consistent effects ([Asher, Scherer & Kim](https://cepr.harvard.edu/resource/asherjmpfactorialdesignfortextmessagingpdf)). One trial disagrees: in São Paulo, generic "salience" messages worked about as well as individualized ones ([J-PAL Brazil](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/how-messages-parents-impact-student-performance-brazil)). The two results fit together if attention to school explains part of the effect and specific facts add the rest. For a helper whose job is a specific form, the specific fact is the task itself. **Dose has a middle.** Three texts a week beat both one and five, one a week cut bottom-quartile literacy by 0.15 SD relative to three, and more texts or more complex texts raised opt-outs ([Cortes et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24827); [Fricke et al.](https://tipsbytext.stanford.edu/research/its-too-annoying-who-drops-out-educational-text-messaging-programs-and-why)). **Timing matters**: weekend parenting tips beat weekday tips, especially for lower-skill children ([NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/oct19/how-timing-parenting-tips-boosts-their-effectiveness-children)), and Chilean attendance gains clustered on Monday through Wednesday after a Monday text. **The sender matters most.** Every null at scale came from a distant sender, and every strong result came from the child's own teacher, counselor or school.

Families say the same thing when asked. In 2025 focus groups in Iowa, including a Spanish-speaking group, parents found generic or critical messages condescending. They asked for a named contact, days missed instead of percentages, no texts when attendance is fine, no repeated alerts for absences already explained, and accurate records, because one error costs trust ([AIR 2025](https://www.air.org/sites/default/files/2025-07/Text-messaging-study-April-2025.pdf)). Spanish-speaking families in a study of more than 3,500 families were **significantly more likely to engage, especially through two-way texting**, and said it lowered barriers of language and tech confidence ([CEPR 2024](https://cepr.harvard.edu/resource/understanding-heterogeneous-patterns-family-engagement-educational-technology-inform-0)). Effects were consistently largest for the students furthest behind: below-median GPA, previously high-absence, at-risk. Well-built messaging narrows gaps, but only for families it reaches. Two caveats keep this honest. Parent misperception is the root problem these trials fix (Philadelphia parents estimated 9.6 absences against 17.8 actual), and Mycelium's tasks are different: the parent usually knows the form exists and has not finished it. The trials show who to send as and how often. They do not show that a conversation completes a form.

## Doing the task beats telling, and conversation alone is not completion

Wherever help and information were tested head to head, help won. The Kids' HELP trial randomized 155 uninsured, eligible Latino children in Texas. Paid, bilingual parent mentors who had navigated Medicaid themselves completed and submitted applications, explained renewal, and acted as liaison. **95% of mentored children were insured at one year against 69%, in about 2 months instead of 5, and 100% were still insured two years after mentoring ended** ([UConn Today](https://today.uconn.edu/2018/03/parent-mentors-improve-latino-childrens-health-insurance-coverage-rates/); [Health Affairs](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/pdf/10.1377/hlthaff.2017.1272)). In the H&R Block FAFSA trial, pre-filling and helping families submit raised filing by 15.7 points for dependents, while information alone did nothing ([J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/role-application-assistance-and-information-college-decisions-results-hr-block-fafsa)). In Pennsylvania, SNAP enrollment among eligible seniors was 5.8% with no contact, 10.5% with letters, and 17.6% with letters plus phone help applying ([MIT Economics](https://economics.mit.edu/research/publications/take-and-targeting-experimental-evidence-snap)). The safety-net texting record says the same thing at the level of individual steps. Louisiana's reminder texts to more than 108,000 clients raised WIC renewals from 29% to 52% and SNAP redeterminations from 51% to 70%. GetCalFresh interview reminders made applicants 55% more likely to complete interviews. mRelief's guided, shortened SNAP application raised applications from 32% to 59% in a Kentucky trial with imperfect randomization ([Digital Government Hub](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Basics-of-Texting-Safety-Net-Clients-2022.pdf); [University of Utah](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/mRelief-simplifies-SNAP-applications_University-of-Utah.pdf)).

Relationship without a fitted task does not move outcomes. The MDRC trial of Communities In Schools case management in 24 schools found **no effect on attendance, course failure or credits after two years**, even though students reported more caring adults, and case-managed students had more suspensions ([MDRC](https://mdrc.org/sites/default/files/CIS%20RCT_Exec%20Sum_Web-REV.pdf)). The strongest home-visit study, a matched comparison in DC, found real but small effects: attendance +0.35 points and disciplinary incidents 9.3% against 12.2% ([Mathematica](https://www.mathematica.org/publications/impacts-of-home-visits-on-students-in-district-of-columbia-public-schools)). Two more results show that talking is not the same as finishing. In a New Orleans early-childhood verification trial, **personalized two-way texts from a named staff member raised verification about 7 points, the same as formal one-way reminders, even though 89% of parents replied** ([ERA-New Orleans](https://educationresearchalliancenola.org/files/publications/20200623-Technical-Report-Gerry-et-al-Increasing-Access-to-Head-Start-Through-Text-Messages.pdf)). Georgia State's Pounce chatbot, the closest causal analog to an AI helper, guided admitted students through each pre-enrollment task by text and **raised on-time enrollment 3.3 points**, with fewer than 1% of student messages needing staff ([AERA Open](https://doaj.org/article/423e9611c02d4af680212430bcdcf47c); [Campus Technology](https://campustechnology.com/articles/2017/03/07/using-ai-chatbots-to-freeze-summer-melt-in-higher-ed.aspx)). Its effect was concentrated among students who had already committed, with no significant effect across the full sample ([AERA Open](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2332858417749220)). Help works best for people who have already decided. Most school-edge tasks fit that case: the parent already wants the meals, the benefit, the child in class.

This settles the open-chat question. The large measured effects come from **structured, task-led exchanges**: a reminder tied to a deadline, a shortened form, one step at a time, a human behind the hard cases. None comes from an open assistant waiting for questions. Open input still matters, because parents will text whatever is on their mind, and Propel found people stalled at an empty text box but spoke freely into a microphone ([Propel, May 2026](https://www.propel.app/insights/voice-by-default/)). The resolution: **accept anything, lead with a task**. The helper takes free text and voice, and every reply ends with one concrete next step. LAUSD's "Ed" is the cautionary case on the other side. Critics said it "mostly repeated information available on the district website" ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/la-unified-faces-criticism-after-collapse-of-splashy-ai-tool-ed/)). A question-answering helper competes with the district website and ParentSquare's new AI assistant. A task-finishing helper competes with nothing the district owns.

The human layer stays. In the federal attendance trial, staff outreach beat automated texts for the highest-absence students. Every working AI deployment for low-income users keeps human escalation: Jacaranda's AI in Kenya triages and drafts for human agents, Propel's chat escalated unresolved cases to staff, and GOV.UK users asked most for an advisor who could see their case ([CEGA](https://cega.berkeley.edu/feature/improving-antenatal-care-for-expecting-mothers-in-kenya/); [Propel, Jul 2025](https://www.propel.app/insights/using-ai-to-help-snap-recipients-diagnose-and-restore-lost-benefits/); [Inside GOV.UK, Mar 2026](https://insidegovuk.blog.gov.uk/2026/03/16/5-things-we-learned-testing-gov-uk-chat-an-ai-assistant-for-government)). Kids' HELP mentors had "been through it" themselves. An agent cannot supply that. The design consistent with the evidence is that the helper does the paperwork and hands relationship-heavy moments (fear, disputes, IEP evaluations, immigration-related worry) to a named liaison or promotora.

## People who have never used AI need the school's name and a task, not a chatbot

Most target parents have a phone and many have never used a chatbot. **98% of Hispanic adults have a cellphone, 93% a smartphone, and 28% rely on the smartphone with no home broadband** (34% of adults under $30K) ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/mobile/markdown)). In Pew's February 2026 survey, 49% of Hispanic adults use AI chatbots, about the national rate, but 50% never do, and 77% of adults 65 and older never do. Non-users cite lack of interest (83%), worry about personal information (79%), distrust of accuracy (76%) and not knowing how (55%) ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/why-dont-people-use-chatbots.md); [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/racial-and-ethnic-differences-in-how-adults-use-and-view-ai.md)). A product that has to be adopted as AI starts behind with half its users. A text from the child's school about the child's form does not ask anyone to adopt anything.

Interaction design for low-literacy users is well studied. In Microsoft Research's experiment with 58 nonliterate or semiliterate adults doing a banking task with no training, **text menus had 0% completion, spoken dialog 72%, and a graphical interface 100% but slowly**. 56 of 90 people in the ethnography could not navigate hierarchical menus at first, and many feared pressing keys would break the phone ([Medhi et al., TOCHI](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf)). Propel's 2026 field research with 40 San Diego SNAP recipients found the same in a modern phone: "The voice channel was where the actual conversation happened" ([Propel](https://www.propel.app/insights/voice-by-default/)). For language mixing, 91 bilingual users in a Microsoft Research study preferred bots that code-mix. A "nudge" policy, light mixing that mirrors the user, was acceptable to everyone, while always mixing was riskier ([Bawa et al., CSCW 2020](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/do-multilingual-users-prefer-chat-bots-that-code-mix-lets-nudge-and-find-out/)). Propel's production chat switched to Haitian Creole mid-conversation without being designed to. No research was found on Indigenous-language speakers such as Mixteco or Zapoteco families using chatbots, which matters in Pajaro Valley and the Central Valley.

Speed is part of trust. GOV.UK Chat averaged 10.7 seconds per answer, which users accepted, but satisfaction rose when faster speeds were simulated ([Inside GOV.UK](https://insidegovuk.blog.gov.uk/2026/03/16/5-things-we-learned-testing-gov-uk-chat-an-ai-assistant-for-government)). Copy rules from safety-net texting are specific: name the agency in every message, use the first name (response rates up "nearly 25%"), stay at or under 320 characters at about a sixth-grade level, say how long the task takes, frame benefits as already the client's, use one tactic per message. Overly casual language did worse, and emoji had no measurable effect ([Digital Government Hub](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Basics-of-Texting-Safety-Net-Clients-2022.pdf)).

### The 30-second test is a data problem before it is an interface problem

"Value within 30 seconds for someone who has never heard of AI" means the first reply must contain something the parent recognizes as true and useful: the child's name, the school, and one task with a reason to do it now. Only the district roster makes that possible, because the helper already knows the children, their student numbers and birth dates, and which forms are open. mRelief's gain came from removing questions, and asking for what the institution already holds both causes abandonment and looks like phishing. The test then breaks into three timings: the first bubble lands within a few seconds (typing pacing must never delay the anchor), the first useful fact arrives in that bubble or the next, and the parent can finish the first step with a one-word reply or a voice note. **A first reply that asks "How can I help?" fails the test. A first reply that asks for the children's names fails it worse.**

### Disclosing AI: in the first reply, after the school and the task

The strongest evidence against upfront disclosure is a field experiment with more than 6,200 customers. Disclosing a chatbot before a sales call **cut purchases by more than 79.7%**, while undisclosed bots performed about as well as proficient human workers. The drop was smaller for customers with AI experience and when disclosure came later in the conversation ([Luo et al., Marketing Science 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v38y2019i6p937-947.html)). That was a sales context with voice bots that sounded human, not a public service. On the other side, public-sector practice discloses: GOV.UK Chat's onboarding states scope, reminds users to check answers and explains data use, and Code for America labels automated messages and always offers a human ([Inside GOV.UK](https://insidegovuk.blog.gov.uk/2026/03/16/5-things-we-learned-testing-gov-uk-chat-an-ai-assistant-for-government); [Code for America](https://codeforamerica.org/?p=11863)). California's bot law (Bus. & Prof. Code §17940) bars bots that mislead people about being artificial in order to incentivize a sale or influence a vote, and requires disclosure that is "clear, conspicuous, and reasonably designed to inform" ([Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/i-am-robot-californias-new-law-requires-disclosure-use-bots)). A school helper probably falls outside "sale or vote", but counsel should confirm, and the reputational case for disclosure does not depend on the statute. A Medi-Cal member told reporters it "took a minute" to realize Kern's voice agent was not human ([CHCF](https://www.chcf.org/blog/2026/07/16/how-ai-powered-outreach-is-helping-californians-keep-their-medi-cal-coverage/)). A school cannot afford that discovery going badly.

The reconciliation uses what Luo found mitigates the drop: disclosure that comes after the person has context, phrased so the person does not have to know what "AI" is. **Disclose in the first reply, as its last bubble, after the school's identity and the task, in plain words ("automated helper", "asistente automático") rather than "AI", paired with the name of a person who can take over.** Repeat it in four places: on any handoff, inside every consent summary before a submission, on the HELP keyword reply, and immediately and truthfully whenever a parent asks "¿es una persona?" On voice calls, the first sentence discloses, as the repo's voice adapter already does. This placement is an inference from Luo plus public-sector norms. It is untested with this population, and placement should be the first A/B test, measured on completion and opt-out, not only on replies.

A first reply to a parent who has just texted the number from the school's message would read:

> **Bubble 1:** Hola, Ana. Soy el ayudante de familias de la Escuela Hall. Veo a Lucía en 3.er grado.
>
> **Bubble 2:** A Lucía le falta el formulario de ingresos de este año. Puede darle a su familia $120 en SUN Bucks para comida este verano. La escuela ya tiene casi todo; solo necesito 2 respuestas. ¿Lo hacemos ahora? Toma 2 minutos.
>
> **Bubble 3:** Soy un asistente automático de la escuela. Nunca pido estatus migratorio ni Seguro Social. Para hablar con la Sra. García en la oficina, escriba PERSONA.

> **English:** Hi Ana, this is the family helper for Hall School. I see Lucía in 3rd grade. / Lucía's income form for this year is missing. It can get your family $120 in SUN Bucks for food this summer. The school already has almost everything; I just need 2 answers. Want to do it now? It takes 2 minutes. / I'm an automated helper for the school. I never ask about immigration status or Social Security. To talk with Ms. García in the office, text PERSON.

The benefit line must match the child's actual eligibility, from the pathway engine, or be left out.

## Reaching every family without cold texts: the district's channel goes first

The sharpest conflict in the evidence is between reach and consent. Default enrollment is the largest single lever (about 95% take-up against about 1% for opt-in), and district leaders in that study overestimated opt-in take-up by about 40 points ([HKS](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/simplification-and-defaults-affect-adoption-and-impact-technology-decision-makers-do-0)). Against that, texting is now the top contact method in US fraud reports ([FTC testimony](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ftc-testimony-jec-hearing-on-the-rising-scam-economy.pdf)), and reported text-scam losses reached $470M in 2024, more than five times 2020. The top scams impersonate trusted institutions, and one asks people to reply YES or NO ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2025/04/top-text-scams-2024)). The FTC tells people not to reply to unexpected texts. On consent, Code for America's guide reads the FCC's 2021 TCPA ruling as letting state and federal agencies text benefits recipients about enrollment without express consent, while contractors, community organizations and local governments lack that permission, and it recommends asking for consent anyway ([Digital Government Hub](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Basics-of-Texting-Safety-Net-Clients-2022.pdf)). A school-sponsored vendor is most likely a contractor. Carriers are also tightening A2P enforcement, and content or sender names that do not match the registered brand draw filtering ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/sms-deliverability-carrier-filtering-10dlc-three-habits)).

The conflict is narrower than it looks, for one reason: **in Bergman's trial, "default" meant the information arrived without the parent doing anything. It did not mean a vendor texted first.** What Mycelium needs by default is that every family with an open task sees a child-specific message about it from a sender they trust. Who carries that first message is a separate choice. The district already reaches families through ParentSquare, TalkingPoints or SchoolMessenger under consent arrangements it already has. ParentSquare reports reaching 99.4% of families in the home language in a typical district, a vendor figure that measures delivery, not reading ([ParentSquare](https://www.parentsquare.com/?p=19269)). So the design has two tiers.

**Tier 1, available now.** At roster load, every family is enrolled; nobody signs up. When the pathway engine finds an open task for a child, the district's own channel sends a short, child-specific message in the home language, from the school, naming the helper's number and the task. The helper number is also printed on the enrollment packet, on flyers, and announced at back-to-school night, and the front office confirms it on the phone. The parent texts or calls the number, and the helper replies. Because the parent writes first, the helper's messages are responses, the scam-fear instinct is answered by the school's own introduction, and the parent controls the start. The cost is that a reply is required, which makes this a simplified opt-in. The 11% simplified opt-in figure is a floor, not a forecast: here the offer repeats with each real task, carries the child's name, and is attached to money or a deadline the family cares about. Nobody has measured it.

**Tier 2, after counsel signs off.** The helper number becomes the school's number, registered under the district's brand. The helper sends one task-specific informational message to the parent number the family gave the school, as the school, with STOP and HELP, and then nothing more unless the parent replies. Whether the consent a parent gave the school when providing a number at enrollment covers its contractor's informational texts is a legal question, and so is whether the helper can act as the school official for that message. If counsel clears it, the pilot should randomize schools between Tier 1 alone and Tier 1 plus the Tier 2 follow-up and measure completion. That comparison answers the reach question directly.

Both tiers keep the scam defenses that safety-net texting found effective: name the school in the first words, use the first name, refer to something the parent already knows is pending, send from one consistent number, give a phone number the parent already knows to verify, put no links in the first message, and send a confirmation after any submission, because confirmations "help build trust and show messages are genuine" ([Digital Government Hub](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Basics-of-Texting-Safety-Net-Clients-2022.pdf)).

Enforcement fear is a first-order constraint, not an edge case. After the January 7, 2025 Border Patrol raids in the Central Valley, **daily absences in five districts rose 22%**, sharpest in pre-K through grade 5, an event study published in PNAS ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/as-ice-actions-ramp-up-study-cites-81k-lost-school-days-after-california-raids/)). About one in four adults in mixed-status families avoided safety-net programs in 2023 for immigration reasons, and 15% of California foreign-born parents were illegally asked for a Social Security number or proof of citizenship when enrolling a child ([Urban Institute](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Mixed-Status-Families-and-Immigrant-Families-with-Children-Continued-Avoiding-Safety-Net-Programs-in-2023.pdf); [The Children's Partnership](https://childrenspartnership.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/imm_fam_snapshot_aciac_2026_final_r5b_2026_08_14.pdf)). The school door creates fear as well as trust. The helper should say, in the family's language, what it never asks and never shares, and back that with policy: minimum data in model prompts, no immigration fields, scheduled deletion. A whistleblower alleged that LAUSD's Ed sent student data in every prompt to servers in seven countries ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/whistleblower-l-a-schools-chatbot-misused-student-data-as-tech-co-crumbled/)), and that claim will be in every buyer's head.

## Fifteen design rules, each tied to evidence

| # | Rule | Evidence behind it |
|---|---|---|

Name the school in the first words of every message that opens a thread. ## Form factor and architecture: SMS on a school number is the floor

The working deployments for low-income, Spanish-speaking adults share a shape. The institution starts the contact, so the person adopts nothing. There is one time-bound job. The person's language is the default and switches automatically. Humans take handoffs, and staff are alerted when someone is unhappy. Kern Family Health Care's voice agent "Angelica" made 800,000 to 1.2 million calls to Medi-Cal members, more than half in Spanish, and the plan reports renewal rates around 95–96%, against 38% before the platform. Those figures are plan- and vendor-reported, the two published sources disagree on volume, and CHCF, which published one account, is an investor in the vendor ([CHCF](https://www.chcf.org/blog/2026/07/16/how-ai-powered-outreach-is-helping-californians-keep-their-medi-cal-coverage/); [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/medicaid/medicaid-work-requirements-medi-cal-ai-agents-reenroll-careforce-california/)). Even discounted, it is the clearest case of an institution-sponsored agent reaching Mycelium's exact families at scale. Consumer agents show the opposite path. Meta's Muse reached about 600,000 US daily users within a month on WhatsApp and its apps, and the same weeks brought reports that it gave a user's home address to a Marketplace buyer and accepted a low offer without asking ([Ecosistema Startup](https://ecosistemastartup.com/muse-el-agente-ia-de-meta-600k-usuarios-y-muchos-sustos/)). Those reports are secondhand. The lesson is to copy the distribution (the messaging app people already use) and avoid the autonomy (act only on an explicit yes).

The per-school number answers the friction Mitch named. One number, printed everywhere the school prints its phone number, is something a front-office clerk can repeat, a flyer can carry, and a parent can save as "Escuela Hall". It also matches carrier rules, because the registered brand and the sender name are the same. Hosted SMS on the school's real line is the logical end state, since the parent already trusts that number. But it also moves the thread onto the district's own line, which raises records and enforcement questions that the separate helper number keeps distinct. It should follow, not lead, and only after counsel answers those questions.

### How a parent first meets the helper

The parent should meet the helper the way they meet anything from school, several times and through people they know. The district's ParentSquare, TalkingPoints or SchoolMessenger message carries the child-specific task and the number. The enrollment packet and re-registration form print the number. The front office says "text this number, it can do the meal form with you." Teachers and family liaisons mention it at conferences and at the English Learner Advisory Committee. A flyer goes home in the backpack. The school's main line can play a short message offering the helper. Each touch repeats the same number and the same plain description: "the school's helper for forms, in your language, by text or call."

### Beside the district's platform, not against it

ParentSquare consolidated the category (it bought Remind's K-12 business, and Remind Hub does not renew past 2026-27), reports serving 22 million students and families, and launched an AI Assistant in August 2026 that answers families' questions from district-published content. It is off by default and controlled by the district ([eSchool News](https://www.eschoolnews.com/newsline/2023/12/08/parentsquare-acquires-remind-expanding-options-for-school-home-engagement/); [Bloomz](https://www.bloomz.com/blog/remind-is-now-parentsquare-what-districts-should-do); [Techstrong.ai](https://techstrong.ai/articles/the-new-ai-bringing-school-home/)). Philadelphia's "Let's Talk" assistant handled about 35,000 messages in a year on a contact page ([PhillyVoice](https://www.phillyvoice.com/school-district-philadelphia-lets-talk-customer-service-ai-platform-k12-insight/)). The family-facing AI districts accept in 2026 is narrow and answers questions. Mycelium should state the division of labor in one line: **the district's platform broadcasts and answers questions; the helper finishes the specific tasks each family owes or is owed, and reports completions back.** In practice, the helper uses the platform as its introduction channel. It never sends broadcasts. It sends handoffs to the same staff who already answer ParentSquare replies. Where integrations allow, it posts completion status back to the SIS so the district's own systems show the form done. This also answers the Liberty, Arizona board, which tabled a ParentSquare contract partly because it was unclear whether it replaced existing tools ([Soar/CP](https://cp.soar.com/articles/6767390/Arizona/Board-tables-ParentSquare-contract-review-after-product-presentation-and-cost-questions)). The helper replaces nothing.

### Architecture in one paragraph

A channel gateway per school (SMS/RCS/voice on one number, WhatsApp as a second number) normalizes every inbound message, including transcribed voice notes, into one family thread keyed to the roster household, not to a phone number, so a parent who switches from SMS to WhatsApp or changes phones keeps one conversation. The roster sync supplies children, IDs, birth dates, home language and open tasks. The pathway engine decides the single next task and the minimum questions. The conversation layer (multi-bubble, typing pacing, progressive disclosure, memory, already in the repo) writes replies under the rules above, with a hard cap on proactive sends. The consent layer shows exact values, binds the parent's natural yes to them, records it, never ticks attestations, and requires an institutional confirmation before saying "done." A handoff queue puts the thread in front of a named staff member. A data-minimizing proxy strips fields the model does not need. A reporting layer gives the district completions, delivery, escalations and errors by language, every month.

## What schools will ask, and how adoption fails

Districts rarely cancel a tool. It dies through non-use. Districts accessed an average of 2,982 distinct tools in 2024-25 ([ClassLink summary of Instructure data](https://www.classlink.com/news/despite-push-to-pare-back-ed-tech-report-finds-districts-inventories-are-still-growing)). The widely repeated "two-thirds of licenses go unused" figure comes from a 2019 Glimpse K12 analysis, not a recent study ([EdWeek Market Brief](https://marketbrief.edweek.org/marketplace-k-12/unused-educational-software-major-source-wasted-k-12-spending-new-analysis-finds/)). In the NYC app trial, use was low even with support and fell when support ended. After buying ParentSquare, Oakland paid a contractor up to $30,000 just to sustain the rollout ([OUSD](https://ousd.granicusideas.com/meetings/2849-board-of-education-on-2025-09-10-4-00-pm/agenda_items/68bdbb2044253823ea040e8c-o-dot-77-25-2004-ratification-by-the-board-of-educati)). A family helper avoids this failure if it needs no activation from staff or families: every family is enrolled from the roster, no teacher builds lists, and the district sees completions monthly without logging in. Pounce needed staff for fewer than 1% of messages, which is the right order of magnitude to promise and then measure.

## What this changes in Mycelium's current plan

**The channel moves from iMessage-first to a school number on SMS.** One number per school on SMS, registered under the district's brand, becomes the floor. RCS, voice and WhatsApp layer onto it, and Photon iMessage becomes an optional upgrade rather than the door. The repo's SMS path through Textbelt, described in `src/integrations/sms.ts` as needing "No A2P/brand registration", should not carry production school traffic, because carriers now filter senders whose content and brand registration don't match.

**Onboarding for school-sponsored families becomes zero questions.** `src/agent/onboarding.ts` currently opens with a branded "Axolotl" paragraph that names "the AI" in its first lines, asks the parent to pick English or Spanish, then asks for an email, the children's names, the school, the city, needs, challenges and a mailing address, and lists capabilities with emoji bullets. For a family that arrives from the roster, every one of those questions except the parent's own needs is something the district knows, and the research says asking for known facts causes abandonment and looks like phishing. The school-sponsored first reply should be the three-bubble pattern above: child, task, disclosure. The consumer onboarding can stay for families who find the helper on their own. The mailing-address step (RELEASE-PLAN item 8, tied to a breach-notification rule) should draw on the roster address in school-sponsored mode if counsel agrees that satisfies the rule.

**The sender voice becomes the school's.** Threads open as "the family helper for Hall School," and the Mycelium brand moves to the HELP reply and the privacy page.

**Proactive sends get a governor.** A per-family cap of two proactive messages a week, merging and suppression when nothing is due, and a timing policy (forms on weekends and evenings, attendance on Mondays).

**Voice becomes a first-class input.** Voice notes are transcribed into the same thread, voice replies go to voice users, and "llámame" opens a call on the school number. Languages the model handles poorly route to a human interpreter.

**Handoff becomes a product surface.** PERSONA reaches a named staff member with the thread visible, automatic escalation triggers fire on the categories above, and response times are reported.

**The pilot becomes the trial nobody has run.** Stagger schools so those not yet offered serve as the comparison group. Within offered schools, randomize disclosure placement, and Tier 2 follow-up if counsel clears it. Measure completion, not replies.

## Open questions for counsel and the pilot

For counsel, the questions are legal and mostly about who is speaking. Does a parent's providing a phone number to the school at enrollment cover informational texts sent by the district's contractor as the school, and does a reply to the district's own channel message count as consent for the helper to continue? Is the helper a "school official" for the roster and the introduction, and does each parent yes put later actions under the parent's own consent, in a way that fits AB 1584's rule that records stay the LEA's property? Does §17940 apply to a school helper, and does the recommended disclosure wording satisfy it either way? If the helper runs on the school's real main line through hosted SMS, are the threads district records subject to public records requests, and how should the helper respond to subpoenas or administrative requests for family threads given enforcement fear? What retention period and deletion schedule is defensible? Does a roster address satisfy the breach-notification address requirement? Who owns the 10DLC brand and the number if the contract ends?

For the pilot, the questions are empirical. What share of families reply when the district's channel introduces the helper with a child-specific task, compared with the 11% simplified opt-in benchmark, and how much does a helper-sent follow-up add? Does placing the disclosure last in the first reply change completion or opt-out compared with placing it first? How many seconds pass between a parent's first text and the first useful fact, and does completion fall off past 30 seconds? What share of families use voice notes, WhatsApp or calls, by language? Do completion gains reach families who speak languages other than English and Spanish and unduplicated students at the same rate as others, or does the helper repeat SNAP outreach's pattern of reaching the less-needy first? How many handoffs per hundred families reach staff, and how fast are they answered? What share of messages are delivered, and how often do numbers change mid-year? Do completed forms produce counted students, issued benefits and recovered days in the district's systems of record?

## Conclusion

The 30-second test turns out to be settled less by interface choices than by two assets Mycelium has to borrow: the district's roster, which lets the first reply contain the child's name and a real open task, and the district's channel, which lets the first contact come from a sender the parent already trusts. Interface choices (SMS floor, voice notes, numbered replies, plain copy) then decide whether the parent can act on that value. Read this way, the disclosure conflict and the consent conflict share an answer. The school introduces the helper, the helper earns trust by doing one concrete thing correctly, and it says plainly what it is once the parent knows why it is there. The evidence says reach comes from defaults and outcomes come from doing the work, so the product's moat is the combination of default enrollment through the district and task completion on the parent's yes. A helper that answers questions has neither.

The biggest unknown is whether a helper whose value requires a reply can capture most of what default enrollment achieved for one-way alerts. The literature cannot answer that, because every default-enrollment result is for information that needed no reply, and every help-beats-information result is for help the person actively sought. Mycelium's first pilot sits exactly in that gap. Designed with staggered schools and completion as the outcome, it would be the first causal evidence on AI-assisted family paperwork in K-12, which makes it useful to districts and investors even if it delivers nothing else.
