# Research brief: verify the "wrong payer" problem at union benefit funds

## Context (read first)

We're evaluating a startup, Axolotl. It sells to union (Taft-Hartley) health
and welfare funds and to public-employer retiree health plans.

**The claim.** These plans pay costs that Medicare, Social Security or the
state should pay, and the members lose money in the same cases. Axolotl:

1. finds those members in the plan's own data
2. contacts them by text
3. files the public-program application with their consent
4. charges the plan per approval

**Your job.** Independently confirm, correct or refute each claim below.
Don't assume any claim is true. Look as hard for evidence that a problem is
*already handled* (by plan design, regulation or existing vendors) as for
evidence that it exists.

## Rules

- **Prefer primary sources.** CMS, SSA, GAO, HHS-OIG, MACPAC and state audit
  offices (NY OSC, NYC Comptroller, county comptrollers). Plan documents
  (SPDs, SMMs) and fund annual reports or Form 5500 filings. Court opinions.
  USRDS. Peer-reviewed studies. KFF and IFEBP.
- **Treat vendor marketing as a claim to test, not evidence.** That covers
  SSDC, Allsup, Advocator, PCG, DUOS and similar.
- **Quote the exact sentence or table cell** for every number, with the URL
  and the publication date or data year.
- **Label every figure** as **SOURCED** (directly stated by a source) or
  **DERIVED** (your arithmetic from sourced numbers; show the math).
- **Report disconfirming evidence prominently.**
- **Use current figures (2025–2026) where they exist,** and say when you
  had to fall back to older data.
- **Don't contact anyone.** Desk research only.

## Claims to test

For each claim, return:

- a **verdict**: CONFIRMED / PARTLY CONFIRMED / REFUTED / CAN'T DETERMINE
- the **evidence**: quotes and URLs
- any **disconfirming evidence**
- a **confidence** level: high / medium / low
- a **corrected figure** if ours is wrong

### A. Part B premium reimbursement and Medicare Savings Programs (our strongest claim)

**A1.** 1199SEIU's National Benefit Fund (NBF) reimburses 50% of the
standard Part B premium to retirees 65+ with 10+ years of service.

**A2.** Retirees enrolled in a Medicare Savings Program (MSP) lose fund Part
B reimbursement, because the state then pays the premium. Check this for:

- the 1199 NBF
- NYSHIP: "not paid by another entity"
- NYC's Office of Labor Relations program

**A3.** New York's 2026 MSP income limit (QI-1) for one person is $2,474 a
month, with no asset test. Also find:

- the couple limit
- which income counts and what disregards apply
- whether New York requires annual renewal for MSP

**A4.** 1199's own 2020 report shows these pensions:

| Fund | Retirees | Paid in 2020 | Average (DERIVED) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Health Care Employees Pension Fund | 81,700 | $951.4M | about $970 a month |
| Greater New York Pension Fund | 12,843 | $79.7M | about $517 a month |
| Home Care Pension Fund | 24,777 | $23.0M | about $77 a month |

The NBF had 56,849 retirees. Confirm these and find newer figures (2022–2025
annual reports or Form 5500s). Also find:

- the *distribution* of pensions, not just the average, if published
- which pension fund NBF retirees mostly draw from

**A5.** What is a realistic Social Security benefit for retired health care
workers and home care aides? Our input was about $1,235 a month for lower
earners retiring at 65 (2024). Use SSA data by earnings level if possible.

**A6.** National MSP take-up: about 56–64% of eligible people enrolled in
2021–23 (MACPAC). Also find:

- New York's take-up rate
- whether take-up differs for people who have employer retiree coverage

**A7.** Our estimate for the NBF is about **$9–14M a year** in Part B
reimbursement the state should be paying. That assumes a third to half of
NBF retirees qualify, and about 40% of those aren't enrolled. Test each
assumption. Then:

- find 1199's actual annual Part B reimbursement spend (annual reports,
  Form 5500 Schedule H, notes to financial statements)
- produce your own low/base/high estimate

**A8.** Which other plans reimburse Part B, and how much do they spend? Our
starting list:

- New York State: $247M in calendar 2021
- New York City: $190M for 130,215 people in 2009
- Los Angeles, Boston (50%), Maine, Hawaii EUTF
- New York school districts and school-district health consortia

Also find:

- current NYC figures
- any other union funds that reimburse Part B
- how common Part B reimbursement is among Taft-Hartley funds overall

### B. Double payments and payments after death

**B1.** Confirm the Nassau County audit: 94 retirees and dependents, about
$1.5–1.6M in duplicate Part B reimbursements, the outstanding balance, and
the cause.

**B2.** Find other audits that found duplicate Part B reimbursements, or
premiums and reimbursements paid after death (NY OSC, NYC Comptroller,
other states). Collect counts and dollar amounts.

### C. Disability → SSDI → Medicare as second payer

**C1.** SSDI awards to disabled workers: 594,749 in 2024. Find the award
rate per 1,000 insured workers, and first-application approval rates for
2024–2025.

**C2.** Medicare paying first for disabled members:

- Medicare is primary for disabled people *not actively working*.
- A multiemployer plan stays primary for *active* disabled members if any
  contributing employer has 100+ employees.
- ALS gets Medicare in the same month as SSDI.

Confirm each with CMS or SSA citations.

**C3. Key design question.** How long do union health funds keep covering
totally disabled members?

- One example we found: "a maximum of 30 months … or until you are eligible
  for Medicare, whichever comes first" (South Central IBEW-NECA).
- Find 10+ more Taft-Hartley plan documents, from large funds in several
  trades and health care, including 1199. For each, record:
  - the disability extension length
  - whether *disability pensioners* get retiree health coverage, and for how
    long
  - whether the plan requires applying for SSDI or Medicare
- Summarize how common each design is.

**C4.** How much does a plan actually save when Medicare becomes primary for
a disabled member? Find independent figures: actuarial studies, state RFPs
and contract results (New Jersey's SHBP/SEHBP with SSDC; Connecticut's
state plan with PCG), and per-member cost data for disabled people under 65.

The figure "$76,540 lifetime savings per transition" appears to come from a
vendor and concern workers aged 65+. Confirm what it actually measures.

**C5.** What do SSDC, Allsup, Advocator and PCG charge plan sponsors (per
award, per enrollment or flat)? Do any of them serve Taft-Hartley funds?
Name the funds if so.

### D. Dialysis (ESRD) — we downgraded this; check the downgrade

**D1.** The 30-month coordination period:

- the group plan must pay first, and can't treat ESRD members differently
- after 30 months, most plans stop paying first whether or not the member
  enrolled in Medicare
- NYSHIP terminates coverage for members who don't enroll

Confirm with CMS rules and several plan documents.

**D2.** About 6% of ESRD patients are Medicare-secondary under a group plan
(USRDS, 2019). Find the newest figure, and any evidence that plans keep
paying first after month 30.

**D3.** Commercial vs Medicare dialysis spending: $238,126 against $80,509
in the first year (JAMA Network Open). Confirm, and check what *Marietta
Memorial Hospital v. DaVita* (2022) lets plans do.

### E. Retirees 65+ without Part B, and COBRA members with Medicare

**E1.** Do most union and public retiree plans "carve out" Medicare
(paying as if Medicare paid first) or require Part B, so that the cost of
missing Part B falls on the retiree, not the plan? Use several plan
documents.

**E2.** About 763,000 people paid a lifelong Part B late penalty in 2019,
averaging about +28%. Find newer data.

**E3.** Is there any evidence that funds pay first by mistake for COBRA or
self-pay members who have Medicare? What does Section 111 mandatory
reporting cover for group health plans, and does it include retirees and
COBRA members?

### F. Other leaks (size check only)

**F1. Other coverage.** CAQH finds other coverage for 5.5% of commercial
group members, and poor coordination costs about $800M a year. Confirm.

**F2. Work injuries.** Cost shifting from workers' comp to group health.
Confirm the NIOSH study (odds ratio 1.25, about $43 a month), and find any
union-fund-specific evidence.

**F3. Dependents.** Ineligible dependents run 3–8% (Mercer). CalPERS
removed 18,282 dependents (2.6%), about $122M. Confirm.

### G. Competition and market

**G1.** Who sells to Taft-Hartley funds or public retiree plans any of:

- SSDI advocacy
- Medicare coordination or enrollment
- MSP or Extra Help enrollment
- Part B double-payment detection

Name clients where public.

**G2.** Did Benefits Data Trust close in August 2024? Did anyone take over
its MSP and Extra Help outreach?

**G3.** Market size:

- number of multiemployer health plans and participants (about 1,400+

- median spend per participant ($13,121 in 2022)
- share covering retirees (74%)
