# Benefit funds: where the money is

October 2026. Research for the benefit-fund buyer (see `/funds` on the site).
Each figure carries its source. Lines marked *inferred* are my arithmetic or
judgment, not a published number.

## The one idea

A fund loses money when it pays a bill that Medicare, Social Security or
Medicaid should be paying. In almost every one of those cases the member is
losing money too: a missing SSDI check, a Part B premium coming out of Social
Security, prescription costs Extra Help would cover.

So the product is one thing: **get the right payer to pay, from the member's
side.** The fund's data finds the moment. A text reaches the member. Axolotl
screens, fills the application and files it on the member's YES, then follows
it to approval. The fund pays per approval and sees the savings in its own
books.

## The buyers

**Union (Taft-Hartley) health and welfare funds**

- About 1,400 multiemployer health plans file Form 5500s. They cover more than
  5 million participants, before dependents.
  ([IFEBP, 15-year landscape](https://www.ifebp.org/cebs/detail-pages/resource/the-multiemployer-health-plan-landscape-a-15-year-look-2008-2022))
- The median plan spends $13,121 per participant per year (2022).
- 74% of these plans cover retirees.
- 5 million participants × $13,121 is about **$65B a year** of fund health
  spending. *(inferred)*
- Counting pension plans too, multiemployer plans cover about 26 million
  workers, retirees and family members. ([NCCMP](https://nccmp.org/healthcare/))
- Fund offices carry real call volume: 27% get more than 2,000 participant
  calls a month.
  ([Plansponsor, Segal survey](https://www.plansponsor.com/health-plans-pose-greatest-multi-employer-plan-admin-cost/))

**Public retiree plans that reimburse Part B.** Same problem, bigger checks.

- **New York State** reimbursed $247M of standard Part B premiums in calendar
  2021. ([NYS budget](https://publications.budget.ny.gov/pubs/archive/fy25/ex/agencies/appropdata/GeneralStateCharges.pdf))
- **New York City** reimbursed $190M for 130,215 retirees and dependents in
  2009. This is the latest audited figure, and the program is larger now.
  ([NYC Comptroller audit](https://comptroller.nyc.gov/reports/audit-report-on-the-compliance-of-the-office-of-labor-relations-with-the-medicare-part-b-reimbursement-program))
- **Others:** Los Angeles, Boston (50%), Maine and Hawaii (EUTF) reimburse too.
  ([Empire Center](https://www.empirecenter.org/?p=5648),
  [Hawaii EUTF](https://eutf.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/2025-Retiree-Highlights-Guide-secured.pdf))
- **Union funds:** 1199SEIU's National Benefit Fund pays half for retirees
  with 10+ years of service.
- **Public buyers already purchase this.** Connecticut put "SSDI application
  help + Medicare enrollment for retirees" out to RFP and hired Public
  Consulting Group to do it.
  ([CT Comptroller RFP](https://osc.ct.gov:443/vendor/rfps/2023/RFP%20-%20Social%20Security%20Disability%20Application%20Assistance.pdf))

## The leaks, largest per case first

### 1. Dialysis after month 30

- **The rule:** for the first 30 months a member is Medicare-eligible because
  of kidney failure, the fund must pay first. That holds for actives,
  retirees and COBRA alike. After that, Medicare pays first, *if the member is
  enrolled*.
  ([Medicare Interactive](https://www.medicareinteractive.org/wp-content/uploads/30-month-coordination-period-factsheet.pdf))
- **Per case:** commercial plans spent **$238,126** in a patient's first year
  of dialysis, against $80,509 for Medicare. Per session it's $1,041 against
  $248. ([JAMA Netw Open via Fierce](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/238216-80509-157707-thats-how-much-more-commercial-insurers-pay-1st-year-dialysis-medicare))
- **For the member:** Medicare primary with the fund secondary usually means
  less out of pocket.
- **Guardrail:** the law forbids a plan from treating ESRD members differently
  during the 30 months. Congress is fighting over plans "pushing patients to
  Medicare"
  ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/nephrology/20240612/legislation-aimed-at-health-plans-that-push-patients-to-medicare-gets-more-co-sponsors)).
  We never steer inside the coordination period. We make sure Part B starts
  on time for month 31, so the member has no gap.
- **Volume:** few members, enormous dollars per case.

### 2. Disability, SSDI and Medicare at month 24

- **The rule:** a member who stops working because of disability can get
  SSDI. After 24 months of SSDI, they get Medicare, which pays first for
  anyone not actively working. Until then, the fund pays everything.
- **For the member:** the average SSDI check is about $1,483 a month (2024).
  First-time approval is about 36%, and a decision takes 6+ months.
  ([Atticus 2024 review](https://www.atticus.com/advice/general/2024-year-in-review-the-state-of-disability-benefits))
- **The fund already cares:** many multiemployer pension plans require an SSDI
  award before paying a disability pension.
  ([NCCMP comment](https://nccmp.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/NCCMP-Comment-to-DOL-on-Proposed-Rule-For-Claims-Procedures-for-Disability-Benefits-1-19-2016.pdf))
  So the member must file anyway, and most file alone.
- **Per case:** the fund moves from primary to secondary on a member whose
  claims run well above the $13,121 median. That's likely $10k+ a year per
  disabled member. *(inferred)*
- **Who sells this today:** Allsup, to insurers and self-insured employers
  ([Allsup](https://www.allsup.com/media/files/2017_Company-Fact-Sheet.pdf)),
  and PCG, to Connecticut. Neither reaches members by text, and neither prices
  per outcome for union funds.

### 3. Part B premiums the state would pay

This is the `/funds` example.

- **Where it applies:** only funds and public plans that reimburse Part B.
  That's concentrated in New York, which is also the easiest place to qualify.
- **The rule:** New York covers up to $2,474 a month for one person, with no
  asset test (2026).
  ([NCOA](https://assets.ncoa.org/ffacfe7d-10b6-0083-2632-604077fd4eca/78306116-4908-4802-ae2f-67968427bb55/2026_MSP_Coverage_and_Eligibility.pdf))
- **Per case:** the 2026 premium is $202.90 a month. A 50% fund saves
  **$1,217 a year**, and so does the member. A 100% reimburser like NYC or New
  York State saves $2,435.
- **The gap:** 5.8 million people nationally are eligible and not enrolled.
  Participation ran 56–64% in 2021–23, so roughly 4 in 10 eligible people
  aren't enrolled.
  ([NCOA](https://assets.ncoa.org/ffacfe7d-10b6-0083-2632-604077fd4eca/a0aed00e-4a9f-43f7-9ab6-a2618769459e/MSP_Factsheet.pdf),
  [MACPAC](https://macpac.gov/publication/medicare-savings-programs-new-estimates-continue-to-show-many-eligible-individuals-not-enrolled))
- **An illustration at 1199** *(inferred)*:
  - About 56,000 retirees are in the Aetna plan.
  - If 1 in 10 qualifies and isn't enrolled, that's 5,600 people.
  - 5,600 × $1,217 is about **$6.8M a year to the fund**, and the same to
    members.
- **A head start in the fund's own data:** the fund already holds the
  member's pension amount, so it can pre-screen income before anyone texts.
  *(inferred)*

### 4. Extra Help in retiree drug plans

- **The rule:** every Medicare Savings Program enrollee automatically
  qualifies for Extra Help.
- **For the fund:** in an employer drug plan (EGWP), CMS pays the plan
  low-income premium and cost-sharing subsidies for those members.
  ([Milliman](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/Medicare-Advantage-EGWPs-Riding-the-Baby-Boomer-wave),
  [Amwins](https://www.amwins.com/docs/default-source/taft-hartley/egwp-vs-rds-sell-sheet-2017-union.pdf))
  So leak 3 feeds leak 4.
- **Per member:** depends on the plan design. Price it with the fund's PBM.

### 5. Dependents and life events

- **The leak:** 3–8% of covered dependents can't prove eligibility. Each one
  removed saves $1,900–3,500 a year.
  ([Chard Snyder / Mercer](https://www.chard-snyder.com/employers-and-advisors/compliance-watch/does-your-health-plan-need-a-dependent-eligibility-audit))
- **Already sold:** TPAs run these audits for funds today. Zenith American
  reports $1.3M a year for one fund.
  ([Zenith](https://www.zenith-american.com/blog/significant-fund-savings-through-zeniths-dependent-audit-verification-process))
- **Our version:** catch the event when it happens, by text. A divorce, a child
  turning 26, a new baby enrolled right. That replaces a punitive audit years
  later. It's member-friendly, but the money is smaller and the market is
  crowded, so it comes last.

### Smaller, but on the same line

- **Medicaid paying the premium (HIPP):** every state must pay employer or
  COBRA premiums for Medicaid-eligible members when it's cost-effective.
  Self-pay members who would otherwise drop coverage keep it.
  ([Texas HHS](https://www.hhs.texas.gov/node/94591))
- **Turning 65:** Part B on time, no lifelong late penalty, and a clean move
  off the active plan.
- **Member services load:** texts that would otherwise be calls to the fund
  office.

## Why this is ours to take

Everyone else does one slice, from the claims side, for someone else:

| Who | What | Sold to |
|---|---|---|
| Allsup | SSDI representation, Medicare coordination | Insurers, self-insured employers |
| Public Consulting Group | SSDI + Medicare enrollment | States, by RFP (Connecticut) |
| BeneStream | Medicaid screening for low-wage workers | Employers ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/health-industry/might-your-workers-be-eligible-for-medicaid-start-up-helps-employers-find-out/)) |
| Fund TPAs (Zenith American etc.) | Dependent audits | Funds |
| Benefits Data Trust | MSP / Extra Help / SNAP outreach | States, health plans; **closed August 2024** ([Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/health/benefits-data-trust-closing-august-23-20240823.html)) |

In my searches I found no one selling union funds a member-side desk that
covers all of these on one number and charges per approval. The nearest
nonprofit, Benefits Data Trust, shut down in 2024.

**The tech that makes it ours** is a trigger engine on the fund's own files:

- eligibility
- claims
- pension
- hours

Each trigger opens a case at the right moment:

- a first dialysis claim
- a disability pension application
- a member reaching 64 years and 9 months
- a retiree whose pension plus Social Security falls under the state limit
- hours dropping to self-pay

Each case runs the same way: a text, a short screen, a filled application,
the member's YES, filing, and tracking to approval. The fund gets a monthly
ledger of dollars moved to the right payer.

## Pricing (proposal)

Paid per approval, never per screen:

| Outcome | Fee | Fund saves |
|---|---|---|
| Medicare Savings Program approval | $300 | $1,217–2,435 a year |
| SSDI award | $1,500–2,500 | $10k+ a year once Medicare is primary *(inferred)* |
| ESRD Part B start at month 31 | Flat fee | Tens of thousands a year per member |

The member never pays. That means no SSA representative fee out of their
back pay.

## Order for a first fund (1199)

1. **Medicare Savings Programs for Part B retirees.** It's cleanest, and the
   fund can count the savings by the next reimbursement cycle.
2. **SSDI** for members applying for a disability pension.
3. **Turning 65 and ESRD timing.**
4. **Life events and dependents.**

## Numbers to get from the fund

- **Part B reimbursement:**
  - retirees on it
  - annual spend
  - pension amounts by band
- **Disability pensions:**
  - applications per year
  - how many have an SSDI award
- **Dialysis:** members with dialysis claims, and their Medicare status
- **Retiree drugs:** EGWP or RDS, and the PBM's subsidy pass-through for
  members with Extra Help
