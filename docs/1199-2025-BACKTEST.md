# Backtest: 1199SEIU National Benefit Fund, 2025

**Question.** If Axolotl had been monitoring the 1199SEIU National Benefit
Fund's (NBF) retirees and the rules around them through 2025, how much Part B
leakage would it have found? Does that square with what the fund reports?

**Answer in brief.**

- Using only public filings, the model finds about **20,000 reimbursed retirees
  and spouses** whose income fell under New York's 2025 Medicare Savings
  Program (MSP) limit.
- About half of them were likely not enrolled. That's **about $9–13.5M** of
  2025 Part B reimbursement the state would have paid instead, and the same
  amount lost by retirees.
- A year of Axolotl outreach would plausibly have moved **$1.8–6.8M a year**
  (base $3.9M) off the fund. That earns **$0.5–1.8M in fees** at $300 per
  approval.
- These numbers line up with the independent estimate we already had (base
  about $3.2M at 2026 prices).
- **What public data cannot show:** the fund's actual Part B reimbursement
  spend, and the real pension distribution. Those two numbers decide whether
  the real figure is the low or the high end.

Reproduce it with: `python3 scripts/research/backtest_1199.py`

The 2025 plan-year Form 5500s aren't filed yet (due mid-October 2026), so
**plan year 2024 is the latest audited year**. 2025 is modeled on top of it.

**Audited financial statements** (2019 Form 5500, the latest found in
public PDF form; [Empire Center copy](https://www.empirecenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/NBFHHSE-5500-2019.pdf))

- Benefits paid are reported only as "Hospital and health," "Disability,"
  "Life" and "Other." **The Part B reimbursement is not broken out.**
- Retiree benefits moved to "currently payable" were $178.3M in 2019. That's
  the yearly cost of retiree benefits, of which Part B is one piece.
- The fund holds reserves for **Medicaid reclamation**: Medicaid recovering
  claims the fund should have paid. That was about $31.8M at year-end 2019
  ($6.9M drugs, $8.4M medical, $16.5M hospital).
  - This runs the opposite direction from our pitch, but it's audited proof
    that payer coordination at 1199 is a live, eight-figure issue.

## 3. The model

**Population**
- Start from the 43,673 NBF retirees receiving benefits (SOURCED).
- Assume 85% are 65+ (ASSUMED). The Part B reimbursement requires 65+ and 10
  years of service.
- Add 0.25 reimbursed spouses per retiree (ASSUMED).
- Result: **about 46,400 reimbursed Medicare lives.**
- If all of them claimed in 2025, the reimbursement would cost **about
  $51.5M**. The fund's real figure is the key unknown.

**Income** (gross, before Part B is taken out)
- **Social Security** comes from SSA's scaled-worker benefits (2025 dollars):
  very low $988, low $1,295, medium $2,140 and high $2,823 a month.
  - 40% claimed early, at 77% of the full amount (ASSUMED).
- **Pension** by tier: $400, $700, $1,150 and $2,000 a month.
- **Tier shares** of 10% / 35% / 40% / 15% (ASSUMED) reproduce the SOURCED
  average pension: the model gives $1,069 against $1,027.
- 20% lognormal spread within each tier.
- 45% are couples, with a spouse adding $1,000 a month (ASSUMED).

**Eligibility:** gross income at or under that year's New York QI-1 limit
(single or couple). QMB is a subset of that.

**Leakage** = eligible reimbursed lives × share not enrolled × the fund's
yearly 50% share.
- Share not enrolled: 40% / 50% / 60%.
- National take-up is 56–64% (MACPAC). People who have retiree coverage enroll
  less often.

**Captured by Axolotl in a year:** 20% / 35% / 50% of the unenrolled eligibles
(ASSUMED; there is no benchmark yet).

## 4. Results (2025)

**Share of reimbursed lives eligible for the MSP**

| Year | Eligible |
|---|---|
| 2024 | 42.4% |
| 2025 | 43.7% |
| 2026 | 44.8% |

**2025 eligible reimbursed lives:** about 20,300. The fund pays $1,110 a year
for each.

| | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eligible but not enrolled | 40% | 50% | 60% |
| **2025 leakage** (fund pays, state would) | **$9.0M** | **$11.3M** | **$13.5M** |
| Members' own loss | same | same | same |
| Enrollments Axolotl moves in a year | 1,624 | 3,552 | 6,090 |
| **Fund savings a year** | **$1.8M** | **$3.9M** | **$6.8M** |
| Fees at $300 | $0.49M | $1.07M | $1.83M |

**Sensitivity**

| Change | Eligible | Base leakage | Base capture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Richer mix (more RNs and techs) | 30% | $7.8M | $2.7M |
| Poorer mix (more aides and CNAs) | 57% | $14.8M | $5.2M |

Varying the married share between 30% and 60% barely moves the result
(43–44%).

## 5. Does it match what they report?

**What matches**
- The modeled pensions reproduce the SOURCED average.
- The retiree counts are consistent with the 2020 Year in Review.
- The implied ceiling on Part B reimbursement (about $51.5M) is about 2.4% of
  the $2.18B in benefits the NBF reported for 2024. That's plausible next to
  the $178M of retiree benefit cost in the 2019 audit.
- The base capture (about $3.9M at 2025 prices) agrees with the independent
  research agent's base of about $3.2M.

**What public data can't confirm**
- The NBF does not report its Part B reimbursement spend, or how many retirees
  claim it.
- It does not report how pensions are spread, only totals.
- It does not report how many retirees are already in the MSP.

**What the fund's files would settle in an afternoon**
1. The Part B reimbursement file: who is reimbursed, and how much.
2. Pension amounts, in bands.
3. Whether a retiree's reimbursement has already stopped, which signals an
   existing state buy-in.

That's the free scan (`FUNDS-LEAK-SCAN.md`), and it can run on de-identified
data.

## 6. What this tells us

- **The opportunity is real at 1199's scale.**
  - At the base case, about **$11M a year** of Part B reimbursement goes to
    retirees the state would cover.
  - About **$4M a year** of it is capturable in year one.
  - Members recover the same again, plus Extra Help.
- **Continuous monitoring matters, not just a one-off scan.**
  - The limits, the premium and the COLA move every year.
  - QI-1 must be renewed every calendar year.
  - 2024→2025 alone added about 630 newly eligible lives.
- **The biggest swing factor is the workforce mix.** That ranges from about
  $8M to $15M of leakage, and only the pension file resolves it. Ask for
  pension bands first.
