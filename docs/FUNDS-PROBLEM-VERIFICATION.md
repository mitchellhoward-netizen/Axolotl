# Is the problem real? Verification of the wrong-payer thesis

October 2026. Each leak in `FUNDS-WRONG-PAYER.md` and on the homepage,
tested against government audits, court records, CMS and SSA data, fund
documents and peer-reviewed studies. I looked as hard for evidence that each
leak is *already handled* as for evidence that it exists.

**Verdicts**

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Verified** | Hard evidence that the fund (or plan) pays money another payer owes |
| **Real, but the member pays** | The gap exists, but plans already protect themselves, so the cost lands on the member, not the fund |
| **Real, claims-side** | It exists, and others already sell the fix from claims data |
| **Unverified** | Plausible, no hard evidence found |

## Summary

| # | Leak | Verdict | Change from before |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Part B reimbursed for retirees who qualify for a Medicare Savings Program | **Verified, and larger than I estimated at 1199** | Upgraded |
| 2 | Part B reimbursed twice, or after death | **Verified** | — |
| 3 | Disability → SSDI → Medicare | **Verified as a market, but the fund saving depends on plan design** | Narrowed; the $76,540 figure was misused |
| 4 | Dialysis after month 30 | **Real, but the member pays** | **Downgraded.** Not a big fund cost |
| 5 | Retirees 65+ without Part B | **Real, but the member pays** | **Downgraded** |
| 6 | COBRA members with Medicare | Unverified | — |
| 7 | Other coverage (e.g. a spouse's plan) | **Real, claims-side** | — |
| 8 | Work injuries billed to the health plan | **Real, claims-side**, small per member | — |
| 9 | Car accidents (no-fault) | Rule verified, size unknown | — |
| 10 | Ineligible dependents | **Real, claims-side** | — |
| 11 | Coverage or payments after death | **Verified** as a recurring audit finding, size unknown | — |

**Bottom line**

The wrong-payer problem is real, but it is smaller and shaped differently
than the homepage says.

- **Where union funds lose the most verified money:**
  - **Part B reimbursements for low-pension retirees** who qualify for the
    state program. This is big at a fund like 1199.
  - **Double payments.**
  - **Disability, but only where disability retirees keep fund coverage
    long-term.** That's typical of public plans, which is why they already
    buy it.
- **Dialysis and retirees without Part B are mainly member problems.** Plans
  already stop paying first; the member is the one who gets hurt. Those are
  still worth solving for members, but they are not fund savings.

## 1. Part B reimbursement → Medicare Savings Program: **Verified, upgraded**

**The rule**

- New York's state employee plan (NYSHIP) reimburses Part B only when "the
  premium is not paid by another entity."
  ([NY Civil Service](https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/nyship/group/5/17/1/medicare-part-b.cfm))
- 1199's National Benefit Fund (NBF) reimburses 50% of the standard Part B
  premium to retirees 65+ with 10 years of service. Retirees claim it
  quarterly.
  ([1199SEIU NBF retirees](https://1199seiubenefits.org/hce-pension/retiree-health-benefits),
  [claim form](https://www.1199seiubenefits.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Medicare-Part-B-Reimbursement-Claim.pdf))
- Once the state pays a retiree's premium, the fund's reimbursement stops.

**1199's own numbers** (2020 Year in Review, pp. 3 and 6):

| | Figure |
|---|---|
| NBF retirees | 56,849 |
| Health Care Employees Pension Fund | 81,700 retirees, $951.4M paid, so about **$970 a month** on average |
| Greater New York Pension Fund | 12,843 retirees, $79.7M, so about **$517 a month** |
| Home Care Pension Fund | 24,777 retirees, $23.0M, so about **$77 a month** |

([1199SEIU 2020 report](https://www.1199seiubenefits.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/2020-Annual-Report-.pdf))

**What qualifies**

- Lower earners who retired at 65 averaged about $1,235 a month from Social
  Security in 2024.
  ([Yahoo Finance / SSA data](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/might-lower-class-retiree-social-173000241.html))
- New York's 2026 limit for one person is $2,474 a month, with no asset
  test.
  ([NCOA](https://assets.ncoa.org/ffacfe7d-10b6-0083-2632-604077fd4eca/78306116-4908-4802-ae2f-67968427bb55/2026_MSP_Coverage_and_Eligibility.pdf))
- $970 + about $1,300 is about $2,270, **under the limit**. The *average*
  single NBF retiree may qualify.

**Estimate** *(inferred)*

- Averages hide the spread: registered nurses retire on more.
- Assume a third to a half of NBF retirees qualify, and about 40% of those
  aren't enrolled (national take-up is 56–64%,
  [MACPAC](https://macpac.gov/publication/medicare-savings-programs-new-estimates-continue-to-show-many-eligible-individuals-not-enrolled)).
- *(Superseded: see "Independent check" below. Expected capture is about
  $3M a year at base, $0.6–9.7M in range. This $9–14M was closer to the
  ceiling.)* That's about 7,500–11,000 retirees × $1,217 = **about $9–14M a year to
  the NBF**, and the same back to retirees.
- That's 1.5–2× my earlier "$6.8M" guess.

**What could make it smaller**

- Retirees who never claim the reimbursement cost the fund nothing today.
  Ask 1199 for its actual annual Part B reimbursement spend.
- Some retirees are already enrolled.

## 2. Part B reimbursed twice, or after death: **Verified**

- **Nassau County:** 94 retirees and dependents were paid twice.
  - $1,507,097 confirmed, $1,623,111 suspected.
  - $1.36M still outstanding as of June 2026.
  - The cause: a spouse drawing the same reimbursement from two public
    employers.
  - ([Nassau Comptroller](https://www.nassaucountyny.gov/DocumentCenter/View/53259/Review-of-Medicare-Reimbursements))
- **School districts:** New York's state comptroller (OSC) audits districts
  for paying premiums for retirees who have died, because "officials did not
  independently verify whether retirees were living."
  ([NY OSC](https://osc.state.ny.us/localgov/audits/schools/2018/plainview-old-bethpage.htm))
- **Implication:** every Medicare Savings Program approval must also stop the
  fund's reimbursement, or the fund pays twice. That check belongs in the
  product.

## 3. Disability → SSDI → Medicare: **Verified market, narrower fund savings**

**What's verified**

- Plan sponsors pay for it:
  - New Jersey's state and school employee health plans (SHBP/SEHBP) and
    Boeing use SSDC.
  - Connecticut put it out to RFP and hired PCG.
  - Allsup sells "Medicare Coordination."
  - (Sources in `FUNDS-COMPETITORS.md`.)
- About 3.8 SSDI awards per 1,000 insured workers a year: 594,749 disabled
  worker awards in 2024.
  ([SSA](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/statcomps/di_asr/2024/sect03.html))
- A multiemployer plan stays primary for *active* disabled members if any
  contributing employer has 100+ employees.
  ([HHS MSP model](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/sites/default/files/hhs-guidance-documents/MSP-Model-for-Multi-Employer-Multiple-Employer.pdf))

**What narrows it: union fund plan design**

- Funds typically extend coverage for a totally disabled member "for a
  maximum of 30 months … or until you are eligible for Medicare, whichever
  comes first."
  ([South Central IBEW-NECA](https://www.scibew-neca.org/html/hspd2210.htm),
  [IUOE Local 98](https://iuoelocal98.org/app/uploads/2025/01/IUOE-98-Health-SMM-Disability-Benefit-2025.pdf))
- That cap roughly matches SSDI's timeline: a 5-month wait, then Medicare 24
  months into benefits.
- **So in those funds, faster SSDI saves the fund little.** It saves the
  *member* a lot:
  - a monthly check of about $1,483 on average
  - no coverage cliff at month 30 if the award comes late
- **The big plan savings are where disability retirees keep plan coverage
  for years.** That's why public plans (New Jersey, Connecticut) buy this.
  Union funds whose disability pensioners keep retiree health are in the same
  position.

**Correction**

The "$76,540 lifetime savings per transition" figure I used comes from a
vendor's actuarial estimate for *employees moving to Medicare at 65 or older*
(the "Medicare Planning Fringe Benefit"). It is not about SSDI.
([SHRM vendor directory](https://vendordirectory.shrm.org/company/935113/health-wealth-advisors-llc))
It should not appear in our SSDI math. Replace it with the fund's own claims
for disabled members once Medicare would pay first.

## 4. Dialysis after month 30: **Real, but the member pays (downgraded)**

**What's verified**

- **The price gap is huge.** Commercial plans spent $238,126 in a patient's
  first year of dialysis, against $80,509 for Medicare. Per session: $1,041
  against $248.
  ([JAMA Netw Open via Fierce](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/238216-80509-157707-thats-how-much-more-commercial-insurers-pay-1st-year-dialysis-medicare))
- **Providers profit from commercial coverage.** In its 2016 rule, CMS said
  provider steering of dialysis patients toward commercial coverage for
  financial gain was widespread. Dialysis companies sued and blocked the rule.
  ([Fierce](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payer/insurance-groups-seek-relief-from-hhs-over-payments-to-dialysis-patients),
  [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/nephrology/20180227/davita-ceo-dialysis-providers-sued-cms-with-great))
- **Plans fight these costs.** In *Marietta v. DaVita* (2022) the Supreme
  Court let plans pay all dialysis providers the same low out-of-network rate.
  ([Supreme Court opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/21pdf/596us2r53_l6h1.pdf))

**What disproves the fund-savings claim**

- **The coordination period is the law.** During it, the plan must pay first,
  and it can't treat ESRD members differently. There's no savings to capture
  there.
- **Plans already protect themselves after month 30.** After 30 months of
  eligibility, "whether enrolled or not … most [plans] stop paying primary
  benefits."
  ([Medicare Interactive](https://medicareinteractive.org/pdf/Medicare-and-ESRD-FAQ.pdf))
  NYSHIP *terminates* coverage for members who don't enroll.
  ([NYSHIP ESRD flyer 2026](https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/pa-market/shared/publications/medicare/2026/esrd-nyship-flyer-2026.pdf))
- **The coordination-period group is small.** Only about 6% of ESRD
  patients are Medicare-secondary under a job-based plan at any time (2019).
  ([USRDS 2022 ADR](https://usrds-adr.niddk.nih.gov/2022/end-stage-renal-disease/9-healthcare-expenditures-for-persons-with-esrd))

**Verdict**

- The fund saving I put on the homepage ("about $238,000 in the first
  year … Medicare first, once enrolled") **overstates it**.
- The real harm after month 30 is to the member: little or no coverage, or
  terminated coverage, if Part B isn't in place.
- It's still worth doing as member protection. It is not a fund-savings line.

## 5. Retirees 65+ without Part B: **Real, but the member pays (downgraded)**

- **Standard plan design already protects the fund:**
  - "carve-out" coordination (pay no more than if Medicare had paid)
  - or a group Medicare Advantage plan that requires Parts A and B. 1199 does
    this: "you must be enrolled in both Medicare Part A and Part B."
  - ([Hawaii EUTF statute text](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/session2010/Bills/HB2537_.pdf),
    [1199SEIU](https://www.1199seiubenefits.org/nbf-retirees-aetna/))
- **The member pays instead:** about 763,000 people paid a lifelong Part B
  late penalty in 2019, averaging about 28% on their premium.
  ([Senate Aging, BENES Act brief](https://www.aging.senate.gov/download/benes-act-data-brief?download=1))
- **Verdict:** a member problem, not a fund cost, except at a fund whose plan
  document doesn't assume Medicare. Ask for the plan document.

## 6. COBRA members with Medicare: **Unverified**

- **The rule is verified:** Medicare pays first for aged or disabled COBRA
  members.
  ([Newfront](https://www.newfront.com/blog/how-cobra-and-medicare-interact-for-retirees))
- **No evidence found** that funds systematically pay first by mistake.
- **Mandatory CMS reporting covers it partly.** Under Section 111, plans
  report covered people 45+ and anyone with ESRD, and get Medicare
  entitlement back from CMS.
  ([CMS GHP overview](https://edit.cms.gov/Medicare/Coordination-of-Benefits-and-Recovery/Mandatory-Insurer-Reporting-For-Group-Health-Plans/GHP-Training-Material/Downloads/GHP-Process-Overview.pdf))
- **Leave it for the scan to measure.**

## 7–11. Other insurers and eligibility: **real, mostly claims-side**

- **Other coverage:** CAQH finds other coverage for 5.4% of commercial group
  members (5.5% is its Medicaid figure). The $800M a year is *administrative*
  cost, mostly borne by providers (Booz, 2010), not wrongly paid claims.
  ([CAQH](https://www.caqh.org/sites/default/files/solutions/cob-smart/cob-smart-one-pager.pdf))
  CAQH, plan vendors and TPAs already work it. Our part is asking the
  member at enrollment and at life events.
- **Work injuries:** members with prior workers' comp claims had 25% higher
  odds of a group health claim and about $43 a month more in costs. That's
  evidence of cost shifting, but small per member.
  ([CDC/NIOSH, Bhattacharya & Park](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/194065))
- **Car accidents:** no-fault pays first for the first $50,000 in New York.
  ([Albany Med](https://www.albanymed.org/?p=73843)) Size unknown.
- **Dependents:**
  - 3–8% of dependents can't prove eligibility (Mercer).
  - CalPERS removed 18,282 (2.6%), about $122M.
  - ([Chard Snyder](https://www.chard-snyder.com/employers-and-advisors/compliance-watch/does-your-health-plan-need-a-dependent-eligibility-audit),
    [CalPERS](https://www.calpers.ca.gov/docs/board-agendas/201510/pension/item-7.pdf))
  - TPAs already sell audits.
- **Context: payer confusion is chronic.** OIG and GAO found Medicare paying
  first by mistake when private plans should have:
  - $85M at Empire over 82 months
  - more than $1B in contractor backlogs
  - ([OIG](https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/1994/review-of-empire-blue-cross-blue-shields-compliance-with-the-medicare-secondary-payer-statutory-provisions),
    [GAO HRD-92-52](https://www.gao.gov/assets/hrd-92-52.pdf))
  - That's the opposite direction, but it shows coordination errors are
    routine.

## What this changes

**1. The fund pitch leads with what's verified**
- Part B reimbursements for retirees the state should cover.
- Double and after-death payments.
- For plans that keep disability retirees on coverage, SSDI and Medicare.

**2. Dialysis and turning 65 move from "fund savings" to "member protection"**
- They still belong on the text line.
- They come off the fund money table.

**3. The homepage needs corrections**
- **The money table:**
  - The dialysis row claims the fund pays about $238K that Medicare should
    pay.
  - The turning-65 row implies a fund cost.
- **The scan's "$2–6M for a 30,000-member fund":** it was built on the
  misused $76,540 figure and on dialysis savings. Replace it with an estimate
  tied to Part B reimbursement and double payments, or with "the scan
  produces your number."

**4. The first target is clearer**
- **Who:** a fund that reimburses Part B for low-pension retirees (1199 NBF),
  or a public plan that reimburses Part B and covers disability retirees
  (NYC, New York State, school-district consortia).
- **Why:** that's where the money is verified.

**5. Ask every fund for these first**
- The plan document's Medicare language
- The disability coverage extension rules
- Annual Part B reimbursement spend and headcount
- Pension amounts by band

## Independent check (outside research agent, October 2026)

An outside research agent re-checked sections F and G of
`RESEARCH-BRIEF-FUNDS.md` and rebuilt two estimates. **Sections A–E have not
been independently checked yet.**

**Changes we accepted**

- **NBF Part B → Medicare Savings Program**
  - New estimate: **$0.64M / $3.2M / $9.7M a year** (low / base / high).
  - The base assumes:
    - 50,000 reimbursed retirees and spouses
    - 25% income-eligible
    - 60% of those not yet enrolled
    - 35% actually enrolled by us
  - The ceiling, if every unenrolled eligible person enrolled, is about
    $9.1M.
  - My $9–14M was effectively that ceiling, not expected capture.
  - The homepage now says "$3–9 million."
  - Newer anchor: 1199 paid $842.4M to 74,502 retirees in 2017, about $942 a
    month. That's consistent with the 2020 figures.
  - New York's QI limit is cited as $2,494 a month single and $3,375 for a
    couple. The $20 disregard likely explains the gap from $2,474. Confirm.
- **Double payment at the NBF is probably rare.** Reimbursement claims
  require the SSA award letter every year, which would show a state buy-in.
  Double payment stays a *public-plan* proof point (Nassau: 99 dependents also
  had another employer's retiree coverage, a problem running two decades).
  It's not an NBF pitch.
- **Retiree coverage:**
  - 74% of multiemployer plans cover retirees of any age, but only **44%
    cover retirees after 65** (2026 survey).
  - 55% of service-industry plans cover retirees at all.
  - 1,478 plans cover 5.31M participants (IFEBP toolkit).
  - The homepage stat is now "44% of funds cover retirees after 65."
- **Disability savings per disabled member** (derived):

  | Plan design | Low | Base | High |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Keeps disability retirees on coverage, each year after Medicare starts | about $9.4K | about $22.1K | about $36.7K |
  | Caps coverage at 30 months, one-time | $0 | about $1.9K | about $5.8K |

  The base uses Allsup's unaudited claim of a 71% cost reduction. Speeding up
  an SSDI award doesn't move Medicare's start date, which is always 24 months
  after SSDI entitlement.
- **Other coverage:** 5.4% of commercial group members, not 5.5%. The $800M
  is administrative cost, not a leak size.
- **Work injuries:** the $43 a month (NIOSH) applies only to nonunion hourly
  men aged 18–34. The largest subgroup increase was $66. No union-fund study
  exists, only vendors (Intellivo sells subrogation to Taft-Hartley plans).
- **Dependents:**
  - Use 2.6–5% for union or public plans (CalPERS realized 2.6%).
  - CalPERS's $122M is cumulative over 2013–2015 and includes modeled
    claims avoidance. It is not annual.
  - Mercer now says 3–10%.

**Competition confirmed**

- Allsup sells disability-to-Medicare coordination to group plans.
- Via Benefits gives Medicare counseling to SAG-AFTRA Health Plan members.
- No vendor was found selling MSP or Extra Help enrollment to Taft-Hartley or
  public retiree plans.
- Benefits Data Trust closed August 24, 2024 (273 jobs) with no national
  successor. In its last year, it filed 23,000 Pennsylvania Extra Help
  applications.

**New leaks it flagged**

- ESRD coordination at the NBF: the fund already has an ESRD Part B form.
- Payments after death: NYC's 2011 audit told the city's Office of Labor
  Relations to check dates of death before reimbursing.
- Accident liability: a vendor claims 5–6% of paid claims. Unverified.
- Group Part D low-income subsidy capture: depends on the drug plan contract.
  Unverified.

## Review fixes (October 2026)

**New York Medicare Savings Program limits for 2026** (verified,
[NLS](https://nls.org/app/uploads/2025/05/Medicare-Savings-Programs-2026-FINAL-3-9-26.pdf))

| Program | One person | Couple |
|---|---|---|
| QMB | up to $1,836 | up to $2,489 |
| QI-1 | $1,836–2,474 | $2,489–3,355 |

- These limits are countable income, after the $20 disregard. So a member
  compares about **$2,494** (single) or **about $3,375** (couple) against
  their gross checks.
- Income counts *before* Part B is taken out of Social Security.
- QI pays Part B only, renews every year, and is first-come, first-served.
- The homepage phone now:
  - uses $2,494
  - asks whether it's just the member or a spouse too
  - asks for income before Medicare is taken out
  - says the fund's half stops, so the member keeps about $101 a month more,
    plus Extra Help
  - promises the yearly renewal
  - ends with "Sent … when the state confirms", not "Filed"

**SSDI help** (verified)
- Helping someone fill out an application doesn't require being an appointed
  representative.
  ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/retirement/social-security/questions-answers/help-applying-for-disability-benefits.html))
- A third party can pay a representative without SSA authorizing the fee, if
  all of these hold:
  - the claimant owes nothing
  - a third-party entity pays
  - the representative waives fees from the claimant on SSA-1696-U4
  - ([POMS GN 03920.010](https://secure.ssa.gov/POMS.nsf/lnx/0203920010))
- A fund paying per award can fit this. Confirm it with counsel before
  selling disability.
- The hero now says "help with disability claims," not "disability checks."

**Framing for 1199**
- The headline now says "would cover," not "should pay."
- The sub leads with "members who are owed help."
- Both work for a union that defends Medicaid funding in public.
