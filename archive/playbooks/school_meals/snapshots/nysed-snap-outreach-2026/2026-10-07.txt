Subject:

Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) Outreach Requirements

Purpose

This memorandum provides information on the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) outreach requirements established under Education Law §915‑a and the implementing regulations in Section 114.6 of the Regulations of the Commissioner of Education, effective January 28, 2026, and offers guidance to support schools in carrying out these requirements. These requirements apply to all school districts, charter schools and non-public schools that operate as School Food Authorities (SFAs) in New York State. The overarching goal of these requirements is to ensure that families receive timely, accessible information about SNAP eligibility and enrollment, thereby maximizing direct certification for federal free‑rate school meals.

Background

The Enacted State Budget for the 2025-2026 fiscal year (Part B of Chapter 56 of the Laws of 2025) added section 915-a to the Education Law, effective July 1, 2025, requiring all school districts, charter schools and non-public schools in the State that participate in the National School Lunch or School Breakfast programs to participate in the Universal Free School Meals Program by serving breakfast and lunch at no cost to students. Additionally, the law required the New York State Education Department (SED), in consultation with the Office of Temporary and Disability Assistance (OTDA), to promulgate rules and regulations for school districts, charter schools and non-public schools to provide SNAP outreach.

Consistent with Education Law §915-a, new Section 114.6 has been added to the Commissioner’s regulations to implement the new SNAP outreach requirements.

Requirements

School districts, charter schools and non-public schools operating as SFAs must develop and implement procedures designed to inform low-income households about the availability and eligibility requirements for SNAP and the process to enroll in SNAP for the purpose of maximizing direct certification for free school meals. Such procedures must include at least one of the following strategies:

Direct referrals to local SNAP outreach providers designated by the OTDA, which offer eligibility pre‑screening, application assistance, and program education.

Distribution of SNAP educational materials during school registration or at other appropriate points of contact with families.

Direct referrals to local social services districts.

SFAs are encouraged to select the strategy, or combination of strategies, that best addresses their local needs and supports effective outreach.

Implementation Guidance

The following best practices support schools in carrying out outreach strategies.

Align Outreach with Existing School Communication Cycles

Integrating SNAP information into routine communication points increases the likelihood that eligible families will engage with available support. Schools can incorporate referrals or educational materials into established communication moments, such as:

Back‑to‑school packets

Mid‑year updates or progress report periods

End‑of‑year communications

Parent‑teacher conferences

School newsletters or email bulletins

This approach reduces administrative burden while ensuring families receive information at meaningful intervals.

Use Public‑Facing Platforms to Increase Accessibility

To support broad and discreet access to SNAP information, whether through educational materials or referral pathways, schools are encouraged to use widely accessed communication channels, including:

District and school websites

Parent portals

Social media platforms

Community bulletin boards

Posting materials in these locations helps reduce stigma and allows families to access information privately and at their convenience.

Strengthen Referral Pathways

For schools choosing to make direct referrals, either to OTDA‑designated SNAP outreach providers or to local social services districts, effectiveness can be enhanced by:

Providing warm handoffs to outreach providers. This can include introducing the family to an outreach worker during a school event, making a brief phone or email introduction on the family’s behalf, or helping the family schedule a time to speak with an outreach provider. The goal is to reduce barriers, build comfort, and ensure families receive timely support in completing SNAP applications.

Conducting targeted outreach to families who may benefit from additional support. Reaching out to households experiencing recent changes such as job loss or reduced hours, or connecting with families who have expressed difficulty meeting basic needs. Schools may also collaborate with counselors, family liaisons, and community partners to ensure outreach is culturally responsive, linguistically accessible, and tailored to the specific circumstances of each family. The goal is to proactively engage families who are most likely to benefit from SNAP but may not be aware of their eligibility or may need additional support navigating the application process.

Leverage Existing Data and Technology

Schools can streamline outreach, whether distributing materials or facilitating referrals, by integrating SNAP information into existing digital systems. Strategies include:

Add the myBenefits link to the SFA website and include it in digital communications to guide families to the OTDA‑approved SNAP prescreening tool (“Am I Eligible?”) and the online application for SNAP and other programs.

Embed SNAP information in online registration systems.

Use automated messaging platforms to send reminders or share resources.

Ensure Meaningful Access for Families with Limited English Proficiency

Schools are reminded that under Title VI of the Civil Rights Act, SFAs must take reasonable steps to ensure that individuals with Limited English Proficiency (LEP) have meaningful access to information about federally assisted child nutrition programs. This may include:

Providing translated SNAP materials

Using interpreters or bilingual staff

Ensuring culturally responsive communication

Next Steps

Select at least one required outreach strategy and incorporate it into local procedures.

Document the procedures and ensure staff responsible for registration, family engagement, or food service are aware of them.

Integrate recommended best practices where feasible to strengthen outreach and support families.

Monitor implementation and adjust strategies as needed to meet community needs.
