# Public-employer disability retirees under 65: is there an SSDI/Medicare market?

Researched 2026-10-07. All sources accessed 2026-10-07. Labels: **verified** means I read the quote
in the primary document (most PDFs were downloaded and searched as text). **secondary** means a
search summary or a non-primary source. **inferred** means my arithmetic or judgement, with the
working shown. The prior notes this builds on are
[`../disability_fund_economics/README.md`](../disability_fund_economics/README.md) and
[`health_costs.md`](../disability_fund_economics/health_costs.md).

## Summary (plain English)

1. The rule is real. NYC and New York State keep disability retirees on retiree health for life. Both tell disabled retirees to enroll in Medicare A and B as soon as they qualify, and then pay second. Both also refund the Part B premium (findings 7–11).
2. The pool is mid-sized. About **32,900 disability retirees under 65** are in the eight NY systems (NYCERS, Police, Fire, TRS, BERS, NYSLRS ERS and PFRS, NYSTRS). **About 17,600 of them are uniformed police and fire** (findings 1–5).
3. Many are not on Medicare. The actuaries themselves assume only **11–13% of NYC police/fire, 38–45% of NYC civilian/teacher, 50% of State PFRS and 80% of other State** disability retirees ever get Medicare before 65 (findings 12–13). That leaves **about 20,000 under-65 disability retirees not on Medicare. About 14,000 of them are police or fire** (inferred).
4. Most of that gap is not winnable. Police and fire disability pensions are granted for being unable to do *police or fire work*, while SSDI requires being unable to do *any* substantial work (findings 14–15). The gap is mainly people SSA would not find disabled, not people who never applied. In the civilian systems it may be partly people who never applied.
5. A time limit cuts it further. SSDI insured status lapses about five years after a person stops working, and back pay covers at most 12 months (findings 16–17). So the long-retired stock is mostly closed off. **New disability retirements (about 1,000+ a year that we can count) are the real market.**
6. **Estimate (inferred): about 450 / 1,160 / 2,380 extra SSDI awards (low / middle / high) from a one-time sweep of the current stock.** Net of the Part B refund, each saves the employer about **$9k / $14k / $20k per Medicare member-year**. That is **$4M / $16M / $48M a year** once all of them are on Medicare, and roughly **$12M / $73M / $286M** over the years left before 65 (arithmetic §A).
7. The per-member saving is smaller than the union-fund figures because the employer refunds Part B. NYC also adds a 12.5% IRMAA load, and NYC's drug cost often sits with union welfare funds, not the City (findings 9, 20, §A).
8. **Connecticut already buys exactly this.** Its Comptroller issued RFPs in 2018 and 2023 for vendors to help disability retirees apply for SSDI, appeal denials and enroll in Medicare, "without cost to the member". It is tied to a 2017 union agreement that makes applying for SSDI mandatory (finding 23). That is the template, and the proof that a public employer will pay. It paid hourly rates, though, not a contingency fee.
9. The member pitch is strong where the pension has no offset: SSDI is pure extra income, about $1,630 a month on average in 2026. That covers NYC Tier 4/6 civilians, NYS Article 15 retirees and Tier 3 Enhanced police and fire. Members under an older Tier 3/Article 14 plan lose 50% of the SSDI amount from their pension but still come out ahead (findings 19, 24, 25).
10. **LA County is mostly out.** It left Social Security in 1983, so its disability retirees rarely have SSDI insured status. A Medicare-only path (MQGE) exists for post-1986 hires but is a niche (findings 6, 22). **Bottom line: real, but a five-to-low-eight-figure-a-year niche inside NY, not a large new market. Sell it as a flow product (new disability retirees) bundled with Part B/MSP work, on the Connecticut model.**

### B. Do they keep employer health coverage, and must they take Medicare?

**7. NYC: accidental-disability retirees get retiree health with no service minimum.** Label: verified.
- NYC OPEB FY2025 report (p.6): retirees need "At least 10 years of credited service … no service requirement for accidental disability retirements" and "Receives a pension check from the NYCRS".
  - URL: https://www.nyc.gov/assets/actuary/downloads/pdf/OPEB_GASB_7475_Report_FY2025.pdf
- NYC Health Benefits Program SPD (PDF dated Sept 2026, p.17): "If you retire from the City or the Department of Education because of an accidental disability … you are eligible for retiree health benefits."
  - URL: https://www.nyc.gov/assets/olr/downloads/pdf/health/health-full-spd.pdf

**8. NYC: enroll in Medicare as soon as eligible, including on disability.** Label: verified (SPD p.22).
- "To avoid a reduction of your benefits, it is essential that you join Medicare Part A … and Part B … as soon as you are eligible. If you do not join Medicare, you will lose whatever benefits Medicare would have provided."
- "If you are over 65 or eligible for Medicare due to disability and did not join Medicare, contact your Social Security Office".
- The SPD does not contain an explicit "must apply for SSDI" rule.
- OPEB p.10: "GHI/EBCBS Senior Care coordinates with Medicare Part A and Medicare Part B on a supplemental basis."
- Same URLs as finding 7.

**9. NYC refunds Part B, including IRMAA, to Medicare retirees (§12-126).** Label: verified.
- OPEB p.10: "the City … reimburse[s] the Medicare Part B Premium for all Medicare-eligible retirees and eligible covered dependents. The reimbursement includes the base Part B premium, and any additional premium charged to high-income retirees".
- OPEB p.22: "for FY 2025 valuation the load applied to base premiums was 12.5%" (for IRMAA) and "90% of Medicare participants are assumed to claim reimbursement".
- The SPD (p.24) conditions include that the retiree is "not receiving Medicare Part B reimbursement(s) from any other source, including Medicaid."
- **So the net saving to the City is reduced by about $2,435 × 1.125 ≈ $2,739 a year** (inferred, 2026 premium).

**10. NYSHIP: disability retirees must enroll in Medicare the moment they are eligible, and NYSHIP then pays second.** Label: verified.
- NYS Civil Service flyer, "Disability Retirees Must Enroll in Medicare As soon as Eligible Or Risk Loss of Medical Benefits". It is an older flyer: it cites the 2003 Part A deductible.
  - "you must be enrolled in Medicare … as soon as you are eligible – even if you are not yet 65 years old. You must be enrolled in Medicare Part A and Part B."
  - "Many State employees who are eligible for a Disability Retirement are also eligible for a Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) allowance … After 24 months of SSDI eligibility, you automatically become eligible for, and must enroll in, Medicare Parts A and B."
  - "NYSHIP will not provide any benefits for coverage available under Medicare."
  - "1. Medicare pays first. 2. NYSHIP (Empire Plan or HMO) pays second."
  - "New York State will reimburse you for the cost of the Medicare Part B premium unless you receive reimbursement from another source."
  - If SSA denies, "send a photocopy of this determination … Your NYSHIP benefits will continue until you become eligible for Medicare coverage." This shows NYSHIP tracks SSDI applications but does not mandate them (inferred).
  - URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/hba/shared/reports/disability.pdf
- Current booklet (Medicare & NYSHIP for NY/PE Retirees, March 2022, p.2): "If you or a covered dependent becomes eligible for Medicare due to disability prior to age 65, you/your dependent must have Medicare Parts A and B coverage in effect on the first day of eligibility for Medicare coverage that is primary to NYSHIP."
  - URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/nyship/shared/publications/medicare/2022/ny-pe-medicare-nyship-2022.pdf

**11. Federal pay order: Medicare is primary over retiree coverage.** Label: verified in the prior note.
- CMS chart: "If you have retiree health coverage ... Medicare pays first." See [`medicare_pay_order.md` §3e](../disability_fund_economics/medicare_pay_order.md).

### D. Why the gap is mostly not SSDI-eligible

**14. The pension disability tests are occupation-specific for uniformed services and close to SSDI for teachers.** Label: verified.
- Police (Tier 2 SPD in ACFR): "An application for Ordinary or Accident Disability may be made by a member who is incapacitated from performing the duties of a police officer." ADR requires the Medical Board to find the member "unable to perform police duties as the result of an accident sustained in the line of duty".
- TRS (ACFR plan summary): ordinary disability if "incapacitated for performance of gainful employment."
- URLs as in findings 2 and 4.

**15. The SSDI test: "any substantial gainful activity".** Label: verified.
- 20 CFR 404.1505(a): "The law defines disability as the inability to do any substantial gainful activity by reason of any medically determinable physical or mental impairment … which has lasted or can be expected to last for a continuous period of not less than 12 months."
- URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/404.1505
- Inferred: this matches the actuaries' 11–13% for police and fire against 45% for TRS/BERS. A firefighter retired at 50 on a knee or a WTC presumption is usually able to do other work.

**16. Insured status lapses (the 20/40 rule).** Label: verified (rule); the five-year reading is inferred.
- 20 CFR 404.130(b): "You must meet the 20/40 requirement … (2) You have at least 20 QCs in the 40-quarter period … ending with that quarter."
- Inferred: a retiree who stopped covered work more than about 5 years ago can win only by proving disability began before their date last insured.
- URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/404.130

**17. Back pay is limited.** Label: verified.
- 20 CFR 404.621(a)(1): "you may receive benefits for up to 12 months immediately before the month in which your application is filed."
- URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/404.621

### E. Social Security coverage, SSDI requirements and offsets

**18. NY public employees are SS-covered; NYC uniformed are covered too.** Label: secondary and verified.
- CRS R46961 (2018 data) gives NY 94.9% of state and local employees covered. This is quoted in [`../public_employers/pension_distributions.md` §7](../public_employers/pension_distributions.md) (secondary).
- FDNY lets members reduce contributions "up to the amount of Social Security (FICA) contributions" (verified, Fire ACFR). Police: "Members may only stop member contributions until earnings for the year reach the level upon which FICA (Social Security) taxes are no longer imposed" (verified, Police ACFR).

**19. Where SSDI is *required*, and where it is *offset*.** Label: verified.
- **NYCERS Tier 3 (Article 14; ACFR p.63).** Ordinary disability requires being "deemed disabled by the Social Security Administration (SSA)". The benefit is "minus 50% of PSSB and 100% of Workers' Compensation payments". Accidental disability is "Deemed disabled by the SSA for the claimed body part".
- **NYCERS Tier 4 and 6 basic plans.** The disability benefit is "The greater of ⅓ of FAS; 1 ⅔% x FAS x years of service; or … the Service Retirement Benefit", with no SSA test and no SS offset.
- **NYSLRS Article 14 (Tier 3/4).** Requires that you "have been awarded primary Social Security disability benefits". The benefit is "reduced by 50 percent of your CO-ESC Social Security disability benefit". Article 15 "is usually greater" and is "not offset by any Social Security disability or Workers' Compensation benefits."
  - URL: https://osc.ny.gov/retirement/publications/1522/tier-3-ordinary-disability-article-14
- **NYC Police Tier 3 (Tier 3 SPD Oct 2024, p.14, in ACFR).** "Non-Tier 3 Enhanced members who retire for Ordinary Disability Retirement ('ODR') will have their benefit reduced by 50% of their primary Social Security Disability Insurance ('SSDI')." Non-enhanced ADR retirees "Upon receipt of SSDI … will have their benefit reduced by 50% of their SSDI".
- **NYC Fire Tier 3 (ACFR p.62, p.86, p.87).**
  - An ODR allowance is payable to a member who "is in receipt of Social Security Disability Benefits (non-Enhanced members only)".
  - "Tier 3 Enhanced Members who retire for ODR or ADR are not subject to the Social Security offset."
  - "Chapter 55 of the Laws of 2025 removes the requirement that Tier 3 FIRE members must be eligible for primary social security disability benefit (SSDI) to receive ordinary disability retirement benefit."
  - ODR retirees "must continue to be in receipt of Social Security Disability benefits to maintain their receipt of pension benefits."
- **Implication (inferred).** Article 14 and Tier 3 non-enhanced retirees were *forced* through SSA, so they are already on SSDI and Medicare or were denied. The gap sits in Tier 1/2/4/6 civilians and Article 15 State/local retirees, whose pensions are not offset, and in uniformed ADR.

### G. LA County

**22. LA County left Social Security in 1983.** Label: verified.
- "The County of Los Angeles withdrew its employees from the Social Security system on January 1, 1983." "Plan G members do not pay into Social Security, but they do contribute toward and are eligible for Medicare."
- URL: https://www.lacera.gov/plan/general-g/plan-g-social-security-and-medicare
- **Medicare-only (MQGE) route.** 42 CFR 406.15 covers "Wages paid to State and local government employees hired after March 31, 1986." It grants entitlement if the person "would meet the requirements of § 406.10, § 406.12, or § 406.13" (which include disability) "if Medicare qualified government employment were social security covered employment."
  - URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/406.15
  - So a post-1986 LACERA disability retiree could get disability Medicare without SSDI cash (inferred; SSA process not checked).
- LACERA reimburses Part B only to retirees who are "Currently enrolled in Medicare Part A … and Part B" and "Not being reimbursed … by another agency".
  - URL: https://www.lacera.gov/sites/default/files/assets/documents/rhc/rhc_guidelines.pdf
- Inferred: the LA County market is small and different (Medicare-only claims, no cash benefit to offer the member). It is not sized here.

### H. Existing buyers, vendors and politics

**23. Connecticut Comptroller RFP, September 21, 2023: the product, already bought by a state.** Label: verified.
- It seeks vendors "to represent those receiving disability retirement benefits under the State Employees Retirement System (SERS) in applications for Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) benefits".
- It also asks vendors to "Identify individuals enrolled in the State's under-age 65 retiree health plan who may be eligible for Medicare coverage, as result of disability … and assist them with Medicare enrollment."
- The SSDI help is to be given "without cost to the member".
- Background: the 2017 SEBAC agreement "provides that all current retirees receiving disability retirement benefits under SERS will be required to apply for Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) benefits as a condition of continued eligibility". "There are approximately 1,400 individuals receiving state retirement disability benefits who will required to apply for SSDI." New disability retirees must apply "within two years".
- Scope: it runs through reconsideration and ALJ hearings, and requires that "any individual who receives SSDI benefits also enrolls in Medicare Parts A and B".
- Pricing: "a fee proposal specific to hourly rates" and "No cost submissions that are contingent upon a State action will be accepted."
- URL: https://osc.ct.gov/vendor/rfps/2023/RFP%20-%20Social%20Security%20Disability%20Application%20Assistance.pdf
- An earlier July 2018 RFP had the same scope, and the search summary says "The state of Connecticut is not currently engaged with a vendor" (secondary). https://www.osc.ct.gov/vendor/rfps/2018/SSDIMedicare/SSDI_Medicare_Enrollment_RFP_7_9_181955%20RER.pdf
- The award and the vendor were not found.
- Other precedent: Colorado PERA used SSDC for SSDI/Medicare help in 2020 (secondary; see [`funds_incidence_market.md`](../disability_fund_economics/funds_incidence_market.md)).

**24. The size of the cash pitch to members.** Label: secondary (ssa.gov returned 403).
- The SSA 2026 COLA fact sheet, via search, gives an average disabled-worker benefit of "$1,630" a month after the 2.8% COLA. That is about $19.6k a year, plus up to 12 months of back pay.
- URL: https://www.ssa.gov/cola/factsheets/2026.html

**25. Union politics: the offset is what unions fight; SSDI itself is not the problem.** Label: secondary (search summaries of Empire Center, PBA and LaborPress).
- The 2016–2017 PBA campaign attacked Tier 3 disability as "50 percent of a smaller base salary, which is then offset by half the value of any Social Security disability benefit". Enhanced Tier 3 removed the offset in 2017 for an extra member contribution.
- URLs: https://www.empirecenter.org/publications/senate-mugs-nyc-on-cop-pensions/ ; https://nycpba.org/press-releases/2017/state-passes-disability-benefit
- Inferred: an SSDI push is net-positive for members wherever no offset applies, and unions in CT agreed to make it mandatory. Unions would object if (a) the pension were offset, (b) the employer used SSDI denial or award to police "disability" status, or (c) it looked like cost shifting after the NYC Medicare Advantage fight. See [`../public_employers/README.md`](../public_employers/README.md) item 9.

**Flow (where the durable market is).** New disability retirements counted: NYSLRS 556 (FY2026), NYCERS 326 (CY2024) and NYSTRS 95, so at least 977 a year. TRS, BERS, Police and Fire flows were not extracted.
