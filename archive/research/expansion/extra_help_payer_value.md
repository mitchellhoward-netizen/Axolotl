# Does Extra Help (Part D LIS) save the payer money too?

Research date: 2026-10-07. Labels: **verified** means I read the quoted text at the URL myself. **secondary** means the text came from a search-result summary, a vendor, or a page I could not fetch myself. **inferred** means it is my reasoning from the sources.

## Summary (plain English)

1. **Sometimes. It depends on how the drug plan is set up.** Anyone in any MSP tier is automatically deemed eligible for full Extra Help. Extra Help only works inside a Part D plan, which includes an employer or union EGWP (group Part D plan) and a group MA-PD.
2. **RDS plans get nothing.** CMS says people whose employer claims the Retiree Drug Subsidy "do not receive LIS benefits."
3. **Extra Help pays in two parts. Only the premium part can reach the payer.** The premium subsidy must first lower whatever premium the retiree pays. Whatever is left then goes toward the employer's or union's share.
4. **The copay part never saves the payer anything.** CMS covers only the cost sharing the retiree actually pays, and the employer's extra drug coverage pays first. The retiree pays less. The plan's cost does not change.
5. **How much (inferred):** in New York, a group plan where the payer covers the whole premium gets about **$468 a year** per Extra Help member in 2026 and about **$485 a year** in 2027. In California it is about **$144 a year** in 2026 and **$87** in 2027. In some regions it is $0 to $60.
6. **When the payer gets $0:** the retiree pays a premium larger than the subsidy (for example, NYC's GHI Part D rider costs the retiree $180 a month). Or the plan charges no premium at all, and then CMS takes the subsidy back.
7. **Compared with the Part B saving, it is small.** Part B is $202.90 a month, or $2,434.80 a year, in 2026. In the best New York case, Extra Help adds about 19% on top.
8. **Who actually gets the money depends on the contract.** In a self-funded or direct-contract EGWP, CMS money passes through to the sponsor. In a fully insured group plan, the insurer receives it and is meant to credit or refund the sponsor's share. Some insurers simply build the expected subsidies into the group rate.
9. **It is the retiree who gains most.** Copays drop to $5.10 for generics and $12.65 for brands (2026), the deductible goes away, and any premium they pay toward the drug plan goes down.
10. **Bottom line:** Extra Help is a small, real second saving for payers that **fully fund an EGWP or group MA-PD premium** (1199SEIU's Aetna plan, CalPERS, LACERA's MA plans, and probably NYSHIP). It is **zero** for RDS plans (LACERA's Anthem plans) and for plans where the retiree pays the drug premium (NYC's GHI Part D rider). Pitch it as "up to about $40 a month per member, contract permitting." Do not pitch it as a second Part B.

## Findings

### A. Rules: does LIS apply in an EGWP, and how is it paid?

1. **verified.** MSP enrollees are deemed eligible for the full subsidy. 42 CFR 423.773(c)(1): "An individual must be treated as meeting the eligibility requirements for full subsidy eligible individuals ... if the individual is a— ... (iii) Eligible for Medicaid as a Qualified Medicare Beneficiary (QMB), Specified Low Income Medicare Beneficiary (SLMB), or a Qualifying Individual (QI)". https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-423/subpart-P/section-423.773 (text from the eCFR versioner API for 2026-09-01). Accessed 2026-10-07.

2. **verified.** The premium subsidy is the lesser of the plan's basic premium and the regional amount. 42 CFR 423.780(b)(1): "The premium subsidy amount is equal to the lesser of— (i) ... the portion of the monthly beneficiary premium attributable to basic coverage ... or (ii) The greater of the low-income benchmark premium amount ... or the lowest monthly beneficiary premium for a PDP". https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/part-423/section-423.780. Accessed 2026-10-07.

3. **verified.** The plan must reduce the enrollee's premium and cost sharing. 42 CFR 423.800(b): "the Part D sponsor offering the Part D plan in which a subsidy eligible individual is enrolled must reduce the individual's premiums and cost-sharing as applicable". https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/part-423/section-423.800. Accessed 2026-10-07.

4. **verified.** CMS pays LICS to the plan as a cost-based payment. 42 CFR 423.329(d)(1): "The low-income cost-sharing subsidy payment amount ... is the difference between the cost sharing for a non-low-income subsidy eligible beneficiary under the Part D plan and the statutory cost sharing for a low-income subsidy eligible beneficiary". https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/part-423/section-423.329. Accessed 2026-10-07.

5. **verified.** EGWP waiver authority. 42 CFR 423.458(c)(1): "CMS may waive or modify any requirement under this part that hinders the design of, the offering of, or the enrollment in an employer-sponsored group prescription drug plan, including authorizing the establishment of separate premium amounts". https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/part-423/section-423.458. Accessed 2026-10-07.

6. **verified.** Both kinds of EGWP are covered. CMS Prescription Drug Benefit Manual, Ch. 12 §10.1: "'800 series' plans ... and (2) plans offered by employers/unions that directly contract with CMS (hereinafter referred to as 'Direct Contract' plans). These '800 series' and Direct Contract PDPs are referred to collectively as employer/union-only group waiver plans ('EGWPs')." The CMS Part D manuals page still lists "Chapter 12 - Employer/Union Sponsored Group Health Plans (v11.10.08)" as the current chapter. Chapter PDF: https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/guidance/transmittals/downloads/dwnlds/r6pdbpdfpdf ; listing: https://www.cms.gov/medicare/prescription-drug-coverage/prescriptiondrugcovcontra/partdmanuals. Accessed 2026-10-07.

7. **verified.** LIS applies in EGWPs and is paid the same way as in individual plans. Ch. 12 §20.11 table: "Payment methodology is the same as for plans offered to individual Medicare beneficiaries, except that the rounded base beneficiary premium ... will be used in the low-income premium subsidy regional benchmark comparison." Also: "Low-Income Cost Sharing (LICS) amounts will be paid retrospectively at year-end reconciliation". (same URL). Accessed 2026-10-07.
   - **inferred:** an EGWP's "premium" for the lesser-of test is the base beneficiary premium (BBP), since EGWPs file no bids. This is 2008 guidance, and I found no newer CMS text that restates it (open question 1).

### B. Who gets the premium subsidy and the LICS? (Q2)

8. **verified. Quote of the pass-through rule.** Ch. 12 §20.12.1: "any low-income premium subsidy amount paid on behalf of an LIS beneficiary accrues first to the benefit of the LIS-eligible employer/union group health plan Part D eligible. Specifically, the low-income premium subsidy must first be used to reduce any portion of the monthly beneficiary premium paid by the Part D eligible ... with any remaining portion of the premium subsidy amount then applied toward the portion of any monthly premium paid for by the employer/union." (same URL). Accessed 2026-10-07.

9. **verified.** Any excess goes back to CMS, not to the sponsor. Ch. 12 §20.12.1: "if the sum of the enrollee's monthly premium ... and the employer/union sponsor's monthly premiums (i.e., total monthly premium) is less than the monthly low-income premium subsidy amount, any portion ... above the total monthly premium must be returned directly to CMS." And: "if there is no monthly premium charged to the beneficiary ... or employer/union, the entire low-income premium subsidy amount must be returned directly to CMS and cannot be retained by the PDP sponsor, the employer/union, or the employer/union group Part D eligible". Accessed 2026-10-07.

10. **verified.** Self-funded sponsors use an "illustrative premium." Ch. 12 §20.12.1: "the PDP sponsor must develop an 'illustrative premium.' The 'illustrative premium' is equal to the premium the employer/union group plan sponsor would have paid if they had purchased an equivalent product ... The same rules outlined above would be applied using the illustrative premium in the place of actual premium." Accessed 2026-10-07.

11. **verified.** Refunds to the retiree must be made within 45 days and can go through the employer. Ch. 12: "the PDP sponsor and the employer/union may agree that the employer/union shall directly refund to the Part D eligible ... the amount of the low-income premium subsidy up to the monthly premium contribution previously collected ... within 45 days". Accessed 2026-10-07.

12. **verified. LICS does not lower the sponsor's cost.** Ch. 12 §20.12.2: "Benefits provided to EGWP enrollees cannot vary based on the Part D eligible's LIS eligibility. In addition, for an LIS Part D eligible enrollee in an EGWP, CMS will subsidize only those cost-sharing obligations actually imposed on the Part D eligible under the plan, which includes any supplemental prescription drug coverage offered by the employer/union group health plan sponsor, with the supplemental coverage primary to the LIS program." Accessed 2026-10-07.
    - **inferred:** LICS pays the gap between what the plan would have charged the retiree and the LIS copay. The plan collects less from the retiree and CMS repays that difference, so the plan's net cost does not change. The retiree keeps the whole saving, and the sponsor cannot shrink its wrap coverage for LIS members to shift cost onto LICS.

13. **verified (insurer practice, 2015).** Kaiser Permanente's employer guide: "In cases where the subsidy is greater than the beneficiary's total premium contribution, the beneficiary should be fully refunded for his or her contribution while the remainder of the subsidy may be retained by you, the employer or trust fund." Example 2: "If you — the employer or trust fund — pays the entire $200 premium ... The $32.20 is not required to be refunded to the employee but may be utilized by the employer or trust fund." https://business-dev2.kaiserpermanente.org/business/shared/shared-assets/plans/ever/medicare-low-income-subsidy-program.pdf. Accessed 2026-10-07.

14. **verified (public-employer precedent, MA-PD).** CalPERS Circular Letter 600-024-07 (Apr 25, 2007): "In cases where a LIS-eligible member provides little or no contribution toward their health care premium, CMS expects Kaiser to reimburse the member's employer for the remainder of the LIS amount". https://www.calpers.ca.gov/docs/circular-letters/2007/600-024-07.pdf. Accessed 2026-10-07.

15. **verified (actuarial, insured pricing).** Milliman (C. White, Sept 29, 2021) lists "Part D Low-Income Cost-Sharing Subsidy (LICS)" and "Part D Low-Income Premium Subsidy (LIPS)" among the CMS subsidies. Its pricing formula is "Required Revenue − CMS Subsidies Net of Sequestration = Group Premium" (quoted through a fetch summary). https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/medicare-advantage-egwps-riding-the-baby-boomer-wave. Accessed 2026-10-07.
    - **inferred:** in an insured group plan, the insurer may price expected LIS dollars into the group rate. In that case the sponsor benefits only through future renewals, and only if the rate is experience-rated.

16. **secondary (vendor).** AmWINS on self-funded EGWPs: "Rebates, Discounts and all Part-D subsidy payments are paid on a 100% pass through basis to the plan sponsor"; "LIS (Low Income Subsidy)—Monthly premium assistance for the member"; "LICS—These copay subsidies are an annual payment after all PDE records are reconciled." https://www.amwins.com/docs/default-source/taft-hartley/egwp-vs-rds-sell-sheet-2017-union.pdf. Accessed 2026-10-07.

### C. RDS plans and auto-enrollment (Q3)

17. **verified. LIS does not apply in RDS plans.** Ch. 13 §30 (Rev. 14, 10-01-18): "Individuals who receive prescription drug coverage through plans other than Part D plans, including those for whom employers are claiming a retiree drug subsidy, do not receive LIS benefits." https://www.cms.gov/medicare/prescription-drug-coverage/prescriptiondrugcovcontra/downloads/chapter-13-premium-and-cost-sharing-subsidies-for-low-income-individuals-v09-14-2018.pdf. Accessed 2026-10-07.

18. **verified. Auto-enrollment risk covers only full-benefit duals.** 42 CFR 423.34(d)(3): "(i) Full benefit dual eligible individuals who are qualifying covered retirees ... and for whom CMS has approved the group health plan sponsor to receive the retirement drug subsidy ... also are automatically enrolled in a Part D plan ... unless they elect to decline that enrollment. (ii) ... CMS provides notice ... and advises them to discuss the potential impact of Medicare Part D coverage on their group health plan coverage. The notice informs individuals that they will be deemed to have declined to enroll in Part D unless they affirmatively enroll ... (iii) All other low income subsidy eligible beneficiaries who are qualified covered retirees are not enrolled by CMS into PDPs." https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/part-423/section-423.34. Accessed 2026-10-07.
    - **inferred:** a retiree with MSP only (QMB, SLMB or QI) and no full Medicaid is in group (iii), so CMS will **not** auto-enroll them out of an RDS plan. The risk is mainly that they choose a standalone PDP themselves to get Extra Help. That could cost them their employer drug coverage (and possibly medical coverage, depending on plan rules), and the sponsor would lose RDS for them. Note also that the regulation's own wording on the full-dual case is contradictory: (i) says auto-enroll unless they decline, while (ii) says they are deemed to decline.

19. **verified.** The RDS amount is a separate subsidy that LIS does not affect. 2026 Medicare Trustees Report, Table IV.B9: RDS "Subsidy amount" is $672 in 2025 and $660 in 2026 per RDS enrollee; LIS "Subsidy amount" is $1,226 in 2025 and $1,254 in 2026 per LIS enrollee. The 2026 Trustees Report PDF was saved in the session scratchpad; it is published on cms.gov under "Trustees Reports". Accessed 2026-10-07.

## Arithmetic

Rule (findings 2, 7, 8 and 9): the subsidy is min(the EGWP's basic premium, the regional amount). The sponsor's share is that subsidy minus the retiree's premium contribution, with a floor of $0. If the plan charges no premium (and there is no illustrative premium), CMS takes the subsidy back.

| Case (inferred) | Monthly LIS premium subsidy | Sponsor gets per year if it pays 100% of the premium |
|---|---|---|
| NY 2026, group MA-PD (BBP $38.99 < NY $58.82) | $38.99 | **$467.88** |
| NY 2026, PDP EGWP in the demo (BBP − $10 = $28.99) | $28.99 | $347.88 |
| NY 2027 (min($41.33, $40.45)) | $40.45 | **$485.40** |
| CA 2026 / 2027 | $12.00 / $7.28 | $144.00 / $87.36 |
| TX or FL 2026 | $4.82 | $57.84 |
| Retiree pays ≥ the subsidy (NYC GHI rider, $180/mo) | n/a | **$0** (all of it goes to the retiree) |
| RDS plan (LACERA Anthem) | n/a | **$0** |

- **Low / middle / high per MSP enrollee in a sponsor's EGWP (inferred):** low **$0** (retiree pays the premium, RDS plan, or a $0-premium plan). Middle **about $350 to $470 a year** (NY payer that pays the premium in full, 2026). High **about $485 a year** (NY 2027; a Northeast region with a high benchmark). LICS adds **$0** for the sponsor in every case (finding 12).
- **Compared with Part B:** $202.90 × 12 = $2,434.80 (repo `tools/site/msp-ny.json`). Best NY case: $467.88 / $2,434.80 = **about 19%** on top of the Part B saving.
- **What the retiree gets (inferred):** the Trustees' average LIS cost per enrollee is $1,254 in 2026, and most of it is cost sharing (89% in 2022, finding 23). In a 25%-coinsurance plan like NYC's GHI rider, the retiree can save up to about $2,100 a year in drug costs plus about $348 to $468 a year in premium.

## Open questions

1. Does CMS still use the EGWP basic premium = BBP (rounded) for the lesser-of test? The source is 2008 Ch. 12. Is the 2026 demo's $10 BBP reduction applied to EGWP LIS premium subsidies? Ask a CMS account manager or an EGWP actuary.
2. Does an LIS flag raise the EGWP's direct subsidy? The direct subsidy is NAMBA × the member's risk score − BBP (Ch. 12 table), and the Part D RxHCC model has separate low-income segments (MedPAC Payment Basics 2025). If LIS members carry higher risk scores, that could be a **third** payer saving, possibly larger than the premium subsidy after the IRA raised the direct subsidy (2026 NAMBA $239.27). This is not quantified. Ask an actuary (Milliman, Segal).
3. Contract terms. Do Aetna (1199), SilverScript (NYSHIP; CalPERS 2026), Blue Shield (CalPERS 2027), Kaiser, SCAN and UHC (LACERA) credit the leftover LIS premium to the sponsor, or price it into the group rate?
4. NYSHIP: the retiree's monthly contribution to the Empire Plan Medicare Rx portion, which decides how much is left for the State after the refund. Read memo NY18-31 directly (cs.ny.gov reset or redirected to login).
5. NYC union welfare funds: is each fund's retiree drug plan an EGWP or RDS, and does the retiree pay a premium?
6. Do the monthly LIS/dual special enrollment periods let an MSP retiree leave an EGWP for a standalone PDP, and does that end their employer coverage? This is the main member-side risk to warn about.
