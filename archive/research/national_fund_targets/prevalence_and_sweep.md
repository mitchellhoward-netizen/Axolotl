# Who reimburses Medicare Part B: how common it is, plus a national sweep

Researched 2026-10-07 for Mycelium. Labels used: **verified** means the quote was read on the sponsor's own page or document (or an official audit). **secondary** means it comes from a search-engine summary, a third party, or a page we could not open ourselves (blocked or 403). **inferred** means it is our own reasoning or arithmetic.

**Scope note.** The shared web-search budget ran out partway through task 3 (the multiemployer sweep), so that sweep is much thinner than the 30+ target. After that point we could only open URLs we already had. We also reused verified findings from `archive/research/ny_fund_targets/` (cited by file) and did not search them again.

## Summary

1. **No published survey counts the plans that reimburse Part B.** We checked IFEBP, Horizon/IFEBP benchmarking, Segal, Milliman, KFF, Pew, GAO and SLGE. None of them publishes a share of plans that reimburse the Part B premium (secondary, see the Surveys section). Every count below is built bottom-up.
2. **Multiemployer (Taft-Hartley) funds: reimbursing Part B is rare.** We verified only about 5 funds that pay toward Part B from fund money as a fixed premium reimbursement: 1199SEIU NBF (50%), MILA (two programs), IUOE Local 94 School Division, and IATSE National ($240 a quarter). A few more have account-based HRAs that can be spent on Part B (HPAE Retiree Medical Trust, JIB/IBEW Local 3, SAG-AFTRA). In those, MSP saves the fund nothing directly (inferred).
3. **The usual multiemployer pattern is that the retiree pays Part B.** The fund then requires Part B enrollment and pays secondary, Medigap-style coverage, or a group Medicare Advantage/EGWP premium. Examples are Plumbers Local 1, Hotel Trades Council, Central States/TeamCare, WGA and DGA (verified, below).
4. **Best estimate for multiemployer funds nationally: about 10 to 40 reimburse a fixed share of Part B,** out of roughly 1,600 multiemployer health plans in our Form 5500 list (inferred, low confidence). The verified cases are concentrated in New York.
5. **Public employers are where the Part B money is.** NY's budget office (FY2020, secondary) says that besides New York, only 5 states reimburse the standard Part B premium: CA, CT, HI, NV and NJ. Ohio phased its reimbursement out (OPERS reached $0 in 2017). We verified CT, HI, NJ, CalPERS and NV PEBP on the sponsors' own pages.
6. **New York is the epicenter.** Civil Service Law §167-a requires **every NYSHIP employer** to reimburse Part B for Medicare-primary enrollees: the State plus about 800 to 900 local governments and school districts (secondary). NYC separately reimburses 100% plus IRMAA under Admin Code §12-126, covering 130,215 retirees and dependents at $190M in CY2009 (verified).
7. **Large non-NY local payers we verified:**
   - LA County: about 39,960 participants, about $100M a year.
   - LACERS and LAFPP (City of LA).
   - Boston: 50% of the premium.
   - Nassau County: 10,240 retirees plus 3,690 dependents covered.
   - Sonoma County: frozen at $96.40, closed group.
   - A scattering of NY and NJ school districts and municipalities.
8. **Best estimate for public sponsors: about 6 states plus about 1,000 local public employers reimburse Part B,** nearly all of them NY NYSHIP participants. This covers **several hundred thousand to about 1 million Medicare retirees and spouses**, and NY State plus NYC are probably more than half of it. This is inferred with medium-low confidence: we found no national retiree count.
9. **MSP saves these sponsors money only where reimbursement stops once someone else pays the premium.** Nassau and LA County say this in writing ("not being paid by another entity", "cannot be reimbursed by another agency"). NYC's, NYSHIP's and the funds' claim rules require proof that the retiree paid the premium (verified).
10. **The trend is toward cutting the benefit:** OPERS eliminated it, CalPERS closed it to new state hires from 2016 to 2020, Hawaii dropped IRMAA and spouses for hires after 7/2023, NJ capped it at $46.10 for post-1997 groups, and City Colleges of Chicago ended it. Funds keep moving retirees into MA/EGWP. The installed base is large but closed and shrinking (verified examples below).

## 5. Open questions

1. **NY State:** how many state retirees and dependents get Part B reimbursement, and what is the annual dollar total? Likely sources are the DOB General State Charges pages, the Comptroller, and the NYSHIP annual report.
2. **NYSHIP participating employers:** what is the current count, and how many Medicare-primary retirees do they have? About 800 to 900 employers are each required by statute to reimburse.
3. **Current NYC totals:** we only have CY2009 figures (130k and $190M). Check the NYC Comptroller or OLR, or the GASB 75 OPEB note in the NYC ACFR.
4. **CalPERS, CT, HI, NJ and NV:** retiree counts and dollars per year. Check each plan's ACFR or OPEB valuation; Segal/GRS valuations usually show the Part B reimbursement line.
5. **Does each sponsor actually stop paying when Medicaid/MSP pays?** Confirmed in writing for Nassau and LA County. For NYC OLR, NYSHIP and the funds, inferred from their "proof of payment" rules. Overpayment risk (Nassau found 94 double-dippers) suggests the cross-checks are weak.
6. **Multiemployer sweep not finished:** ILWU-PMA, UMWA Funds, NYSA-ILA, UAW Retiree Medical Benefits Trust, Bakery & Confectionery, Hawaii and California building trades, SEIU 32BJ/775, and the Form 5500 Schedule H lines that mention "Medicare Part B reimbursement".
7. **Whether IFEBP's members-only surveys ask about Part B reimbursement.** If they do, that would give the only national prevalence number. Ask IFEBP research staff.
8. **Other public-sector checks still needed:** Massachusetts towns, Texas (ERS/TRS), Michigan, Pennsylvania counties, transit (MTA, NJ Transit, SEPTA, WMATA) and large school districts outside NY.
