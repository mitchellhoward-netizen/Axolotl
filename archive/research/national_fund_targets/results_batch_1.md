## Summary

- **Funds that reimburse Part B (full, partial or flat): 1 confirmed of 30.** That one is the **MEBA Medical and Benefits Plan** (MD). It pays back Part B premiums for Medicare-eligible pensioners (and their Medicare-eligible dependents) **only when the gross monthly pension, as a straight-life annuity, is under $1,000**. The SPD is dated December 2025. This is the best Mycelium lead in the batch: the only retirees who get the reimbursement are low-pension retirees, who are also the ones most likely to qualify for MSP, and once the state pays their Part B premium, MEBA stops paying it.
- **HRA or credit arrangements that may cover Part B (weaker): 3–4.**
  - **SAG-AFTRA Health Plan:** the Senior Performers HRA credits $1,140 a year to each Senior Performer and spouse with 20+ Retiree Health Credits, or $240 each with fewer. The fund's FAQ, as summarized by search, lists "Medicare premiums" as a use. The 2021 HRA SPD's examples list only individual-policy premiums bought through Via Benefits.
  - **USW HRA Fund (TN):** a retiree HRA that pays eligible §213 expenses. Its rules on Part B were not found.
  - **NCR Corporation (GA):** a single-employer plan, not Taft-Hartley. Post-65 retirees get a fixed annual subsidy through a retiree reimbursement account (SEC filing).
  - **Pipe Trades Services MN:** a separate Retiree Health Fund (EIN 16-1657260, not the EIN on our list) gives a "credit toward the cost of retiree health coverage." Whether that credit can pay Part B is unknown.
- **Funds that say outright the retiree pays Part B: 4.**
  - **Central States retiree plan:** "you will still owe the Part B premium … ($202.90 per individual per month for 2026)."
  - **WGA Industry Health Fund:** it only waives the $600 a year dependent premium "to help offset" the cost of Part B.
  - **1199SEIU Home Care fund:** "does not provide Retiree Health Benefits." It pays 50% of Part B only for active members with ESRD.
  - **Teamsters 856:** requires Part A and Part B and offers no reimbursement.
- **Post-65 retiree coverage confirmed: 13 of 30.** 9 verified, 4 secondary:
  - UMWA 1993 Plan
  - Central States retiree plan (TeamCareAdvantage)
  - SAG-AFTRA
  - WGA
  - MEBA
  - Michigan Laborers (self-pay Medicare Advantage)
  - Teamsters 856
  - Boilermakers National (UHC Medicare Advantage)
  - Western Teamsters (Anthem MA, secondary)
  - Steamfitters 420 (Anthem MA, secondary)
  - Southern California Drug Benefit Fund (Anthem MA, secondary)
  - Teamsters Medicare Trust (secondary)
  - NCR (retiree reimbursement account, secondary)
- **Pre-65 coverage only:** the two Railroad plans. ERMA covers ages 60–65. After 65 the only option is an optional Plan F that the retiree pays for.
- **No retiree health:** 1199SEIU Home Care.
- **Existing MSP, Extra Help or Medicaid help:** none found in any fund document in this batch.
- **Look-alikes to avoid:**
  - 1199SEIU **National Benefit Fund (Health & Human Service)**, which pays 50% of Part B to retirees, is **not** the Home Care fund on our list.
  - **Teamsters Retiree Trust** (Stockton CA) is not Western Teamsters or the Teamsters Medicare Trust.
  - **carpentersfund.org** is North Atlantic States and **carpenterfunds.com** is Northern California; neither is the NYC District Council.
  - The ProPublica EIN for "Teamsters Medicare Trust" (43-6059037) does **not** match our EIN 43-6196082.

## Findings

1. **Railroad Employees National Health and Welfare Plan (VA, 80-0616625).** UnitedHealthcare's retirement kit, hosted by SMART union, describes the options at retirement. For retirees aged 60 to 65: "If you retire between the ages of 60 and 65 and are not eligible for Medicare, you may be covered under GA-46000." At 65: "If you are eligible for Medicare due to age or disability, you are eligible for the Optional Supplemental Plan for Medicare – GA-23111 Plan F," and "Plan F is optional and supplements the coverage provided by Medicare." Union rate sheets (2008/2010, secondary) show Plan F as a premium the retiree pays. No Part B payment was found. The plan is administered by UnitedHealthcare. retiree_health=to65, part_b=none. Label: secondary. Source: https://static.smart-union.org/worksite/PDFs/healthnews/UHC-retirement_kit.pdf (undated).

2. **UMWA 1993 Benefit Plan (DC, 52-1888497).** The UMWA Health & Retirement Funds' page says "you must enroll in Part B in order to keep your Funds' health services card" and "The Funds is the primary payer for Medicare Part B services if you have no other group health plan that is primary." The page does not mention paying or reimbursing the Part B premium. The plan "contracts with the UMWA 1974 Pension Trust for administrative services." The page names Michael W. Buckner (UMWA) as Chairman. retiree_health=post65 (the plan covers retired miners and requires Part B), part_b=unknown (none mentioned). Label: verified for the coverage terms. Sources: https://www.umwafunds.org/medical-plans-benefits/medicare-part-b-requirements/ and https://www.umwafunds.org/umwa-1993-benefit-plan/.

3. **Central States, SE & SW Areas H&W Plan, Retiree Plan (IL, 36-2154936).** The 2026 TeamCareAdvantage (Humana Group MA PPO) flyer sets these requirements: "retired from the Central States Pension Fund after July 31, 2008 … At least age 63 … Eligible for retiree Health Plan R4." Retirees get coverage "at no cost": 2, 4 or 6 years of coverage from age 65 for retirement at 63, 64 or 65. On Part B it says "you will still owe the Part B premium. These payments ($202.90 per individual per month for 2026) are automatically deducted from your Social Security benefits." The program is not available to UPS members. The fund office is at P.O. Box 5109, Des Plaines, IL. retiree_health=post65, part_b=none. Label: verified. Source: https://myteamcare.org/-/media/TeamCare/Files/Other-Documents/TeamCare-Summit/humana-teamcare-advantage-2026.pdf. Also https://myteamcare.org/benefits/retiree: "The Part B premium is deducted from your Social Security check."
