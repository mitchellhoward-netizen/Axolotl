Researched 2026-10-07. Sources:

  ([health_plans_national.csv](health_plans_national.csv)).
- **Checks of the 150 largest plans** by non-active participants:
  [results_batch_1-5.md](results_batch_1.md) and

- **Surveys, public employers and a wider sweep:**

- **New York in depth:** [../ny_fund_targets/](../ny_fund_targets/README.md).

Every claim in those notes has a quote, a URL and a label (verified, secondary or inferred).

## The answer

**Hundreds of employers pay Part B, but they're mostly public employers. Among union funds it's a
short list.**

1. **Union (multiemployer) funds: about 5–6% of the largest ones.** We got a clear answer for about
   86 of the 150 largest plans. Seven union funds are confirmed to pay toward Part B from fund money:

   | Fund | Where | What it pays | Source |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 1199SEIU National Benefit Fund | NY | 50% of standard Part B, retiree + spouse (Wage Class I/II, ~10 yrs) | live retiree page, SPD, claim form |
   | ILWU-PMA Welfare Plan (West Coast longshore) | CA/OR/WA | **full Part B** for retirees, survivors, dependents | fund's Medicare page |
   | MILA (East/Gulf longshore) | NY | full Part B + IRMAA for 24 months after retiring (since 10/2023); standard Part B for pensioners in Medicare Advantage | fund SPD |
   | MEBA Medical & Benefits (marine engineers) | MD | Part B **only when the pension is under $1,000/month** (15+ yrs) | fund SPD, Dec 2025 |
   | Seafarers Health & Benefits (SIU) | MD | $50/month + $50 for a Medicare spouse | 2021 pensioner booklet |
   | IUOE Local 94 School Division | NY | Medicare premiums up to $7,000/yr per couple | 2026 claim form |
   | IATSE National H&W | NY / national | $240/quarter toward Part B | fund pages |

   - **About ten more let retirees spend their own HRA balance on Part B.** These include Bakery
     (pre-2017 retirees), IBEW Local 3 JIB, HPAE, Pipe Fitters 597, Eastern Atlantic Carpenters, and
     several Operating Engineers and plumbers funds. There, MSP mostly frees up the member's balance
     rather than saving the fund money.
   - **Most funds make retirees pay Part B.** Examples are Central States, St. Louis–KC Carpenters,
     Carpenters for California, Southern California UFCW, NYC Carpenters and Laborers 472/172.
   - **Others end coverage at 65.** Examples are 32BJ, the railroad plans and Heartland.
   - **About 64 of the 150 remain unknown.** Their plan rules are behind logins or not online, and
     phone numbers are in the notes.
   - **Best national estimate: roughly 30–80 multiemployer funds pay toward Part B from fund money
     (low confidence).** That comes from about 6% of large plans, assuming smaller plans offer it
     less often. No survey counts this.

2. **Public employers: hundreds.**
   - **New York's Civil Service Law §167-a** requires reimbursement of the Part B premium for
     retirees and dependents in the State's health plan (statute read). That covers New York State
     and roughly 800–900 participating local governments and school districts (state flyer,
     undated).
   - **New York City** reimburses 100% plus IRMAA (Admin Code §12-126).
   - **Five other states** reimburse on their own pages: California, Connecticut, Hawaii, Nevada,
     New Jersey.
   - **Large local employers** include Los Angeles County (~40,000 participants, ~$100M a year), the
     City of LA pension systems, Nassau County and Boston (50%).
   - **The trend is down.** Ohio's public pension system phased it out to $0 in 2017, CalPERS closed
     it to new state hires, Hawaii and New Jersey cut it for newer hires, and City Colleges of
     Chicago ended it. The people still covered are many, but the group is shrinking.

## What this means for the wedge

- **Union funds: a named, winnable short list, with a cluster on the waterfront.**
  - 1199 NBF is the largest.
  - ILWU-PMA pays the full premium in California, where the archive already has MSP rules. Since
    2026, California's MSP asset limit is $130,000 for one person.
  - MILA, MEBA and Seafarers are all maritime, and their unions know each other. MEBA's rule pays
    Part B only for pensions under $1,000, which is exactly the MSP-eligible group, so every MSP
    enrollment there saves the fund money.
- **Public employers are the large market for Part B savings.** It's a different sale: a city
  benefits office, a school district or a state plan. It's slower, but it's the same product.
  - **Open:** under §167-a, does each participating school district or town carry its retirees'
    Part B cost itself, or is it pooled in the State plan's premiums? That decides who saves and who
    signs.
- **Everywhere, the saving exists only if reimbursement stops once the state pays the premium.**
  Nassau and LA County say so in writing. Elsewhere it's inferred from proof-of-payment rules.
