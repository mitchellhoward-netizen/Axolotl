## The answer

**Yes, it's real, and it's bigger than the union-fund market. But the sale is different, and it
carries political and legal risk that the pitch has to be built around.**

### Why it's real (verified in primary sources)

1. **The rules already stop reimbursement when someone else pays the premium.**
   - **NYC (OLR form):** pays only a retiree "not receiving Medicare Part B reimbursement(s) from
     any other source including Medicaid." We checked this in the PDF.
   - **LA County (LACERA):** pays only those "Paying for their Medicare Part B premium themselves"
     and "Not being reimbursed ... by another agency, such as another employer or state." We checked
     this on the page.
   - **The others:** NYSHIP (2019 booklet), CalPERS, Hawaii EUTF and Nassau County have similar
     rules.
2. **The money is large.**

   | Payer | Part B reimbursement |
   |---|---|
   | NYC | ~$441M in FY2021 incl. IRMAA; ~190k retirees + 63k spouses on Medicare |
   | New York State Health Insurance Program (NYSHIP; State + ~800 local employers) | ~$249M in 2019 (secondary source) |
   | Hawaii EUTF | $119.6M projected for FY2026 |
   | Connecticut | ~$110–150M (our arithmetic) |
   | LA County | ~$100M for ~40k participants |
   | Boston | ~$15M |

3. **For local governments the saving is net.** County and NYC Medicaid shares have been frozen
   since 2015, so a new MSP enrollee costs a town, school district, county or NYC nothing. A State
   employer nets about half, because it pays 50% of QMB/SLMB through Medicaid; QI is fully federal.
   **Local employers are the cleanest buyers.**
4. **Nobody checks today.** Social Security does not share premium data with NYSHIP. NYC matches
   only against pension payroll and death records; its only audit (2011) never tested for Medicaid
   overlap. We found no vendor screening public retirees for MSP. Nassau County found 94 duplicate
   Part B payments ($1.6M suspected) with its own staff.
5. **They do buy outside help to clean up benefit spending.** NYC bought dependent eligibility
   audits (Aon in 2013, and a 2025–28 negotiated acquisition). NYSHIP did the same (Budco 2009, HMS
   2015). Illinois paid HMS only out of savings. Contingency-fee professional services are bought by
   NY towns with one board vote; Varick did one in June 2026 at 30%.

### What makes it hard

6. **The retiree gains almost nothing on the premium.** The employer already pays it back, so after
   MSP the State pays instead of the employer. What the retiree gains is the premium paid up front
   instead of up to 16 months late, automatic full Extra Help on drugs (its value depends on the
   drug plan, which is open for the Empire Plan and NYC plans), and MSP is exempt from estate
   recovery. **Getting retirees to enroll depends on those extras.** The 15% outreach rate from the
   union-fund work may not hold here.
7. **Double payments are money, but clawing them back from poor retirees is a trap.** Retirees who
   are already on MSP are probably still getting the employer check. Stopping it takes about $2,435
   a year from a low-income retiree, and collecting back years is worse. Illinois retirees called the
   savings-based audit an attempt "to cut off some senior citizens". **Our advice: stop the payments
   going forward with notice, never claw back, and never be paid per retiree dropped.**
8. **The legal footing is policy, not statute.** §12-126 (NYC) and §167-a (NYSHIP) say the employer
   reimburses "an amount equal to" the premium. The Medicaid exclusion comes from agency rules, and
   a union could challenge it. The 1994 school-district retiree moratorium was used in 2025 against
   a BOCES that cut Part B. Whether stopping payment for an MSP enrollee counts as a "cut" needs
   counsel.
9. **The politics are raw.** After the NYC Medicare Advantage fight (2021–2025), anything that
   sounds like "saving money on retirees" reads as a cut. Governors proposed trimming Part B or IRMAA
   reimbursement in FY2016–FY2027, and unions and retiree groups beat back each attempt.

## How many retirees, and what it's worth (screening estimates)

- **Likely eligible (ceilings):** NYSLRS-ERS about 39k NY residents, of them about 17k school-district
  support staff. NYC's three systems about 28k, with BERS (school aides, cafeteria, bus) the highest
  rate at 27%. Police and fire are about zero.
- **Typical public mix, per 1,000 reimbursed retirees:** about 130 are under the line. About 70 are
  probably already on MSP (55% take-up), a double-payment question. About 60 are eligible but not
  enrolled, worth up to about $141k a year to the employer.
- **NYC alone:** roughly 14k eligible and not enrolled. That's up to about $33M a year if all
  enrolled, or about $5M a year at 15% outreach.
- **NY local employers, NYC, LA County, Connecticut and Hawaii together:** savings going forward
  plausibly in the low tens of millions a year at middle assumptions. At a 20% fee that is about
  $3–6M a year in revenue. **This is a $5M-scale business if retirees enroll at union-fund rates.**
  That is the number a pilot has to prove.

## How to sell it

- **First buyers:** NY school districts and towns that reimburse Part B. They pay it directly, the
  saving is net, and one board vote can approve a professional-services contract. Then NYC, through
  the procurement rules' §3-11 demonstration-project route (up to 3 years, can start from our
  proposal), or an M/WBE partner. LA County is the best non-NY public target: a written rule,
  re-checked every year, and California's lenient MSP asset limit.
- **The offer:** "We help your retirees get Extra Help on drugs and the State to pay their Part B
  directly, and we keep your Part B records correct." Lead with what the retiree gains, keep it
  voluntary, and sign a HIPAA business associate agreement.
- **The fee:** a share of savings the employer has actually received, with a cap.
- **Before the first contract:** a legal opinion on the moratorium and on statute versus policy,
  and confirmation of how NYSHIP bills Part B to local employers. The State's cost is pooled, but the
  local agency manual says locals reimburse "in addition to" premiums.
