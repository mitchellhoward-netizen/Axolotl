# Would public employers buy Mycelium, how, and how fast?

Researched 2026-10-07. Labels: **verified** means I read it in the primary source at the URL.
**secondary** means a reputable non-primary source, or a primary source seen only through a
search-engine excerpt. **inferred** means our own reasoning from verified text. All quotes are
exact; "[...]" marks an omission. nycourts.gov, gartner.com, ssa.gov and web.archive.org blocked
the fetcher, so some court holdings below are secondary.

## Summary

1. **Would they buy? Probably, but nobody has bought this exact thing yet.** NY public employers
   already pay outside vendors to strip ineligible people off retiree and employee health benefits.
   NYC did it in 2013 (Aon Hewitt) and opened a 2025–28 procurement. NYS did it in 2009 (Budco) and
   2015 (HMS). Illinois paid HMS only "if the state saves money". Nassau County's 2026 audit of
   Part B reimbursement found $1.5M paid out twice. I found **no precedent** of an employer paying a
   vendor to enroll retirees in MSP so that its Part B reimbursement stops. Mycelium would be a new
   category and would need a pilot.
2. **The rule that makes the savings real already exists.** Nassau says reimbursement is
   "contingent on [...] the retiree/dependent's Medicare premium [...] not being paid by another
   entity". NYC reimburses "premiums paid". Neither employer has to change its policy. It only has
   to learn which retirees the State now pays for.
3. **Fastest legal path for a school district or town.** Mycelium can be bought as a *professional
   service*, the "well-established exception" to bidding under GML §103. The buyer follows its own
   GML §104-b policy, usually an RFP or a few written quotes, and the board votes on a resolution.
   One board meeting is enough. The town of Varick did exactly this in June 2026 with a contingency
   utility-bill audit. Do not count on piggybacking: GML §103(16) covers goods and related repair
   services, not professional services.
4. **Fastest path in NYC.** Use a PPB §3-11 *demonstration project* (≤3 years, City Record notice
   ≥10 days before negotiating). Alternatives are a micropurchase (≤$20K) or an M/WBE small
   purchase (≤$1.5M, no competition), which is open only if Mycelium is certified or partners with
   a certified firm. A full RFP had a **median of 376 days** in FY24. The City's own dependent audit
   ran on a "limited number of vendors" negotiated acquisition.
5. **Fee structure that fits.** A contingency fee on *realized* savings: a percentage of the
   reimbursement actually stopped or recovered, paid only after the employer has the money in hand
   (Varick's town attorney insisted on that clause), with a cap and a set term. NY municipalities
   sign 30% contingency deals for bill audits. I found no OSC opinion that bans contingency fees,
   and none that blesses them.
6. **Who decides.** In a district, the business official recommends and the Board of Education
   votes. In a town, the supervisor or comptroller recommends and the town board votes. In NYC, OLR
   (Health Benefits Program) is the contracting agency and the Comptroller registers the contract.
   Retiree-health changes in NYC run through the MLC (the City's coalition of municipal unions), so
   the unions need to agree to the outreach even where they have no legal veto.
7. **Net fiscal effect.** Counties and NYC pay a Medicaid local share frozen at 2015 levels, so a
   new MSP enrollee costs them roughly $0. The **full reimbursement saved is net savings** for a
   county, town, district or NYC (inferred). For the State as employer, QMB/SLMB premiums cost 50%
   State money (FMAP 0.50), so the State nets about 50%. QI premiums are 100% federal up to the
   State's allotment, so the State nets about 100%.
8. **Politics.** The cost shifts to the State and federal Medicaid budgets, which invites a
   "cost-shifting" or "pushing seniors onto Medicaid" attack. Since the NYC Medicare Advantage
   fight, retirees distrust any change to retiree health. Retirees in Illinois called a
   contingency-paid audit a scheme "to cut off some senior citizens". For school-district retirees,
   the 1994 moratorium has been used to strike down cuts to Part B reimbursement.
9. **What the pitch must avoid.** Avoid anything that sounds like cutting a benefit, forcing
   enrollment, or "Medicaid". Lead with what the retiree gains: QMB pays Medicare cost-sharing; any
   MSP tier brings automatic full Extra Help; and MSP is exempt from estate recovery. Promise in
   writing a voluntary, consent-only process and a HIPAA business associate agreement (BAA). Never
   be paid per *denial*.
10. **Biggest open risks.** (a) Is ending reimbursement for an MSP enrollee a "diminution" under
    the school-district moratorium? (b) Does Part B reimbursement count as income for MSP
    eligibility? (c) Federal headwinds: OBBBA §71101 freezes the 2023 MSP streamlining rule until
    2034. (d) Big employers can run the same data match in-house, as Nassau did.

## Findings

### 1. Precedents: public employers paying vendors to clean up health-benefit spending

**1a. NYC, 2025: dependent eligibility audit bought by negotiated acquisition (verified).**
OLR's public notice: "OLR has received approval from the New York City Mayor's Office of Contract
Services (MOCS) to use a competitive Negotiated Acquisition pursuant to Section 3-04(b)(2)(ii) of
the New York City Procurement Policy Board Rules, as there are a limited number of vendors available
and able to perform the work." Invitees: "Deloitte Consulting [...] Aon [...] Gainwell Technologies
[...] Alight". Term: "July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2028". Responses were due "April 18, 2025". The
procurement "is subject to participation goals for MBEs and/or WBEs as required by Section 6-129 of
the New York City Administrative Code."
URL: https://www.nyc.gov/assets/olr/downloads/pdf/rfp/na-deva-public-notice.pdf (EPIN 00225N0002;
City Record listing https://a856-cityrecord.nyc.gov/RequestDetail/20250303025). The notice gives no
contract value or fee basis.
*Inferred:* solicitation in March 2025 and a planned July 2025 start means about 4 months from
notice to target start for an eligibility vendor, before registration.

**1b. NYC, 2013: dependent audit by Aon Hewitt, union lawsuit (secondary).** The PSC-CUNY Clarion
reports that the City "hired a for-profit consulting company, Aon Hewitt, to conduct the audit"
and that procedures were "challenged in court by a coalition of NYC worker unions". The unions won
"a written, comprehensive appeal process; protection from financial penalties [...] and
satisfactory safeguards regarding security and confidentiality."
URLs: https://psc-cuny.org/clarion/august-2013/audit-dependent-coverage ;
https://psc-cuny.org/clarion/september-2013/take-heed-october-4-deadline-citys-dependent-health-care-coverage-audit
*Lesson (inferred):* NYC unions will litigate a vendor-run benefits audit unless appeal and privacy
protections are agreed with them in advance.

**1c. NYS (NYSHIP), 2009 and 2015: dependent eligibility verification by outside vendors.**
- 2015, verified: "The Department of Civil Service has contracted with Health Management Systems,
  Inc. (HMS) to administer the Dependent Eligibility Verification Project." Also: "a Special Amnesty
  Period has been established through legislation."
  URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/hba/shared/publications/letters/2015/dependent-eligibility-verification-2015.pdf
- 2009, secondary (search excerpts of Civil Service memos): Civil Service contracted with BUDCO.
  The project "resulted in the removal of a significant number of ineligible dependents [...] which
  reduced premium costs for many participating employers."
  URLs: https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/hba/memos/2010/pa10-21.pdf ;
  https://www.nysut.org/news/2009/december/nyship-dependent-eligibility-audit-enters-appeals-phase
- 2008 pricing, secondary (search excerpt of the 2008 RFP's Exhibit IV, now 404): bidders quoted
  "an all-inclusive flat fee for each task based on the total number of family contracts", and fee
  changes required an amendment "approved by the Office of the State Comptroller."
  URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/2008DEASRFP/pdf/Exhibit_IV.pdf (dead link).
  *Inferred:* the State has paid per contract (per enrolled family), not a share of savings.

**1d. Nassau County, 2010: dependent audit savings (verified).** "The total estimated savings to
the County for 2010 will be $7.3 million", from "approximately 1,090 dependents enrolled on policies
that the County was paying for" that were not verified.
URL: https://archive.nassaucountyny.gov/agencies/Comptroller/7Msaved.html

**1e. Nassau County, June 2026: audit of Part B reimbursement (verified). The closest analog.**
"The Office of Nassau County Comptroller Elaine Phillips has identified 94 retirees whose
spouses/dependents were receiving Medicare Part B reimbursement from the County and also from their
former employer. The overpayments totaled $1,507,096.97." Also: "the County has recovered
$258,172.30". The rule: "Reimbursement of Medicare Part B premiums is contingent on the retiree
and/or dependent not receiving reimbursement from another source or that the retiree/dependent's
Medicare premium is not being paid by another entity on their behalf." Scale: "approximately 10,240
retirees and 3,690 retirees' dependents as a condition of participation in the New York State Health
Insurance Program (NYSHIP)"; premiums "currently run $202.90 per month per person".
URL: https://www.nassaucountyny.gov/DocumentCenter/View/53265/Medicare-Reimbursement-Audit-PR
*Inferred:* (i) The "paid by another entity" rule is the legal hook for Mycelium: once MSP pays the
premium, the employer's reimbursement stops under existing policy. (ii) The audit was done
**in-house** by the Comptroller's staff, so a large county may prefer a data match it runs itself
over a vendor. (iii) Comptrollers publicize this kind of finding as fighting waste, which is a
politically safe frame for buyers.

**1f. NYC Comptroller, 2011: audit of OLR's Part B reimbursement program (verified, page summary).**
Audit 7I11-085 found "internal control deficiencies in ascertaining eligibility" that led to
improper payments. CY2009 reimbursements went to 130,215 retirees and dependents.
URL: https://comptroller.nyc.gov/reports/audit-report-on-the-compliance-of-the-office-of-labor-relations-with-the-medicare-part-b-reimbursement-program
NYC's current rule (verified): "The City reimburses retirees and their eligible dependents for
Medicare Part B premiums paid, excluding any penalties." Most retirees get the refund
automatically, based on the standard Part B premium.
URL: https://www.nyc.gov/site/olr/health/retiree/health-retiree-responsibilities-assistance.page
*Inferred:* if OLR reimburses automatically at the standard premium, retirees already on MSP may
still be getting reimbursed. That would make part of Mycelium's value an overpayment recovery (like
Nassau's), not only future savings. To be confirmed with OLR.

**1g. Illinois, 2013: dependent audit paid only on savings (secondary, news).** "The state hired
Health Management Systems, Inc., of Texas to conduct the audit for $393,136, but with the caveat
that HMS doesn't get paid unless the state saves money." Retiree reaction: "is it an attempt to cut
off some senior citizens"; "You're talking about 80-year-olds getting a letter demanding personal
documents".
URL: https://www.illinoistimes.com/news/retirees-rankled-over-health-benefits-audit-11452248/
(Nov 7, 2013).

**1h. NY municipalities sign contingency deals for audits (verified).** Town of Varick minutes,
June 2, 2026, on a forensic utility-bill audit: "Their fee is 30% of the amount of the past
overcharges credited and collected by the client"; "If there are no refunds or credits, there are no
fees collected by EGS." The town attorney added that payment comes "after the town has received the
town's refund." The motion "carried unanimously directing Supervisor Robert Hayssen sign the
Forensic Bill Auditing Agreement."
URL: https://varickny.gov/?p=2579
Similar, secondary (search excerpts, paraphrased): Albany County (2006) authorized a Troy & Banks
utility audit, choosing the firm from contractors on a New York State contract. The Town of Alden
(2006) reasoned that a utility audit was a specialized service not required to be bid.
URL: https://www4.erie.gov/alden/sites/www4.erie.gov.alden/files/2022-09/TBM06.05.06.pdf

**1i. Medicare Advantage / EGWP conversion as a "savings vendor" buy (secondary).** NYC's Aetna MA
contract was reported at more than $15B over the initial term, with expected savings of about
$600M a year (Becker's, City & State). In September 2025 the MLC approved a new active/pre-Medicare
plan (EmblemHealth/UnitedHealthcare) with "savings of up to $1 billion annually compared to the
current plan" (verified):
https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2025/09/mayor-adams--statement-on-municipal-labor-committee-approval-of-
*Inferred:* NYC buys health savings in billion-dollar blocks, and only with MLC sign-off.

**1j. Not found.** I found no public contract in which an employer pays a vendor to enroll retirees
in MSP, LIS or Medicaid to offset its own Part B reimbursement, with or without a contingency fee.
I also found no NY municipal pharmacy-audit contingency contract or Medicare secondary-payer
recovery contract. Outside NY, the NC State Health Plan pays its third-party-liability vendor on
contingency (secondary: https://shpnc.org/documents/board-trustees/third-party-liability-recovery-services-rfppdf/open).

### 2. New York procurement rules

**2a. Bid thresholds, GML §103(1) (verified).** Bidding applies to purchase contracts "involving an
expenditure of more than twenty thousand dollars" and public works "involving an expenditure of
more than thirty-five thousand dollars".
URL: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GMU/103
OSC adds: "There are several exceptions to the competitive bidding and competitive offering ('best
value') requirements, such as purchases made through the New York State Office of General Services
[...] emergency purchases, professional services and purchases from a legitimate sole source."
URL: https://www.osc.ny.gov/files/local-government/publications/pdf/seeking-competition-in-procurement.pdf

**2b. Professional services exception (verified).** OSC: "there is a well-established exception to
these competitive bidding requirements with respect to the procurement of professional services,
such as those rendered by attorneys, engineers or accountants." OSC says professional services
include services "requiring specialized or technical skills, expertise or knowledge, the exercise
of professional judgment or a high degree of creativity."
URLs: https://www.osc.ny.gov/files/local-government/publications/pdf/professional-service-procurement.pdf
(July 2018); Seeking Competition guide, p.18.
OSC's 2018 review: of 720 professional-service agreements audited, "502 (about 70 percent) of
these contracts had been awarded without some type of a competitive process" (same 2018 PDF).
*Inferred:* benefit eligibility screening and enrollment help (Medicaid/MSP rules, judgment calls
on each case) should fit the professional-services exception. A district's or town's lawyer makes
that call. The exception has no dollar ceiling.

**2c. GML §104-b policies (verified).** The board must "by resolution, shall adopt internal
policies and procedures" for procurements not subject to bidding. Policies must say how
"alternative proposals or quotations [...] shall be secured", and must "require justification and
documentation of any contract awarded to other than the lowest responsible dollar offeror." The
board may decide, "in the sole discretion of the governing body," when seeking alternatives is not
in the locality's best interest. "The governing board shall annually review its policies and
procedures."
URL: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GMU/104-B
*Inferred:* Mycelium must ask for each target's §104-b policy. Many require 2–3 written quotes or an
RFP above a dollar threshold. A cheap first-year pilot below the quote threshold, or a policy that
exempts professional services, may need only a board resolution.

**2d. Piggybacking, GML §103(16) (verified): goods only.** OSC: localities may "purchase apparatus,
materials, equipment or supplies or contract for services related to the installation, maintenance
or repair of apparatus, materials, equipment, and supplies through the use of a contract let by the
United States or any agency thereof, any state or any other county, political subdivision or
district therein", if "let either to the lowest responsible bidder or on the basis of best value".
URL: Seeking Competition guide, p.14; statute https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GMU/103
*Inferred:* Sourcewell/OMNIA-style cooperative contracts can't be used in NY to buy a professional
service like Mycelium's under §103(16). They would add nothing anyway, since professional services
are already exempt from bidding.

**2e. OGS centralized contracts, GML §104 (verified).** Localities may buy "commodities, materials,
equipment, technology, food products, supplies or services" available under State Finance Law §163,
over $500. URL: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GMU/104
*Inferred:* an OGS contract covering this service would let every NYSHIP employer buy without its
own RFP. Getting on one is a long State procurement, so this is not a first-year path. Albany
County's 2006 utility-audit vendor reportedly came from a State contract (secondary, paraphrase),
so OGS may have contracted contingency-style audit services before.

**2f. Contingency fees (gap).** I found no OSC opinion either prohibiting or approving
shared-savings or contingency contracts for NY municipalities or school districts. Searches of OSC
legal opinions came up empty. Practice shows they are signed: Varick (30%, 2026), Ulster County
(secondary, https://comptroller.ulstercountyny.gov/powering-up-comptroller-surveys-dollars-behind-countys-telecommunication-and-utility-services),
and Albany County. A 2017 Senate bill (S7672) would have barred cities from contingency-paid
utility-tax auditors (secondary, status unknown): https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2017/S7672.
*Inferred:* contingency is allowed in practice for recovery-type professional services, but have
counsel confirm it, and define "savings" objectively as reimbursement actually stopped or recovered.

**2g. BOCES (verified, partial).** Education Law §1950(4)(d): BOCES may, "At the request of
component school districts, and with the approval of the commissioner," provide services "on a
cooperative basis", including "such other services as the commissioner may approve."
URL: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EDN/1950
*Inferred:* a BOCES cooperative service ("CoSer") could carry Mycelium to many districts at once,
but it needs SED approval and fits the BOCES annual planning cycle. It is slower than one
district's resolution and better as a second step.

**2h. NYS agencies (Civil Service as plan sponsor) (verified).** State agencies may buy "in an
amount not exceeding one hundred fifty thousand dollars" without formal competition. From firms
certified "pursuant to article fifteen-A of the executive law" (M/WBE) or as veteran-owned, the
limit is "one million five hundred thousand dollars" (SFL §163(6)).
URL: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/STF/163
Contracts that "exceeds fifty thousand dollars in amount" need OSC approval, and OSC has "ninety
days" to act (SFL §112). URL: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/STF/112

**2i. NYC PPB Rules (verified, PPB Rules PDF dated April 2026).**
- Micropurchase, §3-08(c)(1)(ii): "For procurements of goods and all services except construction
  the value of which is $20,000 or less [...] no competition is required".
- Small purchase, §3-08(a)/(c)(1)(iii): "not more than $100,000"; "at least five vendors must be
  solicited at random from the appropriate citywide small purchases bidders list".
- M/WBE small purchase, §3-08(c)(1)(iv): "No competition is required for the procurement of goods,
  services, and construction from M/WBE vendors", but the officer "must attempt to obtain at least
  three price quotes from M/WBE vendors". The cap is the Charter §311(i)(1) amount, which the NYC
  Comptroller confirms is $1.5M: "Effective December 19, 2023, the method's threshold was raised to
  $1.5 million." (https://comptroller.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/documents/Calendar-2025-Third-Quarter.pdf)
- Demonstration project, §3-11: "a short-term, carefully planned, pilot exercise designed to test
  and evaluate the feasibility and application of an innovative product, approach or technology not
  currently used by the City." The ACCO must find, among other things, "the product, approach, or
  technology is not currently in use in City government" and "there is an intent to acquire [...] if,
  after testing and evaluation, a decision is reached to continue its use". The term "shall not
  exceed three years", extendable "for an additional period of up to three years with CCPO
  approval". Notice "shall be published at least once in the City Record at least ten calendar days
  before negotiations are expected to begin". It "may be initiated by an unsolicited proposal".
- Negotiated acquisition, §3-04(b)(2)(ii): "there is a limited number of vendors available and able
  to perform the work".
- Registration, §2-12: contracts "shall be presented to the Comptroller for registration."
URL: https://www.nyc.gov/assets/mocs/downloads/Regulations/PPB/PPBRules.pdf
*Inferred:* the §3-11 demonstration project, started by an unsolicited proposal to OLR, is the
cleanest NYC route. The "not currently in use in City government" test fits, since no MSP-screening
service appears in City records. A $20K micropurchase pilot (e.g., one retiree cohort) is the
fastest door but very small.

**2j. MWBE (verified).** NYC: MBE/WBE goals under Admin Code §6-129 applied even to the DEVA
negotiated acquisition (1a). NYS: Article 15-A certification raises the discretionary limit to $1.5M
(2h). GML §103(16) "does not waive" M/WBE obligations (statute page).
*Inferred:* certifying as an M/WBE, if the founders qualify, or teaming with a certified firm,
shortens the path in both NYC and NYS.

### 3. Who decides

**3a. Part B reimbursement is a NYSHIP-wide employer duty (verified).** Civil Service Law §167-a:
the premium "shall be paid monthly or at other intervals to such active or retired employee from the
health insurance fund", and "All other employer contributions to the health insurance fund shall be
adjusted as necessary to provide for such payments."
URL: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CVS/167-A
NYSHIP: "Part B premium reimbursement happens when Medicare is primary"; it does "not reimburse Part
D premiums or Part D IRMAA." URL: https://nyship.ny.gov/medicare-and-nyship
*Inferred:* for NYSHIP participating employers (PEs), the reimbursement flows through the State
health insurance fund and comes back to the PE in its premium charges. A PE's savings depend on
Civil Service (the Employee Benefits Division) adjusting the PE's charges, so **Civil Service is a
gatekeeper** for any NYSHIP school district or town even when the district signs the contract.
Confirm how PE Part B costs are billed (see open questions).

**3b. Non-NYSHIP districts and consortia (secondary).** Many districts reimburse Part B under their
collective bargaining agreements or consortium plans. For example, the Putnam Northern Westchester
Health Benefits Consortium "requir[es] all such eligible beneficiaries to enroll in the Medicare
Part B program as their primary insurance" (2023 decision, search excerpt:
https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/pdfs/2023/2023_34921.pdf). The Wappingers CSD reimburses Part B
"on a semi-annual basis", with partial reimbursement where a retiree is partly reimbursed "from
another source" (search excerpt of its January 2025 form; the PDF has since moved).
*Inferred:* decision-makers are the district business official (recommends), the Board of
Education (votes the contract), and for consortium districts the consortium board (pays claims). In
towns and counties: the personnel/benefits office plus the comptroller or treasurer, and the
governing board votes. Nassau's comptroller runs Part B reimbursement directly (1e).

**3c. NYC (verified/secondary).** OLR's Health Benefits Program runs Part B reimbursement and was
the contracting agency for the dependent audit (1a). The Comptroller registers contracts (2i).
Retiree-health changes run through the MLC: Mayor Adams said on June 20, 2025 that the City will not
proceed with Medicare Advantage "at this time" and "We have informed union leadership that we are
pursuing other avenues" (verified:
https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2025/06/mayor-adams-on-future-medicare-advantage).

**3d. Unions and the school-district retiree moratorium (secondary).** Chapter 729 of the Laws of
1994 bars a district from diminishing retirees' health benefits unless it makes "a corresponding
diminution in the health insurance benefits or contributions of active employees". In 2025 the
Appellate Division applied it against Orange-Ulster BOCES, which had "reduced reimbursements to
retirees for certain Medicare Part B charges and eliminated reimbursements for Medicare Part D
premiums" (2025 NY Slip Op 05076, search excerpt; the court site blocked fetching):
https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/3dseries/2025/2025_05076.htm
*Inferred:* ending reimbursement for a retiree whose premium the State now pays is arguably not a
diminution, because the retiree still pays $0 and the policy already excludes premiums "paid by
another entity". A union or retiree group could still challenge it. Get the retiree organization
and union to endorse the outreach first, and make enrollment voluntary.

### 4. Sales-cycle evidence

**4a. NYC procurement cycle times (verified).** MOCS FY24 Citywide Indicators: "The median
end-to-end cycle time for all RFPs was 376 days in FY24". The median for competitive sealed bids was
"328 days". Also: "Professional Services has the highest retroactivity, with just 37% of contracts
registered within 30 days" of the contract start date.
URL: https://www.nyc.gov/assets/mocs/downloads/Resources/citywide-indicator-reports/previous-year/FY24ExecutiveSummary.pdf

**4b. Gartner public-sector buying cycle (secondary; the press release blocked fetching).** "At 22
months, the public sector has the longest average buying cycle for technology purchases compared to
other industries". This came from a late-2021 survey of 1,120 executives, of whom 79 were public
sector. Figures are from news reprints:
https://ciotechasia.com/government-tech-purchase-decisions-take-on-average-22-months/ ; original
https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2022-09-06-gartner-survey-finds-government-tech-purchase-decisio

**4c. Budget calendars (verified).** School districts: the annual meeting and budget vote "shall be
held on the third Tuesday of May" (Education Law §2601-a; fiscal year July 1, not in the excerpt
read). URL: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EDN/2601-A
Towns: tentative budget "On or before the thirtieth day of September"; adoption "not later than the
twentieth day of November"; the fiscal year begins "on the first day of January". URLs:
https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/TWN/106 ; https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/TWN/109
*Inferred:* a contingency deal needs no new appropriation, since the fee comes out of money the
employer no longer spends. That lets it skip the budget calendar, which is a real speed advantage.
A fixed fee would need a line in the May (district) or November (town) budget. Expect 2–6 months
for a small district or town and 12–24 months for NYC or the State.

### 5. Politics, privacy, and what the pitch must avoid

**5a. NYC Medicare Advantage fight (secondary).** The Court of Appeals ruled on June 18, 2025, in
*Matter of Bentkowski v City of New York* (2025 NY Slip Op 03690), that the City may implement an
Aetna MA plan (City & State: https://www.cityandstateny.com/politics/2025/06/nys-highest-court-sides-city-over-retirees-medicare-advantage-fight/406165/).
The City then shelved the plan anyway (3c). Mayor Mamdani reportedly opposes MA (secondary, unverified).
*Inferred:* NYC retirees and their organizations are organized, litigious and suspicious of any
change that saves the City money on retiree health. A Mycelium pitch to NYC needs retiree
organizations as partners, not targets.

**5b. Cost-shifting to Medicaid (inferred).** The employer's savings are paid by State and federal
Medicaid (section 6). I found no public controversy specifically about employers steering retirees
into MSP, which matches 1j: it hasn't been done visibly. The likely attacks are (i) from the State:
"localities shifting costs onto the State Medicaid budget"; (ii) from retirees: "pushing seniors
onto welfare".
Answers, verified:
- MSP is exempt from estate recovery. 42 U.S.C. 1396p(b)(1)(B)(ii) excludes "medical assistance for
  medicare cost-sharing" (added by Pub. L. 110–275, effective January 1, 2010).
  URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/1396p
- Every MSP tier makes the retiree automatically eligible for full Extra Help. 42 CFR 423.773(c)(1):
  an individual "must be treated as meeting the eligibility requirements for full subsidy eligible
  individuals" if eligible "as a Qualified Medicare Beneficiary (QMB), Specified Low Income Medicare
  Beneficiary (SLMB), or a Qualifying Individual (QI)".
  URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/423.773
- MSP is an entitlement Congress created for exactly these people.

**5c. Federal headwind (secondary).** OBBBA §71101 "prohibits the Secretary from implementing,
administering, or enforcing certain provisions" of the 2023 MSP streamlining rule "until October 1,
2034" (King & Spalding:
https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-explained-a-detailed-review-of-key-changes-for-the-healthcare-industry).
*Inferred:* the federal government chose to slow MSP enrollment. A pitch that boasts of "maximizing
federal dollars" is politically exposed, so frame it as helping retirees use benefits they are
entitled to.

**5d. Privacy (verified + inferred).** ERISA Title I "shall not apply to any employee benefit plan
if— (1) such plan is a governmental plan" (29 U.S.C. 1003(b)):
https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/1003. HIPAA's "Health plan" includes "(i) A group health
plan" and "(xvii) Any other individual or group plan [...] that provides or pays for the cost of
medical care". A business associate is a person who "creates, receives, maintains, or transmits
protected health information" on a covered entity's behalf (45 CFR 160.103):
https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/160.103
*Inferred:* a governmental retiree health plan is a HIPAA covered entity even though it is outside
ERISA. Mycelium would be its business associate and must sign a BAA. Income and asset data the
retiree gives Mycelium directly falls under consent and NY's general data-security law, not HIPAA
(not researched here). NYC unions demanded "satisfactory safeguards regarding security and
confidentiality" in 2013 (1b).

**5e. The pitch must avoid:**
- "Cut retiree benefits", "stop reimbursements", "Medicaid", or "shift costs to the State". Say
  instead: "help retirees claim State/federal premium help they qualify for".
- Fees per retiree *dropped*, or any incentive that looks like denial-for-pay. That was the Illinois
  retiree complaint (1g).
- Mandatory enrollment, or threats of losing reimbursement. Keep it voluntary and with consent, and
  have the employer stop reimbursing only on the State's confirmation.
- Promising NYC savings without the MLC and retiree groups (3c, 5a).

### 6. Fiscal effect on NY governments

**6a. FMAP (verified).** KFF lists New York's FMAP at "0.5" for FY2026 and "0.5000" for FY2027.
URL: https://www.kff.org/medicaid/state-indicator/federal-matching-rate-and-multiplier/?state=NY

**6b. QI is 100% federal up to an allotment, permanently funded (verified).** 42 U.S.C. 1396u-3(d):
"to the extent that such assistance does not exceed the State's allocation [...] the Federal medical
assistance percentage shall be equal to 100 percent; and (2) to the extent that such assistance
exceeds such allocation, the Federal medical assistance percentage is 0 percent." The national total
is set "for 2016 and, subject to paragraph (4), for each subsequent year, the total allocation
amount is $980,000,000", indexed to the Part B premium.
URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/1396u-3

**6c. Local Medicaid share frozen (verified OSC 2006; secondary NYSAC 2026).** OSC: "New York is one
of the few states in the nation that requires a local contribution"; "Growth in Medicaid
expenditures is capped at 3.5 percent in 2006, 3.25 percent in 2007 and 3.0". URL:
https://www.osc.ny.gov/files/local-government/publications/pdf/medicaidupdate.pdf
NYSAC, June 24, 2026, citing the SFY27 Briefing Book: "The State takeover, which capped local
districts' Medicaid costs at calendar year 2015 levels, is projected to save local districts a total
of $9.2 billion in FY 2027. Since 2015, the Medicaid cap has saved counties and New York City $62.8
billion." The enacted budget "assumes the continuation of the local cap on Medicaid costs through FY
2027." URL: https://nysac-001-us.govstack.com/media/crul1ovk/sfy-2027-enacted-budget-county-impact-report-6-25-26.pdf

**6d. Net result by employer type (inferred from 6a–6c, 3a):**

| Employer | Saves (reimbursement it stops paying) | Pays in new Medicaid cost | Net |
|---|---|---|---|
| Town, village, school district | 100% | $0 (no Medicaid share) | about +100% |
| County, NYC | 100% | about $0 (local share frozen at 2015 levels) | about +100% |
| NYS as employer, QMB/SLMB enrollee | 100% | 50% (non-federal share) | about +50% |
| NYS as employer, QI enrollee (within allotment) | 100% | 0% | about +100% |
| NYS as Medicaid payer for a local retiree | 0% | 50% (QMB/SLMB) | a cost to the State |

So every local employer gains about one-for-one. Statewide, the State bears a 50% share of each
QMB/SLMB local retiree enrolled. That is the real political exposure (5b). QMB also covers
cost-sharing, which may save the employer's secondary plan money but costs Medicaid more. That is
not quantified here.
