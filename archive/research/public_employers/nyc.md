# New York City: Medicare Part B reimbursement and the MSP saving

Researched 2026-10-07 (all URLs accessed 2026-10-07). Labels: **verified** = read in the primary
source and quoted; **secondary** = news, advocacy or a search-tool summary of a source we could not
open; **inferred** = our arithmetic or reasoning, not stated by any source.

## Summary in plain English

1. The saving is real on paper. The City's own Part B form already says it reimburses only a retiree who "is not receiving Medicare Part B reimbursement(s) from any other source including Medicaid." So when the State starts paying a retiree's premium through MSP, the City should stop paying (finding 4).
2. The City pays the reimbursement once a year, in April, for the previous calendar year. It goes to the same account as the pension, or by check. The Office of Labor Relations (OLR) Health Benefits Program runs it. Proof is a copy of the Medicare card. Retirees enroll once and do not re-apply (findings 1–3).
3. It is large. The City paid $398M in base Part B plus $43M in IRMAA in FY2021 (IBO). In 2020 it was $382M, up from $54M in 2000 (OLR). The actuary counts about 252,000 Medicare-eligible retirees and spouses and puts the Part B accrued liability at **$24.8B** (June 30, 2024). Our estimate for 2025 is about $500–570M a year (findings 5–8).
4. **The big catch: the retiree gains almost nothing on the premium.** Under the "any other source" rule, a retiree who joins MSP loses the City check worth the same amount. What the retiree does gain is the premium paid up front rather than a year late, plus the extras MSP unlocks, such as Extra Help on drugs. The City keeps the premium saving. So the pitch to retirees has to be about those extras, and the buyer is the City (inferred, finding 4).
5. We found no evidence that OLR checks Medicaid or MSP status. It matches retirees only against pension payroll and the Social Security death index. The one audit of the program (Comptroller, 2011) did not test for Medicaid overlap. Some retirees may be collecting from both the State and the City, which is a separate recovery angle (findings 9–10).
6. NYC's own Medicaid share has been frozen at 2015 levels, and the State pays everything above it. A new MSP enrollee therefore costs NYC roughly nothing extra, so the City's ~$2,435 a year is close to a **net** saving. The State (QMB, about half) and the federal government (QMB half; QI 100% up to an allotment) bear the cost (findings 19–22).
7. Politics are hot. The 2021–2025 fight over moving retirees to Medicare Advantage ended when Mayor Adams dropped the switch in June 2025. The health fund those savings were meant to refill is insolvent; a December 2025 Comptroller audit says dissolve it. Retirees distrust the City and the unions (MLC/DC 37/UFT), and a new Council bill (Aug 2026) would freeze retiree benefits (findings 11–16).
8. Part B reimbursement is set by local law (§12-126), not bargained. A screening vendor changes no benefit, so it should not need Council or MLC sign-off. In practice, though, OLR (commissioner Renee Campion, kept on by Mayor Mamdani) and OMB would decide. HRA/DSS processes the MSP applications. The MLC and retiree groups will notice anything that looks like cutting benefits (findings 12, 15, 17).
9. NYC Aging already funds small MSP outreach contracts (about $18–23K each, through its HIICAP counseling program). We found no City outreach aimed at City retirees and no existing vendor for this (finding 18).
10. Before selling, we need three things: (a) how many City pensioners are under 186% FPL, (b) whether OLR can match against HRA's MSP rolls, and (c) whether a sale framed as a saving survives contact with the retirees' lobby. See the open questions.

## Findings

### 1. Mechanics

1. **Who is eligible (verified).** OLR's application lists four conditions: a City pension ("The Medicare-eligible retiree is receiving a pension from a City of New York pension system"), coverage "under a City of New York health plan", Medicare status in that plan, and condition 4 (finding 4). It covers "Medicare-eligible retirees and their Medicare-eligible dependents for any Medicare Part B premiums (excluding any penalties)". The form also says: "Once you submit this application, you will be enrolled ... and will not have to resubmit an application every year." Proof required: "YOU MUST PROVIDE A COPY OF YOUR MEDICARE CARD". The form also sends some retirees elsewhere: "Queens Borough Public Library retirees, Brooklyn Public Library retirees, and City University of New York retirees should contact their agency's benefits office directly"; "Retired NYCTA civilians ... must contact the Transit Authority."
   https://www.nyc.gov/assets/olr/downloads/pdf/health/med-b-application.pdf

2. **How and when it is paid (verified).**
   - Amount: the 2025 FAQ says "Standard reimbursement amount for Calendar Year 2025: $185.00 per person, per month."
   - Timing: "Payments are automatically issued in April for the previous year."
   - Method: direct deposit "will be a separate payment from your pension payment"; without direct deposit, "you will receive a check in the mail by the end of April."
   - IRMAA: "you may be eligible for any amounts paid over the standard premium amount through Income Related Monthly Adjustment Amount (IRMAA)." IRMAA is claimed separately, on yearly IRMAA forms.
   - Disputes: "Please submit a copy of your Form SSA 1099".
   - Part D premiums and Part B penalties are not reimbursed.

   The application also says reimbursement "will occur in the spring of the year, following the close of the year in which you paid Medicare Part B premiums."
   https://www.nyc.gov/assets/olr/downloads/pdf/health/faq-medicare-part-b.pdf ; application above.
   *Conflict:* the 2011 Comptroller audit says "The Office issues annual reimbursement checks in August" (CY2009 practice). The current FAQ says April.

3. **Who administers it (verified).** The office is the NYC Health Benefits Program, "Attn: Medicare Unit, 22 Cortlandt Street, 12th Floor" (application). The Comptroller's audit says "The New York City Office of Labor Relations (Office) administers various employee programs, including the Medicare Reimbursement Program." The Office of the Actuary says: "OLR administers the Program ... ACCNY Section 12-126 defines OPEB payable to include Health Insurance, Medicare Part B Premium Reimbursement, and Welfare Fund benefits." The pension systems supply the payment channel: "Please notify your pension system directly of any bank account changes" (FAQ).
   https://comptroller.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/documents/7I11_085.pdf ;
   https://www.nyc.gov/assets/actuary/downloads/pdf/OPEB_GASB_7475_Report_FY2025.pdf

4. **The rule when Medicaid/MSP or another party pays (verified; the consequences are inferred).**
   - The form's condition 4 reads: "The retiree is currently paying Medicare Part B premiums and is not receiving Medicare Part B reimbursement(s) from any other source including Medicaid."
   - The form's tax note covers only "the Medicare Part B premiums actually paid to Medicare by retirees, pursuant to Section 12-126".
   - §12-126(b)(1) itself has no Medicaid exclusion. Its text is restated in full in Int. 874-2023, which was not enacted. It ends: "each year thereafter, the City shall reimburse covered employees in an amount equal to one hundred percent of the Medicare Part-B premium rate applicable to that year."
   - So the Medicaid exclusion is OLR's administrative condition, not statutory text (inferred). A retiree could argue the statute requires paying the full rate. The words "reimburse" and "actually paid" support OLR's reading (inferred).
   - **Consequence (inferred):** once MSP pays the premium, the retiree stops qualifying, and the City saves the standard premium: $202.90 × 12 = $2,434.80 in 2026. MSP enrollees are low-income, so no IRMAA saving. The retiree's premium cost is unchanged: the State pays instead of the City. What the retiree gains is not waiting about 4–16 months for the April check, plus MSP's linked benefits (finding 18).
   https://www.nyc.gov/assets/olr/downloads/pdf/health/med-b-application.pdf ;
   Int. 874 text: https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/View.ashx?M=F&ID=11546632&GUID=157EEA04-3151-4380-95F3-12BDAAEBDCB1 (status: https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=5982439&GUID=37E949CB-EE49-4A08-88F5-DC10512F6E77 — "Filed (End of Session)" 12/31/2023)

### 2. Size

5. **FY2021 spending (verified, IBO).**
   - Total: "In Fiscal Year 2021 New York City paid $3.2 billion for the provision of health care to its over 250,000 retirees."
   - Part B: "Reimbursement of Medicare Part B premiums for all Medicare-enrolled retirees, $398 million."
   - IRMAA: "$43million."
   IBO testimony, Oct 2021 (live URL now redirects to ibo.nyc.gov; read via Wayback):
   https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://ibo.nyc.ny.us/iboreports/medicare-advantage-testimony-october-2021.pdf

6. **Trend (verified, OLR).** Commissioner Renee Campion testified on Oct 28, 2021: "the reimbursement of Medicare Part B coverage for retirees has gone from $54 million in 2000 to $382 million in 2020, an increase of over 600%." She also said "the City reimburses retirees and their spouses for the cost of Medicare Part B coverage. These are increasingly unusual and increasingly expensive benefits."
   https://www.nyc.gov/assets/olr/downloads/pdf/collectivebargaining/final-city-council-testimony-oct28-2021-10am.pdf
   CY2009 data point (verified, Comptroller): "Medicare Part B reimbursements totaling $190,051,486 for 130,215 New York City retirees and dependents", plus "$6,379,312 ... (IRMAA) to 4,973 higher income retirees."
   *Conflict/caveat:* OLR's 2020 figure ($382M), IBO's FY2021 figure ($398M) and the Comptroller's CY2009 figure ($190M) use different year bases (calendar vs fiscal year). They are consistent in direction.

7. **Headcount, FY2025 actuarial valuation (verified).**
   - Retirees currently "Medicare Eligible": 189,628 participants and 62,827 spouses.
   - Retirees eligible for Part B reimbursement "currently or in the future": 290,487 participants and 116,637 spouses.
   - Plus Part B-only component-unit retirees (HHC, SCA, WFA, CUNY Senior Colleges): 31,461.
   - Claim-rate assumption: "90% of Medicare participants are assumed to claim reimbursement based on historical data."
   - Premium assumption: "the annual premium used was $2,158.20". The IRMAA load is "12.5%".
   - The valuation contains no Medicaid or dual-eligible offset: a text search found no mention of Medicaid (inferred: the City does not model MSP offsets).
   https://www.nyc.gov/assets/actuary/downloads/pdf/OPEB_GASB_7475_Report_FY2025.pdf (dated September 5, 2025)

8. **Liability and current-year size (verified + inferred).**
   - Part B accrued liability, City excluding component units, June 30, 2024 valuation at a 4.21% discount rate: "Part B Premium ... d. Total $24,836,256,249" (Entry Age Accrued Liability). That is about 24% of the $102.9B total. The retired-member portion is $14,120,476,091.
   - The trust's balance sheet shows a liability "Medicare Part B $279,393,466" at June 30, 2025.
   - Inferred: this looks like the January–June half of CY2025 reimbursements, payable in April 2026, which implies roughly $560M a year.
   - Cross-check (inferred): 252,455 Medicare-eligible × 90% × $2,220 (12 × $185) ≈ $504M base, or about $567M with the 12.5% IRMAA load.
   - The Comptroller's fiscal note (June 2025) puts total OPEB unfunded liability at "approximately $100 billion" and says that "In FY 2024 the OPEB plan had nearly 570,000 members, with approximately 257,300 receiving benefits."
   OPEB report above; https://comptroller.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/fn_4_opeb.pdf

### 3. Audits

9. **Comptroller audit 7I11-085, Aug 19, 2011, CY2009 (verified).**
   - Overall finding: "The Office has adequate internal controls ... and avoiding duplicate payments."
   - Exceptions found: "$2,892 in improper reimbursements to five retirees" (dependents terminated from Medicare, or deceased) and "$1,157 questionable payment to one of 25 retirees with foreign addresses".
   - Method: OLR matches "against the Pension Payroll system and the Social Security Death Index".
   - **The audit did not test for Medicaid/MSP overlap**: the word "Medicaid" does not appear in the report (verified by text search).
   https://comptroller.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/documents/7I11_085.pdf

10. **No later audit found (inferred from search).** We found no Comptroller, DOI or OSC audit of Part B reimbursement after 2011, and none about premiums already paid by Medicaid. Two searches were run; this is not an exhaustive negative.

### 4. Politics and decision-making

11. **The Medicare Advantage push.** The City Council committee report on Int. 874 (Jan 9, 2023, verified) says:
   - "On July 14, 2021, MLC voted to approve a contract to move City retirees from traditional Medicare with supplemental city insurance (i.e., GHI Senior Care) to a Medicare Advantage plan."
   - "would result in $600 million in annual savings."
   - The savings target came from the June 2018 City–MLC deal for "$1.1 billion over fiscal years 2019-2021, and additional savings of $600 million in every following fiscal year."

   Commissioner Campion said in 2021 that the plan would mean "Retirees continue to have a robust program of premium-free health insurance plus their Medicare Part B reimbursements" (verified, OLR testimony).
   Committee report: https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/View.ashx?M=F&ID=11560768&GUID=A5B4D23B-3A74-477D-BEE0-882A5F7E426F

12. **Int. 874 (verified).** Introduced "by request of the Mayor" on 1/4/2023. It would have let the City and MLC set a "benchmark plan". It was never passed: "Filed (End of Session)" on 12/31/2023. This shows §12-126 changes need the Council.
   Separately, CBC said reimbursement "is specified in local law and can be changed by the City Council and Mayor" (secondary, search summary of CBC; https://www.plansponsor.com/group-suggests-nyc-retirees-pay-for-medicare-premiums).

13. **Lawsuits.**
   - The Comptroller's fiscal note (June 2025, verified): "In December 2024, the NY State Court of Appeals ruled that the City must cover the cost of the Senior Care plan because it is lower than a cap set by the HIP HMO plan."
   - On June 18, 2025, the Court of Appeals, in *Matter of Bentkowski v City of New York*, 2025 NY Slip Op 03690, reversed the retirees' promissory-estoppel win. It held that §12-126(b)(1) does not require a Medicare supplemental plan (secondary: search summaries of https://www.nycourts.gov/CTAPPs/Decisions/2025/Jun25/57opn25-Decision.pdf; the opinion page returned 403).
   - *Conflict:* press coverage labels the December 2024 ruling inconsistently ("Court of Appeals" versus Appellate Division). The Comptroller's note says Court of Appeals.

14. **Outcome (secondary).**
   - UFT, Sept 5, 2025: "The Adams administration has dropped its effort to implement a Medicare Advantage health care plan"; "Mayor Eric Adams announced on June 20 that his administration did not intend to move forward with the switch." https://www.uft.org/news/retired-teachers-chapter-news/rtc-information/city-pulls-plug-on-medicare-advantage-plan
   - The City Reporter, Aug 13, 2026: "then-Mayor Eric Adams ditched the effort last summer."

15. **2026 state of play (secondary, The City Reporter, Aug 13, 2026).**
   - The Marte bill "would prohibit the city from establishing any health care program that would 'diminish' retirees' current access to care."
   - "Neither Mamdani nor city Comptroller Mark Levine, who was sworn in on January 1, have said how they plan to deal with the issue of health costs going forward."
   - DC 37 and the UFT "fiercely opposed prior efforts by the retirees and the Council"; the unions argued such laws "infringed on their collective bargaining rights."
   - The MLC "has been operating without an elected leader since ... May 2025," with "Henry Garrido, DC 37's executive director" as de facto leader.
   - Retiree group head Marianne Pizzitola "has complained ... that Mamdani and his surrogates have allegedly ignored repeated requests for meetings."
   https://www.thecityreporter.nyc/2026/08/13/medicare-advantage-ny-city-council-menin-mamdani/

16. **Health Insurance Stabilization Fund insolvent (secondary, The City Reporter, Dec 30, 2025, on the Lander audit).**
   - The audit found the fund "owes the city $3.1 billion".
   - It quotes the audit: "the audit recommends that the City work with the MLC to dissolve the Fund."
   - "The fund is paid for by taxpayers and jointly managed by the city and the MLC."
   https://www.thecityreporter.nyc/2025/12/30/lander-health-stabilization-fund-municipal-labor/
   Consistent with this, the Comptroller's fiscal note says "Its disposable balance was essentially depleted in FY 2024" (verified).

17. **Who decides (verified + inferred).**
   - Mayor's release, Jan 27, 2026 (verified): "Renee Campion will continue to serve as Commissioner of the Office of Labor Relations (OLR)." https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2026/01/mayor-mamdani-announces-additional-appointments-across-key-city-
   - A 2026 civil-service task force names "Office of Management and Budget Director Sherif Soliman ... Department of Social Services Commissioner Erin Dalton and Office of Labor Relations Commissioner Renee Campion" (secondary, search summary of https://nysfocus.com/2026/10/05/mamdani-unions-civil-service-government-hiring).
   - Inferred:
     - OLR owns the Part B program and its data, so it is the buyer.
     - OMB holds the budget line: retiree health pay-as-you-go runs through the Retiree Health Benefits Trust.
     - DSS/HRA processes MSP applications in NYC. The Legal Services NYC summary says applications go to HRA (secondary, https://legalservicesnyc.org/?p=701).
     - Pension systems (NYCERS, TRS, BERS, Police, Fire) hold addresses and bank details.
     - MLC consent is not legally required for an outreach vendor, because the benefit is in local law. But given finding 15, a pitch framed as "saving the City money on retirees" could be read as another benefit cut.

### 6. Fiscal: who pays when a NYC resident enrolls in MSP

19. **NYC's Medicaid share is frozen (verified, OSC).** "The State caps the local share of Medicaid at calendar year 2015 levels". Amounts: "The January Plan budgets $6.6 billion in FY 2025 and $6.5 billion in FY 2026 for the City share of Medicaid expenses." The page is undated; it cites the FY2026 Preliminary Budget.
   https://www.osc.ny.gov/reports/budget/fed-funding-ny/medicaid-nyc

20. **History of the cap and freeze (verified, CBC ~2018 via Wayback; DOB 2012).**
   - The 2005 cap: "In 2005 a cap on the local share was proposed as part of Governor George Pataki's executive budget and was enacted"; growth was capped "at 3.5 percent, 3.25 percent, and 3" percent.
   - The 2012 phase-out: "in 2012 the Governor and Legislature enacted a phaseout of local cost growth, ultimately resulting in a freeze ... annual growth was reduced from 3 percent to 0 percent in 2015."
   - Since then: "All nonfederal costs above the frozen amount are now paid for by the State." NYC's frozen share is "$5.3 billion".
   - State Division of the Budget, FY2012-13: "reducing growth in the local share of Medicaid payments by 1 percent annually beginning in 2013-14, fully eliminating all growth by 2015-16".
   https://web.archive.org/web/2024id_/https://cbcny.org/research/still-poor-way-pay-medicaid ;
   https://web.archive.org/web/2024id_/https://www.budget.ny.gov/pubs/archive/fy1213archive/eBudget1213/fy1213littlebook/HealthCare.pdf

21. **QI is federally funded up to an allotment (secondary/older primary).** "The Federal matching rate for Medicaid payment of Medicare Part B premiums for QIs is 100 percent for expenditures up to the amount of the State's allotment" (CMS 2004 letter, as summarized by search; https://medicaid.gov/federal-policy-guidance/downloads/smd012804.pdf). QMB is paid at the regular Medicaid match (Senate Finance staff summary, secondary).

22. **Net effect for NYC (inferred).** Under the 2015 freeze, an extra MSP enrollee adds nothing to NYC's local Medicaid payment, which is fixed in dollars. The State pays the non-federal QMB share; QI is federal up to the allotment. So the City's ~$2,435 a year per retiree is a **net** City saving, not offset by NYC's Medicaid bill. The cost moves to the State and federal government. That matters if the State ever reopens the freeze, or if Albany objects to a City program that pushes costs onto the State. We did not confirm that the freeze survived the FY2027 State budget (open question).

## Open questions

1. **How many City pensioners qualify?** The actuarial report gives counts but no income distribution. We need pension amounts by tier, plus Social Security, against 186% FPL for single and couple, from OLR/NYCERS/TRS data or the Comptroller's pension reports. Many City retirees have both a pension and Social Security, which could push them above QI. The eligible share may be small; this decides whether the deal is $5M or $50M.
2. **Does OLR ever match against HRA/State MSP rolls or CMS buy-in files?** If not, some retirees on MSP may be collecting both (a recovery angle, finding 10). If it does, the "already enrolled" pool is already netted out.
3. **How does OLR learn a premium is Medicaid-paid?** Self-report, SSA-1099, or nothing? Would a newly enrolled retiree's April check be clawed back for the MSP months? Unknown. This shapes retiree consent and messaging.
4. **Is the "including Medicaid" condition legally firm?** §12-126 says "reimburse ... one hundred percent of the Medicare Part-B premium rate". A retiree or union could argue for paying regardless. We found no case law.
5. **Does the 2015 local-share freeze still hold for SFY2026-27,** and is MSP Part B buy-in spending inside the capped local share? We need a current DOB/OSC source.
6. **Who signs?** Would OLR contract directly, or route it through the MLC or the Retiree Health Benefits Trust? Would OMB count the saving in the Financial Plan? Does the City need HRA data-sharing agreements (retirees' consent, HIPAA, State DOH approval)?
7. **Pass-through populations** (CUNY/TIAA, Brooklyn and Queens libraries, NYCTA civilians, HHC, SCA) are reimbursed by their own employers. These are separate buyers.
8. **Exact current Part B outlay.** We need an OMB budget line (Miscellaneous budget, retiree health / "Part B") for FY2025–FY2027. We did not find one; the latest verified figure is FY2021.
9. **The Bentkowski opinion text** (403) and the December 2025 HISF audit itself (we read press coverage only) should be read directly.
