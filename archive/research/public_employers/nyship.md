# NYSHIP Part B reimbursement: is enrolling retirees in MSP a real saving for New York public employers?

Researched 2026-10-07. All sources accessed 2026-10-07. Labels: **verified** = the quote was read in the primary document itself; **secondary** = taken from a non-primary source, or from a search-engine excerpt of a primary page that could not be fetched (budget.ny.gov and nycourts.gov blocked or timed out every request today); **inferred** = my reasoning, not stated in any source.

## Summary (plain English)

1. The rule is real. Civil Service Law §167-a makes every NYSHIP employer reimburse Medicare-primary retirees and their spouses or dependents for Part B, including IRMAA. Civil Service's own retiree booklet says this reimbursement stops if the premium "is being paid by another entity on your behalf". Medicaid/MSP is such an entity. New York City (which is not in NYSHIP) names Medicaid outright.
2. The rule is not enforced automatically. NYSHIP starts reimbursement on its own at age 65, and Civil Service says Social Security "does not currently share Medicare premium information" with it. When MSP starts paying, the reimbursement probably keeps going until someone reports it. Once found, the retiree "will be required to repay". So enrolling a retiree in MSP without stopping the reimbursement creates a debt for the retiree, not a saving for the employer.
3. Who pays depends on the employer:
   - **State and public authorities:** the cost is spread across NYSHIP premiums ("socialized across all plan participants"). A saving goes to the pooled fund, and the State's own Medicaid budget picks up part of the MSP cost. That makes the State a weak buyer.
   - **The roughly 800 local Participating Agencies (school districts, counties, towns):** they pay each retiree directly, "in addition to the employer's premium contribution". MSP is paid by State/federal Medicaid, so every MSP enrollee is a direct cash saving for the locality. This is the sellable segment.
4. Size per person: $202.90 × 12 = **$2,434.80 a year** per Medicare-primary person in 2026, plus any IRMAA (MSP enrollees would almost never owe IRMAA).
5. Size for the State: about $249M in calendar 2019 (budget excerpt). By my estimate that is about 150k people reimbursed, and it would be roughly $370M at 2026 premiums. No 2024–26 count was found.
6. Number of employers: about 97 Participating Employers (public authorities) and about 802 Participating Agencies (local governments and school districts).
7. Political sensitivity is high. Governors proposed cutting IRMAA or capping Part B reimbursement in most budgets from FY2016 to FY2027. Unions and retiree groups opposed every time, and the Legislature has restored it every time so far. The FY2027 outcome is unconfirmed.
8. Duplicate payments really happen. Nassau County's June 2026 audit found 94 spouses/dependents paid Part B by both the County and another employer: about $1.5M confirmed, with $258k recovered so far. Nassau does no Medicaid/MSP cross-check.
9. No existing State or local program was found that screens NYSHIP retirees for MSP or Medicaid, or checks reimbursement against Medicaid buy-in. That gap is the opening, and also the risk.
10. The retiree has almost no direct incentive: the employer already makes them whole on Part B. Main retiree upsides are QMB cost-sharing protection and automatic Extra Help (inferred). The pitch has to be built for the employer, with honest disclosure to the retiree.

## Findings

### 1. Mechanics

**F1 (verified): what the statute says.** §167-a ties the payment to "an amount equal to the premium charge", paid from the health insurance fund and allowed to be included with the pension payment. The statute itself does not mention who actually pays the premium.
- Quote: "Upon exclusion from the coverage of the health benefit plan of supplementary medical insurance benefits for which an active or retired employee or a dependent ... is or would be eligible ..., an amount equal to the premium charge for such supplementary medical insurance benefits for such active or retired employee and his or her dependents, if any, shall be paid monthly or at other intervals to such active or retired employee from the health insurance fund. ... in the case of a retired employee receiving a retirement allowance, such amount may be included with payments of his or her retirement allowance."
- URL: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CVS/167-A (page shows last revision 2014-09-22)

**F2 (verified): the "paid by another entity" exclusion, and how payment is made (2019 booklet for State and PE retirees).**
- Quote: "When Medicare is primary to NYSHIP, NYSHIP reimburses you for the standard Medicare Part B premium (excluding any penalty for late enrollment) and any IRMAA you must pay for Part B, unless you receive reimbursement from another source or if your Medicare premium is being paid by another entity on your behalf."
- Dependents: "also must reimburse you for the standard Part B premium and any Part B IRMAA imposed for any Medicare-primary dependent, provided the dependent is not reimbursed by another source or the premium is not paid by another entity."
- How paid: "If you receive a pension check through the New York State and Local Retirement System or New York State Teachers' Retirement System, any reimbursement for Medicare Part B will be added to your pension check." Direct-pay retirees get a premium credit and a "quarterly refund check from the Office of the State Comptroller" for any excess.
- Repayment: "If you receive reimbursement for the Medicare Part B premium for yourself and/or your dependent when not eligible, you will be required to repay the amounts that were paid incorrectly."
- URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/nyship/shared/publications/medicare/2019/ny-pe-medicare-nyship-2019.pdf ("Medicare & NYSHIP", April 2019, pp. 6, 11)

**F3 (verified): current 2026 wording is softer (conflict with F2).** The March 2026 "Medicare and NYSHIP" booklet no longer says "paid by another entity" in its reimbursement section. It says only:
- "After you retire, NYSHIP will reimburse you for the Part B premium through your pension check, a premium reduction or a quarterly refund check. If you and/or your dependent are reimbursed from another source, contact EBD."
- "This reimbursement is automatic for anyone Medicare-eligible because they have reached age 65."
- IRMAA "must" be claimed each year with a "Medicare Part B IRMAA Reimbursement Application" and proof (SSA-1099, RRB-1099 or CMS-500).
- URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/pa-market/shared/publications/medicare/2026/ny-pe-medicare-nyship-2026.pdf (March 2026, pp. 2, 11)
- Also verified in "Your Coverage in Retirement" (March 2026): "NYSHIP reimburses you and your covered dependents for the standard Part B premium"; and IRMAA reimbursement is paid by "a check from the Office of the State Comptroller ... IRMAA reimbursement cannot be applied to a pension or direct-deposited." URL: https://www.farmingdale.edu/human-resources/pdf/ny-your-coverage-in-retirement-2026.pdf (a SUNY campus copy of the Civil Service publication)

**F4 (verified): the State has had no SSA premium data, and recovers overpayments by offset (Dec 2017 notice).**
- Quote: "The Social Security Administration (SSA) ... does not currently share Medicare premium information directly with NYSHIP. ... if NYSHIP discovers that an enrollee or dependent has been reimbursed more than the actual premium paid, NYSHIP may offset any future Medicare Part B premium reimbursements to recover these amounts."
- URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/hba/shared/notices/2018/2018_medicare_part_B_reimbursement_notice.pdf
- Inferred: unless this has changed since 2017 (not confirmed), the start of an MSP buy-in is invisible to NYSHIP.

**F5 (verified): Medicaid dual eligibles were a known group in 2016, and the "another entity" rule appears again.**
- Quote: NYSHIP "reimburses New York State enrollees for their standard Medicare premiums when Medicare is primary to NYSHIP coverage, unless the premium is paid by another entity or is fully reimbursed elsewhere." The same letter lists "Enrollees/dependents who are eligible for both Medicare and Medicaid" among those who might pay the higher $121.80 premium.
- URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/hba/shared/notices/2016/medicare-part-b-differential-letter.pdf (Feb 2016)

**F6 (verified): Participating Agency (local) rules.** The PA Manual, Section 3.8 (pages dated 02/01/08, in the consolidated 3/01/18 manual), sets four points:
- Reimbursement is required "whether or not the person is enrolled in Medicare".
- It covers "any income related monthly adjustment amount (IRMAA)".
- It is required "in addition to the employer's premium contribution".
- On other payers: "If a Participating Agency has documentation that an employee or dependent who is eligible for Medicare coverage is receiving Medicare reimbursement from another source (e.g., a public agency or private employer), the Participating Agency is not required to provide a duplicate Medicare reimbursement."
- On recovery: "The agency has the right to require a refund of Medicare Part B premium amounts that were incorrectly reimbursed."
- On method: payment can be by "Checks ... (minimum once a year)" or by a reduced retiree contribution.
- URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/hba/shared/manuals/pa/pdfs/PA_Manual.pdf (2007 single-section copy: https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/pa-market/shared/manuals/pa/pdfs/3.8_medicare.pdf)
- Inferred: the PA wording speaks of "reimbursement from another source", not "paid by another entity", so Medicaid is not named. Also, "whether or not ... enrolled" frames the benefit as a fixed entitlement rather than a pass-through of premiums actually paid. That leaves legal room for a retiree or union to argue that payment continues even under MSP. See open questions.

**F7 (verified): New York City states the Medicaid exclusion outright.** New York City is outside NYSHIP; the analog is useful.
- Quote: reimbursement requires that "The retiree is currently paying Medicare Part B premiums and is not receiving Medicare Part B reimbursement(s) from any other source including Medicaid."
- URL: https://www.nyc.gov/assets/olr/downloads/pdf/health/med-b-application.pdf

**F8 (secondary): IRMAA added in 2007.** The 2007 PA memo PA07-31, as quoted in a search excerpt, says Civil Service concluded §167-a "does, in fact, require NYSHIP employers to reimburse the entire premium paid for Part B". The memo itself sits behind an Okta login.
- URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/hba/memos/2007/pa07-31.pdf

### 2. Who bears the cost

**F9 (verified): the statute pools the cost for the State and public authorities.**
- Quote: "This cost shall be included in the calculation of premium or subscription charges for health coverage provided to employees and retired employees of the state, public authorities, public benefit corporations or other quasi-public organizations of the state ... All other employer contributions to the health insurance fund shall be adjusted as necessary to provide for such payments."
- URL: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CVS/167-A

**F10 (verified): the State's actuary confirms the cost is spread across premiums.**
- Quote: "The State reimburses the Medicare Part B premium of Medicare eligible participants, including additional premiums for higher-income participants, the cost of which is socialized across all plan participants through plan premium rates." The report also notes "fewer than 5% of enrolled individuals" are expected to exceed the IRMAA threshold.
- URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/pa-market/pdfs/2020-PA-Assumptionreport.pdf (Aon, Aug 2021, p. 4)

**F11 (verified): local Participating Agencies pay their own retirees directly.**
- Quote: "Regardless of a Participating Agency's rate of contribution, Section 167a of the NYS Civil Service Law requires that the Participating Agency must also reimburse the Medicare Part B premium for each enrollee (i.e., retiree, spouse or other eligible dependent of retiree)..." and "A Participating Agency may not reduce its employer rate of contribution" because of it.
- The frequency of payment is the agency's choice: "Frequency of Medicare Part B reimbursement (i.e., monthly, quarterly, or yearly)".
- URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/pa-market/shared/manuals/pa/pdfs/1.4_requirements_for_agency.pdf
- Field example (verified): Nassau County says it "is obligated to reimburse eligible retirees for Medicare Part B premiums because the County participates in ... NYSHIP" and pays "twice a year, in June and December." URL: https://www.nassaucountyny.gov/DocumentCenter/View/53259/Review-of-Medicare-Reimbursements

**F12 (verified): terminology (a conflict with the brief).** In Civil Service usage:
- "Participating Employers (PEs)" are public authorities and public benefit corporations (e.g., the Thruway Authority).
- "Participating Agencies (PAs)" are local governments: school districts, special districts and municipal corporations.
- Cities of 1 million or more (i.e., NYC) are excluded from NYSHIP.
- URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/pa-market/shared/manuals/pa/pdfs/1.4_requirements_for_agency.pdf

**F13 (inferred): where the saving lands.**
- **PA retiree enrolls in MSP:** the PA saves $2,434.80 a year (2026), and Medicaid (State/federal) takes on the premium. This is a cash cost shift away from the locality.
- **State or PE retiree enrolls in MSP:** the saving goes to the pooled NYSHIP fund. The State is both the main NYSHIP payer and the Medicaid payer, so its net gain is only the federal share of the MSP cost. Federal matching rates for QMB/SLMB/QI were not verified here.

### 3. Size

**F14 (secondary; search excerpt of a DOB page that could not be fetched): State cost.**
- FY2019 Executive Budget, General State Charges: cost "increasing from $194 million in Calendar Year (CY) 2017 to $242 million in CY 2018."
- FY2020 Executive Budget: standard reimbursement at "$135.50 per month in calendar year 2019, and the cost of this reimbursement is $249 million."
- URLs: https://www.budget.ny.gov/pubs/archive/fy19/exec/agencies/appropData/GeneralStateCharges.html ; https://www.budget.ny.gov/pubs/archive/fy20/exec/agencies/appropData/GeneralStateCharges.html
- Inferred: $249M ÷ ($135.50 × 12) ≈ 153,000 people reimbursed. At $202.90 that would be about $373M a year if the headcount held, before IRMAA. These are State figures only, apparently excluding PAs.

**F15 (verified): NYSHIP scale.**
- 2018 enrollment: 619,035 enrollees, 1,243,836 total covered lives, of which 1,090,663 in The Empire Plan. The table does not split out Medicare-primary members. URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/extdocs/pdf/NYSHIP%20Enrollment.pdf
- Employers: "At present, there are roughly 97 Participating Employers and about 802 Participating Agencies in NYSHIP." URL: https://www.cs.ny.gov/extdocs/pdf/Empire%20Plan%20Hospital%20Pricing%20Report_Plan%20Years%202023%20and%202024.pdf
- Conflict (secondary): an undated Civil Service FAQ reportedly says "nearly 900 local government employers"; the page did not load. https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/pa-market/faq.cfm

**F16 (verified): one large PA.** Nassau County "currently provides benefits for approximately 10,240 retirees and 3,690 retiree dependents". The dollar value of Medicare reimbursements "has increased by 61 percent since 2017". The dollar totals are only in charts that could not be extracted. URL: https://www.nassaucountyny.gov/DocumentCenter/View/53259/Review-of-Medicare-Reimbursements

**F17 (secondary): retiree incomes are modest.** Retiree group RPEA testified: "Most retirees receive a pension under $30,000 per year." URL: https://nyassembly.gov/write/upload/publichearing/001506/006568.pdf
- Inferred: a pension alone under about $30k may still exceed QI limits once Social Security is added. The MSP-eligible share of NYSHIP retirees is unknown and probably small (low single-digit percent), but no data was found. Illustration only: if 2% of about 153k State-reimbursed people qualified, that is about 3,000 people and about $7.4M a year.

### 4. History and politics

**F18 (secondary; search excerpts of DOB pages): proposals by year.**
- FY2020 Executive Budget: ending IRMAA reimbursement would "save taxpayers $2.9 million in FY 2020 ... increasing to $12.4 million in FY 2021." Capping standard reimbursement at the 2019 $135.50 would save "$2.3 million in FY 2020 and $12.2 million fully annualized in FY 2021."
- FY2022 Executive Budget: eliminating IRMAA saves "$4.0 million in FY 2022, increasing to $17.1 million in FY 2023." The $148.50 cap saves "$1.8 million in FY 2022 and $9.3 million fully annualized in FY 2023."
- FY2026 (DOB): "eliminate the reimbursement for the Medicare Part B IRMAA for high income State retirees and create an annual premium refund for certain State retirees". The search tool paired this with savings of "$12.3 million in FY 2026 and $12.9 million in FY 2027", but a follow-up search could not find that figure, so it is unconfirmed.
- URLs: https://www.budget.ny.gov/pubs/archive/fy20/exec/agencies/appropData/GeneralStateCharges.html ; https://publications.budget.ny.gov/pubs/archive/fy22/ex/agencies/appropdata/GeneralStateCharges.pdf ; https://www.budget.ny.gov/pubs/archive/fy26/ex/agencies/appropdata/GeneralStateCharges.html

**F19 (verified): FY2027 proposal and the opposition.**
- PEF Retiree Organization testimony (Feb 25, 2026): "Governor Hochul has again proposed legislation in the Executive Budget that cuts the IRMAA benefit ... (S.9005/A.10005 – PPGG - PART EE)". The proposal "would retroactively and arbitrarily discontinue the IRMAA Medicare Part B reimbursement for retirees who retired after 2012 from positions in grade 9 or above." URL: https://nyassembly.gov/write/upload/publichearing/001506/006905.pdf
- RPEA testimony on the FY27 proposal: "Reject elimination of Medicare IRMAA reimbursement". The document is dated "February 25, 2025", which conflicts with its own FY27 subject; probably a typo for 2026. URL: https://nyassembly.gov/write/upload/publichearing/001506/006568.pdf
- RPEA in 2025: "Previous budgets have eliminated this reimbursement. The legislature has restored it every time." URL: https://nyassembly.gov/write/upload/publichearing/001450/005913.pdf
- RPEA also claims "Chapter 602 of the Laws of 1996 created Section 167-a". This is **unverified**; it looks wrong, since §167-a predates IRMAA (2007).
- NYSUT Retiree Council 22 (Spring 2026): "this year's budget does not include funding for Medicare's IRMAA reimbursement ... our union is already fighting to get IRMAA rebates restored- lobbying which has been successful every year in the past." URL: https://rc22.ny.aft.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/2026/Newsletter%20March%202026%20V2%20Final%202%2026%2026.pdf
- FY2027 enacted outcome: **not found**.

**F20 (secondary; search excerpt, court page blocked): 2006 court ruling against the State.** In *Matter of United Univ. Professions v State of New York*, 36 AD3d 297 (3d Dept, Nov 22, 2006), the State had reinterpreted §167-a. The court found that "the new interpretation of Civil Service Law § 167-a was inconsistent with the plain language of the statute and its legislative history." URL: https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/3dseries/2006/2006_08692.htm
- Inferred: courts read §167-a strictly in favor of retirees.

### 5. School districts outside NYSHIP, and audits

**F21 (secondary; search excerpts, court pages blocked): districts reimburse Part B by contract or past practice, and courts block cutoffs.**
- *Matter of Baker v Bd. of Educ.* (Wappingers, 2d Dept 2006): the district had reimbursed Part B "as part of continuing health insurance benefits provided for in their respective collective bargaining agreements". Stopping in October 2003 violated the 2003 retiree-benefit Moratorium (L. 2003 ch. 48). https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/3dseries/2006/2006_03578.htm
- Chenango Forks (29 Misc 3d 706, 2010): the district had reimbursed "continuously since at least 1980" without a CBA requirement, and the court directed it to continue. https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/3dseries/2010/2010_20360.htm
- Northeastern Clinton (3d Dept 2014): https://nycourts.gov/Reporter/3dseries/2014/2014_01496.htm
- Chappaqua (2d Dept 2021): an order to "reinstate reimbursement for the Medicare Part B surcharges" was affirmed. https://nycourts.gov/reporter/3dseries/2021/2021_03318.htm
- Whether each of these districts is in NYSHIP was not confirmed. Inferred: Part B reimbursement is common in New York school districts by CBA, inside and outside NYSHIP. No count of non-NYSHIP districts that reimburse was found.

**F22 (verified): OSC audit of a school district.** Plainview-Old Bethpage CSD (2018M-148): "the District requires Medicare-eligible retirees to annually certify in writing that they are still eligible for reimbursements." Auditors warned that relying on this "could result in the District paying unnecessary costs", and that "Most retirees belong to the New York State Teachers' Retirement System which does not notify districts when members have died." No overpayment was found among 540 retirees. URL: https://osc.ny.gov/files/local-government/audits/pdf/plainview-old-bethpage.pdf

**F23 (verified): OSC guidance on deceased retirees.** "An audit of 20 school districts revealed ten school districts who collectively paid $239,000 in health insurance benefits for deceased or ineligible retirees." The guidance lists "uncashed Medicare reimbursement checks" as one way to detect deaths. URL: https://www.osc.ny.gov/local-government/publications/cost-saving-ideas-monitoring-health-insurance-premiums-retirees

**F24 (verified): Nassau County Comptroller audit, June 15, 2026 (a county audit, not OSC).** The County found 99 dependents who also had retiree benefits through another employer; 94 duplicate payments were confirmed. The audit covered payments "as far back as 2002". The problem "was occurring for two decades or more". Totals: "$1,623,111.02" suspected, "$258,172.30" recovered as of June 1, 2026. Nassau requires a signed affidavit at retirement and puts endorsement language on each check. It checks other municipal employers only; Medicaid is not mentioned. URL: https://www.nassaucountyny.gov/DocumentCenter/View/53259/Review-of-Medicare-Reimbursements

**F25 (verified): NYC Comptroller audit, 2011.** NYC's Part B program reimbursed retirees who had died, and payments continued "three to four years after the retirees were deceased" in some cases. URL: https://comptroller.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/documents/7I11_085.pdf

### 6. Existing screening for MSP or Medicaid

**F26 (inferred from absence): no program found.** No State or local program was found that screens NYSHIP retirees for MSP or Medicaid, or matches Part B reimbursement against the Medicaid buy-in. The checks that do exist are:
- self-attestation (Plainview-Old Bethpage annual certification; Nassau affidavit);
- employer-to-employer duplicate checks (Nassau);
- NYSHIP's acknowledged lack of SSA premium data (F4).

The nearest analog is NYC's form, which names Medicaid (F7). Searches of NYSHIP and DOH documents found no GIS on how an employer's Part B reimbursement interacts with MSP eligibility.

## Open questions

1. **Does §167-a let an employer stop reimbursing when Medicaid/MSP pays?** The statute pays "an amount equal to the premium charge" and the PA Manual says "whether or not the person is enrolled". The "paid by another entity" exclusion comes from Civil Service publications (2016, 2019), and the 2026 booklet dropped that wording. Given *UUP v State* and the school-district Moratorium cases, a union challenge is plausible. A legal opinion or a written answer from Civil Service EBD is needed.
2. **Payer of last resort:** Medicaid is generally a last-resort payer. Could DOH or the local Medicaid office treat the employer's reimbursement as a third-party resource and refuse MSP, or count it as income? Not found; ask DOH, or check the MSP GIS on income budgeting.
3. **Does SSA now share premium or buy-in data with NYSHIP** (it did not as of the Dec 2017 notice)? If not, the State has no detection, and existing MSP enrollees may already be "double paid".
4. **Current headcount and dollars:** Medicare-primary NYSHIP enrollees and dependents, and State and PA Part B reimbursement totals for 2024–26. Try DOB General State Charges for FY2025–27 (the site was down today), Civil Service annual reports, State and PA GASB 75 valuations, and a FOIL request to Civil Service EBD.
5. **FY2027 enacted budget:** was the PPGG Part EE IRMAA cut enacted or restored?
6. **Number of school districts outside NYSHIP** (self-insured or consortium, e.g., BOCES health plans) that reimburse Part B by CBA. Sources to try: NYSSBA or NYSUT contract databases, Teachers' Retirement System data.
7. **Federal financing of MSP in New York** (QMB/SLMB at the regular match, QI federal allotment), the local share, and so the State's net saving. Needed for any pitch to the State itself.
8. **What share of NYSHIP retirees are MSP-eligible** (QMB ≤138% FPL, QI ≤186% FPL, no asset test)? No data; retiree income distributions could come from the State retirement system (NYSLRS) or Teachers' Retirement System (TRS) pension tables combined with Social Security estimates.
9. **Retiree consent and incentive:** moving the premium to MSP leaves the retiree's net income on Part B unchanged and may expose them to repayment of past double payments (F2, F4, F6). How should the product disclose this, and should it only enroll retirees going forward with simultaneous notice to the employer?
