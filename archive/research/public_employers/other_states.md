# Public employers outside New York that reimburse Medicare Part B

Researched 2026-10-07 for Mycelium. This pass builds on `archive/research/national_fund_targets/prevalence_and_sweep.md`.

**Labels**
- **verified**: we read the quote ourselves on the sponsor's own page or document, or on a state agency or government audit page.
- **secondary**: a search-engine summary, a third party (KFF, a union, a broker), or a page we could not open.
- **inferred**: our own arithmetic or reasoning.

PDFs were downloaded and their text extracted locally. All quotes come from that extracted text or from the page. The 2026 standard Part B premium is $202.90 a month ($2,434.80 a year).

## Summary: the best non-NY targets, ranked

1. **Los Angeles County (LACERA) is the strongest non-NY target.**
   - About 39,960 participants and about $100M a year in 2026.
   - The Board letter makes payment conditional on the retiree "currently paying for Medicare Part B premium themselves" and "not being reimbursed ... by another agency".
   - The program is re-verified every year, and California's MSP asset limit is lenient ($130,000).
   - The County pays none of the Medi-Cal cost, so all of the Part B it stops paying is savings (inferred).
   - The deciders are the Board of Supervisors and the CEO; LACERA administers.
2. **Hawaii EUTF is the largest by dollars.**
   - Part B reimbursement for the State alone is projected at **$119.6M in FY2026**, with $2.95B of accrued Part B liability.
   - Payment is "upon receipt of proof of payment to SSA or CMS", and EUTF collects overpayments.
   - The ERS average pension is $34,402, with a big low-pension tail.
   - Drawbacks: Hawaii uses the federal $9,950 asset test, and the State pays part of the Medicaid cost.
3. **Connecticut has the most MSP-eligible retirees per dollar.**
   - MSP income limits are the most generous in the country (QMB $2,807 a month, ALMB $3,272), with **no asset test**.
   - Roughly 85,700 retirees, spouses and survivors are covered; the per-capita Part B reimbursement is $2,038.
   - Drawback: the Comptroller pays automatically ("assumes retirees pay ... the base premium"), so the saving appears only if the State stops paying people it enrolls in MSP.
   - The State also pays part of the MSP cost (Medicaid match).
4. **CalPERS (State and CSU annuitants) is the largest in headcount.**
   - The written stop rule is explicit: "If you're no longer paying a Part B premium ... contact us immediately to stop" it.
   - California's asset test is $130,000.
   - Drawbacks: we found no published count or dollar total, and the benefit is closed to state hires from 2016 to 2020 onward.
5. **Boston pays 50% of the premium.**
   - 15,497 retirees and beneficiaries get City retiree health benefits.
   - Massachusetts has no MSP asset test and high income limits.
   - As a local employer, Boston keeps the whole saving. It also pays Part B late-enrollment penalties for about 2,082 people.
   - **Cambridge (85%)** and **Newton**, whose program is income-tested at 65% of area median income, are similar Massachusetts cases.
6. **University of California (UC) is a newly found reimburser.**
   - UC credits Part B ($185 per person basis in 2026) to the pension check whenever UC's contribution exceeds the plan premium.
   - California's MSP rules apply. We found no count.
7. **New Jersey covers a lot of retirees but the payment is small.**
   - The State plan covers 50,858 retirees; the Local Education plan covers 105,130.
   - Many State retirees are capped at $46.10, and State employees hired after 1995 get nothing.
   - NJ has the federal asset test, and the State pays part of the Medicaid cost.
8. **LACERS and LAFPP (City of Los Angeles) are weaker targets.**
   - Both pay the standard premium only, to the retiree only.
   - Their pensions are high: LACERS averages $4,932 a month, and LAFPP pays police and fire pensions. That leaves few MSP-eligible retirees.
9. **Small or odd cases:**
   - Imperial County, CA reimburses the Part A *and* Part B premiums.
   - LA Metro had about 700 retirees and $0.58M in FY2009.
   - Sonoma County is frozen at $96.40.
   - The Port Authority of NY & NJ reimburses through HealthEquity/WageWorks.
   - NJ municipalities reimburse under Chapter 88 or N.J.S.A. 52:14-17.38(b).
   - Nevada PEBP gives a premium credit or an HRA, so MSP saves it little.
10. **Key structural point (inferred):** MSP is Medicaid. QMB and SLMB are paid with the federal/state match, and QI is 100% federal. A **state** employer such as CT, HI, CA or NJ gets back the full reimbursement but pays its Medicaid share, so its net saving is roughly 100% of the reimbursement minus its state share (40–50% of the premium for QMB/SLMB, $0 for QI). A **local** employer (LA County, Boston, Cambridge, UC, LACERS) keeps the whole saving. The pitch is cleanest for counties and cities.

## Numbered findings with quotes

### California

1. **LA County: the program, its size and its cost.**
   - Source: Board of Supervisors letter "Medicare Part B Premium Reimbursement Program for 2026" (document date 12/9/2025). https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/bos/supdocs/210237.pdf
   - History: "Since the inception of the Program in 1992, the County has reimbursed the full cost of the standard Medicare Part B premium for any retiree enrolled in a Medicare Risk HMO or Medicare Supplement Plan." (verified)
   - Size: "There are an estimated 39,960 retirees and their dependents enrolled in these plans." (verified)
   - Cost: "increase the Program costs by approximately $9.2 million per year to a total of $100 million (compared to the projected 2025 Program year costs of $99.6 million)." (verified)
2. **LA County: the eligibility rule that matters for MSP.** The letter lists four criteria, including: "3) currently paying for Medicare Part B premium themselves; and 4) not being reimbursed for the Medicare Part B premium by another agency." (verified)
   - LACERA's page says the same thing: "Paying for their Medicare Part B premium themselves" and "Not being reimbursed for their Medicare Part B premium by another agency, such as another employer or state". (verified) https://www.lacera.gov/program-basics/premium-reimbursement
   - Inferred: a retiree whose Part B is paid by Medi-Cal (QMB/SLMB/QI) fails criterion 3, so the County stops paying once it knows.
3. **LA County: why the program exists, and how MSP fits that logic.**
   - The County renews the program only after finding each year that it is cost effective: "the savings that result from the reduction in Medicare Part B premiums must be greater than the cost". (verified)
   - Actuarial basis: "Cheiron concluded that without the Program, the County would pay an additional $260 million in medical plan premium reimbursements but see a cost savings of $128 million with the Program for a net County cost increase of $132 million per year." (verified)
   - Cheiron's data: "there were 41,383 retirees in this category as of June 30, 2025. Of these, 12,185 had less than 25 years of service". (verified)
   - Inferred: MSP keeps retirees in Medicare (the County's goal) while moving the Part B premium to Medi-Cal. That is pure savings for the County, which funds no part of Medi-Cal.
   - Administration: LACERA administers the plans and does the annual verification. The CEO's Retiree Health/Classification and Compensation unit brings the renewal to the Board, and the Board decides (verified from the letter's structure: "Reaffirm the Board's right to change or terminate the Program").
4. **LA County pensions.** LACERA's 2025 ACFR shows an average annual allowance of "$61.8" thousand for 74,767 payees at June 30, 2024.
   - Short-service tiers are much lower. For General members retired FY2015-16, the average monthly benefit is "$1,619" (5–9 years of service) and "$1,809" (10–14 years). (verified) https://www.lacera.gov/sites/default/files/assets/documents/annual_reports/ACFR-2025.pdf
   - Inferred: the low-service tail sits near California's 2026 SLMB/QI countable-income limits ($1,596 / $1,796), before counting Social Security.
5. **CalPERS (State and CSU): the stop rule.** (all verified) https://www.calpers.ca.gov/retirees/health-and-medicare/medicare
   - "If you're no longer paying a Part B premium to the SSA, contact us immediately to stop the Part B reimbursement."
   - "...you'll be required to reimburse us."
   - "...we'll automatically reimburse the eligible amount of the standard Medicare Part B premium..."
   - Contracting agencies are excluded: "CalPERS doesn't administer reimbursement of Medicare Part B premiums for retirees of contracting public agencies."
   - Cutoffs by first state hire date: Units 9 and 10 from 1/1/2016; most units from 1/1/2017; Unit 16 from 4/1/2017; Unit 5 from 1/1/2020.
6. **CalPERS: how the benefit is funded.** CalPERS Circular Letter 600-077-06 (Nov 2006): "The State reimburses employees and annuitants for their Part B premiums, if the member's total premium is less than the State employer contribution amount. This 'Part B premium reimbursement' cannot exceed the State contribution amount." (verified, dated) https://calpers.ca.gov/documents/600-077-06/download
   - Decision-makers: CalHR and the Legislature (PEMHCA §22879) set the benefit; CalPERS administers (inferred from the page's citation of section 22879).
   - Size: we found no current count or dollar total, because the State Controller's GASB 75 reports returned 403. The LAO said in 2008 that the annuitant program covered "about 220,000 retired state government and California State University employees, their family members" (secondary, search summary).
   - Pension: the CalPERS Facts at a Glance FY2024-25 shows state miscellaneous service retirees at about $3,840 a month (secondary; the PDF text was read through a search summary).
7. **University of California.** (all verified) https://ucnet.universityofcalifornia.edu/compensation-and-benefits/health-plans/medical/retiree-plan-costs.html and https://ucnet.universityofcalifornia.edu/retirees/understanding-your-retiree-benefits/eligibility-for-retiree-health-and-home-benefits/
   - "Medicare Part B reimbursement may apply if your premium cost is $0.00."
   - "Part B reimbursement is based on a Medicare Part B premium of $185.00 per person." (labeled "2026 monthly costs")
   - "Reimbursements vary and are added automatically to your monthly retirement payment."
   - The eligibility page says: "UC reimburses you for a portion of the standard Medicare Part B premium you pay for Medicare coverage."
   - Decision-maker: UC Office of the President and the Regents (inferred).
8. **LACERS.**
   - "Retired Members ... with at least 10 years of Service ... are eligible for a reimbursement of their standard (basic) Medicare Part B premium." (prior pass)
   - "IRMAAs are not part of the basic premium and, therefore, are not reimbursed." (verified) https://www.lacers.org/article/medicare-part-b-premium-2026
   - Segal's 6/30/2025 OPEB valuation: "LACERS will reimburse the retiree the basic Medicare Part B premium. LACERS does not reimburse survivors or dependents any part of their Medicare Part B premium."
   - Retirees: "Total number of retirees 16,195"
   - Average monthly pension: "4,932". (verified) https://www.lacers.org/sites/main/files/file-attachments/lacers__actuarial_valuations_for_retirement__health_benefits_combined_2025.pdf
9. **LAFPP.** (verified) https://content.lafpp.lacity.gov/medicare-part-b-premium-reimbursement-for-2026/
   - "Part B reimbursement only applies to Retired Members or Qualified Survivors who are eligible for an LAFPP health subsidy."
   - "LAFPP does not reimburse IRMAA fees."
   - Headcount: the Segal 6/30/2025 summary shows 14,540 current retirees, beneficiaries and dependents (secondary).
10. **Imperial County.** The 2026 retiree handout says: "In order to qualify for the reimbursements of Medicare Part A and Part B premiums (or Part D-IRMAA if applicable), you must enroll in the County's Retiree Health Plan. Reimbursement is only for retirees (not dependents) who have at least ten (10) years of service with the County." (verified) https://hr.imperialcounty.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/RETIREE-HEALTH-INSURANCE-Handout-2026.pdf
    - Inferred: QMB pays the **Part A** premium too (and California's Part A buy-in started in 2025, per the archive's CA MSP playbook). Imperial may be paying Part A premiums that QMB would take over.
11. **LA Metro.** OIG audit 10-AUD-09 (4/27/2010): "All Metro retirees are entitled to either total or partial Medicare Part B reimbursement when they enroll in Medicare Part B, except for United Transportation Union retirees."
    - "During FY 2009, Medicare Part B reimbursements totaling $576,180 were paid to about 700 retirees."
    - Eligibility needs only "proof of enrollment in Medicare Part B". (verified, dated) https://www.metro-oig.net/__static/be48718012e1b5f8e46f1299268a2b19/10-aud-09-medicare-part-b-reimbursements-to-retirees-4-27-10.pdf

### Hawaii

12. **EUTF rules.** (verified) https://eutf.hawaii.gov/medicare/part-b-reimbursement/
    - Hired before 7/1/2023: retirees "and their spouses or DPs/CUPs" paying premiums "(including IRMAA)" are eligible, "excluding penalties".
    - Hired on or after 7/1/2023: reimbursement "excludes penalties and IRMAA", and "Spouses or DPs/CUPs are not eligible".
    - "Reimbursement will begin upon receipt of proof of payment to SSA or CMS."
    - Over-reimbursement: "you will be subject to collection of the overpayment".
    - The page says nothing about Med-QUEST. Inferred: a QMB/SLMB enrollee pays nothing to SSA, so has no proof of payment.
13. **EUTF size.** State of Hawaii OPEB valuation as of 7/1/2024: the Part B accrued liability is "$ 2,954,826,000" of the "$ 11,122,280,000" total.
    - Projected Part B payments: "2026 ... 119,640,000" and "2027 ... 128,440,000".
    - Retirees: "40,158". (verified) https://eutf.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/SOH-OPEB-Valuation-Report-07012024-secured.pdf
    - SB1315 (2023): "The State's approximate annual medicare part B premium reimbursement is $98 million of which approximately $8.5 million is the IRMAA." (verified) https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/session2023/Bills/SB1315_.PDF
    - The counties (Honolulu, Maui, Hawaii, Kauai) have their own EUTF OPEB valuations that were not checked, so the statewide total is higher (inferred).
    - Decision-makers: the EUTF Board of Trustees, which runs the program, and the Legislature, which sets the rule in HRS §87A-23 (SB1315 amends "Section 87A—23").
14. **Hawaii pensions.** ERS ACFR FY2024: the average annual pension for 50,389 pensioners is "$34,402" (3/31/2024).
    - The average monthly service pension is "$2,863" overall.
    - By years of service: "$882" (10–14), "$1,418" (15–19), "$1,858" (20–24). (verified) https://ers.ehawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ACFR-2024-Final-Web-r.pdf
    - Hawaii's 2026 MSP income limits: QMB $1,550, SLMB $1,856, QI $2,086 (individual). (secondary, SSA POMS HI 00815.023 via fetch summary) https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.NSF/lnx/0600815023
    - Inferred: roughly 13,000 ERS retirees with under 20 years of service have pensions at or below the QI limit before Social Security. The binding limit is the $9,950 asset test.
