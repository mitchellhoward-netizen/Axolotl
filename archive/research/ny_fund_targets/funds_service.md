# NY service / retail / hospitality / home-care funds: MSP target research

Researched 2026-10-07. Form 5500 screening numbers come from `pension_plans.csv` and `health_plans.csv` in this folder. "avg_monthly_benefit" means benefits paid / recipients / 12. It is a screening number, not a pension amount for any one person.

## Summary (plain English)

1. In this group only one fund clearly still covers retirees after 65: the **NY Hotel Trades Council (HTC) Health Benefits Fund**. Its Retiree Health Plan runs through the fund's own Health Centers. The retiree must pay their own Part B premium and assign Part B to the fund's Health Center.
2. **No fund in this group was found to reimburse the Part B premium.** HTC says retirees "must maintain" their own Part B premium. None of the other funds' published documents mention reimbursement.
3. So MSP's main effect, the state paying the $202.90/month premium, is a **saving for the member, not the fund**, at every fund here.
4. HTC could still save money indirectly. The fund pays the 20% Part B coinsurance and deductibles for its retirees. For a retiree on QMB, Medicaid pays Medicare cost-sharing. Whether the HTC Health Center can bill Medicaid for that is not confirmed (inferred; needs a call to the fund).
5. **There is a real risk at HTC.** Joining MSP automatically qualifies a person for Extra Help (LIS). CMS then "facilitated enrolls" LIS-eligible people into a Part D plan. HTC ends retiree coverage, with no way back, for anyone who joins a Part D plan. Any HTC pilot must handle the Part D opt-out (or confirm that HTC claims the retiree drug subsidy, which excludes people from facilitated enrollment).
6. **32BJ** (35,057 pension retirees, about $584/mo) covers early retirees only until 65 or Medicare eligibility, whichever comes first. After that there is no fund coverage, so no fund saving. But it has the largest pool of low-income Medicare retirees here, so MSP would be a strong free member benefit.
7. **UFCW Local One** (upstate) has a retiree benefit that ends at 65 and has a $7,500 lifetime cap, so there is no Part B saving. **Local 338** tells retirees to buy Medigap or Medicare Advantage themselves. **SEBF** offers retirees only self-pay dental and optical. **Local 272**'s welfare SPD shows no retiree medical tier. None of these four has a Part B saving.
8. **DC 37 Local 389 Home Care** (about $166/mo) and **Local 1102** (about $156/mo) have the lowest pensions and likely the highest MSP eligibility rates. They are small funds in critical status, and no post-65 retiree health was found.
9. **United Welfare Fund** is the Taft-Hartley fund of the United Service Workers Union (USWU, IUJAT) in New Rochelle. It covers about 30,000 union members in many industries. No retiree coverage was found, and the Form 5500 lists more actives (88,543) than participants (82,662).
10. **Pilot ranking:** no fund here earns an "A" on fund savings today. HTC is the best conditional candidate (B, upgrade to A if the Medicaid crossover and the Part D issue check out). 32BJ is the best "member benefit" partner (B). The rest are C.

## Numbered findings

All accessed 2026-10-07 unless noted.

### 32BJ

1. **Verified.** The 32BJ Health Fund extends coverage for some retirees aged 62–65, ending at Medicare. The Metropolitan & Suburban SPD (June 1, 2024, with SMM 12/17/2025) says: "You and your eligible dependent(s) will be eligible for Plan coverage until (1) you become eligible for Medicare, (2) you reach age 65 or (3) your pension is suspended, whichever occurs first." To qualify, the last day worked must be "before age 65, but after age 62" and the member needs "15 years of pension service credit." https://health.32bjfunds.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/SPD_Metro_Suburban_20240601_SMM_20251217_ENG.pdf
2. **Verified.** VERIP early retirees get the same end point. Same SPD: "You and your eligible dependent(s) will be eligible for Plan coverage until (1) you become eligible for Medicare, (2) you reach age 65 or (3) your pension is suspended, whichever occurs first." Medicare-eligible dependents of early retirees "must enroll in both Medicare Part A and Part B coverage." Same URL.
3. **Verified.** The Health Fund's list of plans (Metropolitan, Suburban, Tristate, Basic, Part-time, Kaiser, NYC Schools Part-time, Suburban PA) includes no retiree or Medicare plan. https://health.32bjfunds.org/health-plans/
4. **Secondary.** The 32BJ pension FAQ, quoted in a search-engine excerpt (the live page now returns 404), says: "Some early retirees qualify for retiree health until age 65. These benefits are designed to supplement your Social Security and Medicare benefits provided by the Federal government." https://pension.32bjfunds.org/en-us/faqs.aspx
5. **Verified.** The pension site tells retirees to enroll in Medicare: "You are encouraged to apply when you are within three months of age 65". https://pension.32bjfunds.org/ready-to-retire/
6. **Verified.** The funds are administered in-house. "Building Service 32BJ Benefit Funds", "25 West 18th Street, New York, NY 10011-4676", Member Services "800-551-3225". https://32bjfunds.org/about-the-funds/
7. **Verified.** The Metropolitan Plan "covers participants who work in residential and commercial employment in Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens and Staten Island". A Spanish SPD is published (`..._SPA.pdf`), so Spanish is a published language. SPD URL as above; Spanish version linked from https://health.32bjfunds.org/metropolitan/
8. **Secondary.** Peter Goldberger is named Executive Director of the 32BJ Benefit Funds, overseeing "the eleven multi-employer funds" (search excerpt of a Health Affairs author page). https://www.healthaffairs.org/do/10.1377/hauthor20240802.128753/full/
9. **Secondary.** Manny Pastreich is president of 32BJ SEIU: "former Secretary-Treasurer Manny Pastreich became the union's new president" (search excerpt). https://www.seiu32bj.org/press-release/kyle-bragg-retires-manny-pastreich-new-president/

After 65 the fund pays nothing toward Medicare, so MSP gives **no fund saving**, but these are large low-income member pools.

### Hotel Trades Council (HTC)

11. **Verified.** The fund runs a Retiree Health Plan through its own Health Centers: "you must bring your Medicare card into our Central Administration office and designate the Health Center to be a Medicare Part B (medical) provider. There are no deductibles or co-insurances paid by the Retiree or Retiree's spouse when using the Health Center except for pharmacy co-pays." https://www.hotelfunds.org/retiree-health-plan/
12. **Verified.** Retirees pay their own Part B premium: "You must maintain your monthly Medicare Part B premiums to remain in the Retiree Health Plan." There is no reimbursement language. Also: "With a Health Center referral, a Retiree Health Plan participant will not have the standard deductibles or 20% co-pays for outside Part B medical services." And: "Hospital benefits are not covered by the Retiree Health Plan." Same URL.
13. **Verified.** Eligibility: "Be a 'Hotel Industry Retiree'… you must work for a Contributing Employer until you're eligible for a Pension benefit"; "Have been part of the Health Benefits Fund Plan for at least 36 months". Same URL.
14. **Verified.** Joining a Part D or MA plan ends coverage: "you and your spouse must not enroll in a Medicare Part D plan… a Health Maintenance Organization (HMO) or a Medicare Advantage plan… If you do, you or your spouse will be terminated from Retiree Health Plan coverage." Spouses/dependents "may not reenroll at any time in the future." https://www.hotelfunds.org/retiree-health-plan/termination/
15. **Verified.** The fund's FAQ wording is informal and partly contradicts finding 11: "if you are eligible to sign your Medicare Pt. B over to the union, we do not cover the 20% that Medicare does not. We become the Part D plan ONLY which is the prescription drug plan." https://www.hotelfunds.org/faqs/
16. **Verified.** "Eligible pensioners may elect to get their medical and prescription drug coverage through the Health Centers… pensioners must qualify… assign their Medicare Part B benefits to the Funds." The fund "owns and operates Health and Dental Centers in Midtown Manhattan, Harlem, Brooklyn, and Queens". https://hotelworkers.org/for-members/benefits/health-benefits/health-benefits-nyc
17. **Verified.** Fund office: "305 W. 44th Street", "New York, NY 10036", "(212) 586-6400". It serves "over 90,000 Union members, dependents and retirees of the New York City hotel industry." The site is in English and Spanish. https://www.hotelfunds.org/about/
18. **Secondary.** The union is led by Rich Maroko, president of the Hotel and Gaming Trades Council (search excerpt: "Richard Maroko … is the new president of the Hotel Trades Council", NJ Globe 2020). https://hotelworkers.org/rich-maroko
19. **Verified (CMS rule that matters for HTC).** CMS places LIS-eligible people (which includes everyone on MSP) into Part D plans: "Facilitated enrollment applies to partial-benefit dual eligibles and other LIS-eligible individuals… Other LIS eligible individuals not enrolled in a Part D plan are facilitated enrolled into a PDP by CMS." The exclusions include people who "Have opted out of auto-enrollment" and "individuals for whom an employer or union is claiming the retiree drug subsidy." The guidance also describes LI NET enrollment for LIS-eligible people without Part D. CMS CY2026 MA & Part D Enrollment and Disenrollment Guidance, §40.1.8. https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cy-2026-cd-enrollment-and-disenrollment-guidance.pdf
20. **Inferred.** HTC pays Part B cost-sharing that Medicaid covers for QMB enrollees, so the fund may save if its Health Center can bill NY Medicaid for QMB coinsurance. This is not confirmed. MSP also removes the retiree's $202.90/mo premium burden, which protects their HTC eligibility because coverage depends on keeping up Part B. HTC has 18,708 pension retirees (avg about $786/mo) and 24,404 health-fund participants (CSVs).

## Open questions

1. HTC: can its Health Centers bill NY Medicaid for Part B coinsurance for QMB retirees, and would they? This is the only plausible fund saving in this group.
2. HTC: does the fund claim the Retiree Drug Subsidy? If it does, its retirees are excluded from CMS facilitated Part D enrollment. If not, every MSP enrollee needs an opt-out, or they could lose HTC coverage for good. How does the fund treat LI NET enrollment?
3. HTC: the FAQ ("we do not cover the 20%… We become the Part D plan ONLY") conflicts with the retiree page. Which is current? Is the fund itself a Part D/EGWP sponsor? If so, LIS money would flow to the fund.
4. HTC: how many of the 18,708 pensioners are enrolled in the Retiree Health Plan?
5. Local 210 Affiliated H&I Fund: does the 2006 retiree medical plan still exist, and does it pay any Part B?
6. UFCW Local 1500 Welfare Fund, Local 1102 Health & Benefit Fund, Local 338 Benefits Fund: is there any post-65 retiree benefit? Ask for the current SPDs.
7. 32BJ: would the Funds' Executive Director (Goldberger, secondary) back a member-benefit MSP outreach with no fund saving? What share of the 35,057 retirees live in NY?
8. NY member share and languages: no fund here published these. Only Spanish is confirmed (32BJ SPD, HTC site).
