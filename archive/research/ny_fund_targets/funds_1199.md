# 1199SEIU funds: deep dive for Mycelium (MSP / Part B savings)

Researched 2026-10-07. All URLs accessed 2026-10-07 unless noted. Labels: **verified** = primary source (fund website, SPD, fund form, fund annual report, the fund's own site); **secondary** = trusted third party (news, union press); **inferred** = our reasoning from the quoted sources, not stated by anyone.

## Summary (plain English)

1. **The Part B saving is real at the National Benefit Fund (NBF).** The NBF pays eligible retirees back **50% of the standard Part B premium, for the retiree and the spouse**. In 2026 that works out to about $101.45 a month, or $1,217 a year, per covered person (inferred arithmetic).
2. Who qualifies: NBF retirees from the Health Care Employees Pension Fund (the 82,266-retiree hospital/nursing home plan) who retire with Wage Class I or II benefits and generally **10+ years of service**. They must have Part A and Part B, and are put into the Fund-paid **1199SEIU Aetna Medicare Advantage plan**.
3. The payment is a **claim the retiree files**, quarterly or yearly, with proof of Part B payment. If MSP starts paying a retiree's Part B, the retiree has no premium left to claim, so the Fund's 50% payment should stop. That is the saving (inferred).
4. **The Greater New York Benefit Fund (GNY) does not reimburse Part B for retirees.** It needs 25 years of service and covers the retiree only, not the spouse. It reimburses Part B only for working members in an ESRD/secondary-payer case, so there is no Part B saving there.
5. **Home care retirees get no retiree health from 1199.** The Home Care Benefit Fund's SPD says: "The Benefit Fund does not provide Retiree Health Benefits." The Home Care Pension Fund pays about $75 a month on average. These are the **poorest 1199 retirees, about 32,000 of them**, so MSP would help them a lot, but the funds save nothing.
6. The **NBF Rochester-area plan** also provides no retiree health. For the **Regional Pension Fund (Buffalo)**, a separate pension-only fund with 6 trustees, we found no retiree health benefit.
7. The Funds already take Medicare money seriously. A 2018 report says Medicare Advantage plans are re-bid "to help ensure the most favorable premiums" and that the Part D group plan is set up "to maximize available Medicare subsidies". A Trustees' committee, **CAVIC**, tracks cost-saving programs against "mandated savings targets". That is the door to pitch through.
8. **We found no evidence that any 1199 fund helps retirees apply for MSP, Extra Help or EPIC.** Medicare education comes from the Medicare Rights Center's "Seniors Out Speaking" webinars and Aetna orientations. The Member Assistance Program offers "referrals to entitlement programs (food stamps, Medicaid...)", but it is described as a benefit for working members.
9. Who decides: the boards are joint, union and employer, with the **League of Voluntary Hospitals and Homes** on the employer side. The Funds are run by **Executive Director Donna Rey, EdD**. The union president is **Yvonne Armstrong**, elected May 2025, who earlier was the trustee and senior executive vice president for home care and nursing homes. Vendor contracts are "re-bid routinely via an RFP process".
10. **Bottom line:** NBF is a good first target with a quantifiable Part B saving, made larger by spouses. Pitch it to CAVIC and the Retiree Health Benefits office as a savings program. Pitch home care and GNY as a member benefit, possibly through the union. The big unknown is **how many NBF retirees have low enough income for MSP**. Their pensions average about $1,027 a month, and the QI limit for a single person is about $2,494 gross a month, so many may qualify, but nobody has published a count.

### B. National Benefit Fund (NBF), the hospital and nursing home side

4. **verified: Part B 50% reimbursement (SPD).** "If you retire with Wage Class I or Wage Class II Benefits, you will be reimbursed for 50% of the standard Medicare Part B premium. You may file a claim annually or once each quarter to get this benefit. The Benefit Fund will not reimburse for claims that are more than two years old." Also: "Eligible retirees with either the 1199SEIU Medicare Advantage Plan or secondary health coverage with the Benefit Fund also receive additional benefits through the Fund, including reimbursement for 50% of the standard Medicare Part B premium, Life Insurance and Burial Benefits." NBF Complete SPD (as of January 2021, still listed as current), pp. 155–156. https://www.1199SEIUBenefits.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/NBF-Complete-Summary-Plan-Description.pdf
5. **verified: claim form (rev. 01/24), covers retiree and spouse.** "Eligible retirees may submit a claim for 50 percent of the standard Medicare Part B premium for the retiree and spouse. Eligibility is based on years of service and age at retirement." "If this is your first time filing a claim ... you must include copies of your Medicare Health Insurance card and one of the following: a payment voucher, a Social Security Administration Award Letter or proof of Medicare Part B premium payment." "Reimbursement will only be made up to and including the month the claim is received." Return to "1199SEIU Benefit Funds, PO Box 2661, New York, NY 10108-2661 ... by fax to (646) 473-7089; or by email to RetireeHealth@1199Funds.org." https://www.1199seiubenefits.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Medicare-Part-B-Reimbursement-Claim.pdf
6. **verified: still live in 2026.** The NBF retirees page (footer "©2026 1199SEIU Funds") lists "50 percent Medicare Part B premium reimbursement, burial and life insurance directly from the National Benefit Fund" and says "you must be enrolled in both Medicare Part A (hospital) and Medicare Part B (medical)". https://www.1199seiubenefits.org/nbf-retirees-aetna/ ; also https://www.1199seiubenefits.org/?p=83424
7. **verified: eligibility.** Medicare-eligible retiree health requires "at least 10 Years of Pension Fund Credited Service under the 1199SEIU Health Care Employees Pension Fund". The retiree must "Leave covered active service with Wage Class I or Wage Class II Benefits: » At or after age 65; or » Between ages 60–64 ... » Between ages 62–64 with at least 20 Years ... » Before age 65 with a Disability Pension". The retiree must also "Be receiving a pension from the 1199SEIU Health Care Employees Pension Fund" and "Be enrolled in Medicare Part A and Part B." NBF SPD pp. 153–154 (URL as finding 4).
   - The current web overview gives the same tiers: age 65 + 10 years; Unreduced Early at 62.5 + 25 years; Reduced Early at 55 + 10 years ("Ages 55 to 59 get only the Prescription Plan at 65"); Disability at any age + 10 years. A footnote adds: "Members born before July 1, 1959, or commencing benefit payments prior to January 1, 2019, are eligible for retiree health benefits at age 62 with 20 years of service." https://1199seiubenefits.org/hce-pension/retiree-health-benefits
   - Timing rule: "If you leave covered employment and do not begin to receive your pension within one year ... you will not be eligible for supplemental retiree health benefits through the 1199SEIU National Benefit Fund." (same URL)
8. **verified: what the coverage is.** Wage Class I retirees get "Medicare Advantage Benefits • Hospital • Medical • Prescription drugs • Dental • Vision care • Hearing", plus "Medicare Part B 50% reimbursement • Life insurance (member only) • Burial (member only)". Wage Class II retirees get "Limited hospital and medical (secondary to Medicare) • Vision care • Hearing • Medicare Part B 50% reimbursement". Enrollment is required: "You are required to enroll in the 1199SEIU Medicare Advantage Program, if you are eligible, in order to receive Retiree Health Benefits". Retirees with a waiver, retirees living abroad or in U.S. territories, and Wage Class II retirees get Fund-sponsored Part D (EGWP) plus secondary coverage. NBF SPD pp. 153–156.
   - The carrier is Aetna: "1199SEIU Aetna Medicare Advantage Plan ... a large nationwide network". https://www.1199seiubenefits.org/nbf-retirees-aetna/ . A 2023 change: "expand the 1199SEIU Medicare Advantage Plan's service area to include the U.S. territories of Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands". https://www.1199seiubenefits.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/National-Benefit-Fund-Greater-New-York-SMM.pdf
9. **verified: spouse and survivor coverage.** "Your spouse is eligible if you are eligible, and if they were enrolled before you retired; your children are not eligible." For pensions starting on or after Oct 1, 1998, "benefits for your eligible spouse will continue for the remainder of their life." The Fund also reimburses Part D IRMAA: "you will be reimbursed for your Medicare Part D Income-related Monthly Adjustment Amount (IRMAA)." NBF SPD pp. 151–156.
10. **verified: NYC government retirees are an exception.** "If the City makes contributions to the Benefit Fund on your behalf, effective July 1, 2001, you are covered by the Fund only for the following supplemental retiree benefits: • Vision care; • Prescription drugs; and • Life insurance." So these retirees get no Part B reimbursement from the NBF. NBF SPD p. 152.
11. **inferred: the size of the saving.** The 2026 standard Part B premium is $202.90 (repo `tools/site/msp-ny.json`). The NBF pays 50% = **$101.45/mo = $1,217.40/yr per covered retiree or spouse**. Once MSP (QMB/SLMB/QI) pays the premium, the retiree's Social Security no longer deducts it, the retiree cannot show a premium paid, and the claim should end. Each 1,000 NBF retirees moved onto MSP saves about $1.22M a year, more where spouses are covered. Caveat: the payment depends on a claim being filed, so retirees who never claim produce no saving. We need the Fund's actual claim volume.

### C. Greater New York Benefit Fund (GNY), mainly nursing homes

12. **verified: eligibility is stricter and covers the retiree only.** "you need to retire with Eligibility Class I benefits and at least 25 years of Pension Fund Credited Service to be eligible for any retiree health benefits." "Supplemental retiree health benefits are available for retired members only. There is no coverage for spouses or dependents." The retiree must be "receiving a pension from the 1199SEIU Greater New York Pension Fund". GNY Retiree Health Overview (2024; English and Spanish). https://www.1199seiubenefits.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Greater-New-York-Retiree-Health-Overview.pdf
13. **verified: what the coverage is.** Retirees living in "New York City's five boroughs, Nassau, Suffolk or Westchester County" get the "1199SEIU Aetna Medicare Advantage Plan" (out-of-pocket maximum $3,400). Retirees outside that area must be in "the Fund's Medicare Part D Prescription Drug Program". For them the Fund pays "Your Medicare Part A first-day deductible" and coinsurance, plus Rx "up to $1,500 per calendar year", and "may include reimbursement for your Medicare Part D monthly premium based upon the Fund's allowance". (same URL)
14. **verified: Part B is required, but there is no retiree Part B reimbursement.** "you must enroll in Medicare Part A, Medicare Part B and ... either the 1199SEIU Aetna Medicare Advantage Plan or the Benefit Fund's Medicare Part D Prescription Drug Program in order to receive your supplemental Fund benefits." Neither the 2024 overview nor the GNY retirees web page mentions Part B premium reimbursement for retirees. https://www.1199seiubenefits.org/gny-retirees . The GNY SPD (June 2017) mentions 50% Part B only for working members where "the Fund is secondary to Medicare" (ESRD context): "The Fund will provide reimbursement for 50% of the standard Medicare Part B premium for months where the Fund is secondary to Medicare." https://www.1199SEIUBenefits.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/Greater-New-York-Benefit-Fund-SPD.pdf
    - **inferred**: GNY has **no retiree Part B saving**. GNY covered only 2,821 retirees in 2018 (finding 2).

### D. Home care: Home Care Benefit Fund (NBF for Home Care Employees) and Home Care Pension Fund

15. **verified: no retiree health.** "SECTION VI – RETIREE HEALTH BENEFITS. The Benefit Fund does not provide Retiree Health Benefits." 1199SEIU National Benefit Fund for Home Care Employees SPD, June 2025, p. 131. https://www.1199seiubenefits.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Home-Care-Benefit-Fund-Complete-SPD.pdf
16. **verified: the Part B reimbursement covers working members only.** For working members with ESRD after the coordination period: "The Fund will provide reimbursement for 50% of the standard Medicare Part B premium for months when the Fund is secondary to Medicare." Eligibility Class II working members can "enroll in a premium deduction to elect the Fund's health reimbursement benefit", which pays premiums and co-pays "up to $2,000 a year (or $4,000 a year for those over age 65)" while enrolled in "Medicare Parts A and B" or "Medicare Part C". (same SPD, pp. 40, 128)
    - **inferred**: older home care aides who are still working, especially Class II members using the reimbursement account for Medicare costs, are a small MSP angle. It is funded by the member's own "premium deduction", so it is unclear whether the fund would save anything.
17. **verified: the Home Care Pension SPD describes pension benefits only.** It covers "your pension benefits under the 1199SEIU Home Care Employees Pension Fund" and lists "Non-pension benefits, such as health insurance" only in the PBGC section. The SPD is dated December 2025; the phones are "(646) 473-8666. Outside New York City, please call (800) 575-7771." https://www.1199seiubenefits.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Home-Care-Pension-SPD.pdf
18. **verified (2018)**: the Home Care Benefit Fund had "––––" (none) under retirees, against 28,428 active members. https://www.1199seiubenefits.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/2018-Annual-Report.pdf
    - **inferred**: about 32k home care retirees (avg pension ≈ $75/mo) have **no 1199 retiree health**. They are most likely on Medicare alone or dual-eligible, and are the strongest MSP need group. Saving them money would not save any fund money.

### J. Bottom line for Mycelium

35. **(a) Is there a Part B saving?** **Yes at NBF (verified benefit, inferred saving):** 50% of the standard premium per eligible retiree and spouse, about $1,217 per person per year in 2026. **No at GNY, NBF Rochester, the Home Care funds or the Regional fund** (findings 14, 15, 19, 20).
36. **(b) Best pitch.**
   - **NBF:** a savings program that fits CAVIC's "mandated savings targets". Mycelium finds Part B claimants likely eligible for MSP, enrolls them with their consent, and the Fund's 50% Part B claim ends. A secondary point (inferred, to confirm with the Fund): MSP enrollees automatically get Extra Help, and in a group MA or EGWP with Part D, CMS's low-income subsidy can lower the plan's drug cost. Whether 1199 or Aetna keeps that saving depends on their contract. This matches the Funds' stated aim "to maximize available Medicare subsidies".
   - **GNY and out-of-area NBF retirees:** member benefit, plus a possible Part D premium saving where the Fund reimburses Part D premiums "based upon the Fund's allowance" (finding 13; inferred).
   - **Home care:** a pure member benefit. The likely buyer is the union (Armstrong came up through home care), the Retired Members Division or a state or philanthropic partner, not the funds.
37. **(c) Lowest-income retirees.** **Home Care Pension Fund retirees** (avg ≈ $75/mo, about 32k) have no 1199 retiree health (verified, finding 15). Regional/Buffalo retirees (≈ $409/mo) have none we could find. NBF retirees average ≈ $1,027/mo before Social Security, so many are probably under the NY QI limit. Per the repo's `msp-ny.json`, that is $2,474/mo single after the $20 disregard, about $2,494 gross (inferred; needs real income data).

## Conflicts and inconsistencies

- **Outside-NYC phone:** (800) 575-7771 (Part B claim form; Home Care Pension SPD; NBF overview) vs (800) 892-2557 (GNY overview; hce-pension retiree page). These may be different lines for Pension and for GNY Benefit.
- **Trustee rosters are out of date:** the NBF SPD (Jan 2021) and HCE Pension SPD (Dec 2023) list George Gresham as union President; Armstrong has been president since mid-2025 (finding 31). Also, the 2021 NBF roster lists 16 union and 18 employer names, while the Pension SPD says "equal numbers". Votes may be weighted, or the roster may count alternates.
- **Regional Pension Fund:** participants are "in excess of 12,000" (Jan 2024, search summary) vs "in excess of 13,000" (Jan 2025 page) vs 12,115 (2024 Form 5500). Address is Suite 100 vs Suite 125 (search summary vs About page). The differences are small.
- **Retiree totals differ:** "more than 133,000 retirees and their beneficiaries" (Funds site, pensions) vs "Some 94,000 1199SEIU retirees" (union site, likely only those active in the Retirees Division or a different count) vs 2024 Form 5500 sums (82,266 + 32,333 + 2,932 retirees, plus the GNY Pension Fund, not extracted).
- **Third-party claim-form summaries** (formspal, legalclarity) mention an SSA-1099 requirement. The Fund's own form asks for an SSA Award Letter (finding 5). Use the Fund's form.
