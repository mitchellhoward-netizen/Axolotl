# NY building-trades, nurses, Teamsters, entertainment, transit and DC 37 funds: Part B savings check

Researched 2026-10-07. All URLs were accessed on 2026-10-07 unless marked otherwise. Retiree counts and average monthly
pensions come from `pension_plans.csv` / `health_plans.csv` in this folder (Form 5500 screening numbers: avg_monthly_benefit = benefits paid / recipients / 12).

Labels: **verified** means a primary document says it. **secondary** means a third party, a search-engine snippet, or a union article says it. **inferred** means it is our reasoning and is not stated by any source.

## Summary (plain English)

1. Only two funds on this list were confirmed to pay retirees' Part B premiums out of fund money: **IUOE Local 94 (School Division)**, up to $7,000 a year per couple, and the **IATSE National Health & Welfare Fund** Retiree Health Benefit Plan, a flat $240 a quarter.
2. For these two, each retiree who gets MSP should lower the fund's reimbursement bill (inferred). The ceiling is the 2026 premium of $202.90 × 12 = $2,434.80 a year at Local 94, or $960 a year at IATSE.
3. Most building-trades funds here do **not** pay the Part B premium. They require retirees to enroll in Part B and pay it themselves. The fund then pays the Part B deductible and the 20% coinsurance (JIB/Local 3, Division 1181, Local 15, Local 94 Commercial), runs a group Medicare Advantage plan (Carpenters; Local 282 per a secondary source), or covers only prescription drugs (Sheet Metal 28).
4. Two funds (JIB/Local 3 HRA, Plumbers Local 1 HRA) let retirees use their **individual HRA balance** for Medicare/Part B premiums. That balance is already credited to the member, so MSP there mostly protects the member's own money rather than cutting fund costs (inferred).
5. The Teamsters (NYS Council H&H Premium Assistance and the UPS Retiree Health Fund) and the NYSNA "Bridge to Medicare" cover retirees **only until Medicare**. They have no Part B role.
6. DC 37 retirees get Part B reimbursed by the **City of New York** (Admin Code §12-126), not by the union fund. MSP for a DC 37 retiree would save the City money, not DC 37 (inferred).
7. The lowest-pension pools in this group are **JIB Employees Security Fund** retirees (2,874 retirees, about $283/mo) and **Division 1181 ATU** school-bus retirees (5,979 retirees, about $856/mo). Neither fund pays Part B, so MSP would be a member benefit, not a fund saving.
8. Entertainment funds are national, and no NY share of members was found. SAG-AFTRA gives seniors a fixed HRA ($1,140/yr) and Equity-League offers self-pay Medicare coverage. AFM-EPF is pension-only.
9. **Not found** (no public SPD or the documents sit behind a login): Mason Tenders, Excavators 731, Local 282 (beyond a Medicare Advantage notice), Iron Workers 40/361/417, Steamfitters 638, Painting Industry Insurance Fund, Bricklayers, IUOE 137, Upstate NY Engineers, Teamsters Local 445, and the NYSNA Retiree Medical Plan details.
10. Pilot recommendation: **IUOE Local 94 School Division = A**. IATSE, Division 1181 and JIB (ESF) = B. Everything else is C until the "not found" funds are checked by phone.

## Numbered findings

### IUOE Local 94 (School and Commercial divisions)

1. **verified.** School Medicare retirees get only premium reimbursement plus a death benefit. "The School Medicare Retiree Plan medical benefits are exclusively limited to the Death Benefit and Medicare Related Premium reimbursements (benefit formerly known as the "Reimbursement of Medicare Part B and Part D Premiums")." https://www.local94.com/affiliated-funds/health-and-benefit-trust-fund/school-retirees.aspx (accessed 2026-10-07)
2. **verified.** "Retirees will be eligible to receive a reimbursement of an annual calendar maximum of $7,000 effective 1/1/2019 for Medicare Related Premiums for the annual calendar premiums paid. The benefit includes the eligible retiree's spouse." Same URL. Also: "The School Medicare Retiree Plan does not require a premium payment."
3. **verified.** Eligibility: "Have fifteen (15) years of Total Credited Service (as defined in the Central Pension Plan), Be receiving a pension under the Central Pension Plan, and Must have continuous coverage under the Fund for the five (5) years immediately preceding their respective retirement date". https://www.local94.com/media/170684/SchoolSMMRetireeEligibilityBeneficiaryDepdntChildFinalKF.pdf (accessed 2026-10-07)
4. **verified.** The claim form (Revised 1/2026) names "WILLIAM FARANDA Executive Director" and "DEREK J. DAVIS Administrator", lists the address "331-337 West 44th Street New York, NY 10036", and accepts "Form SSA-1099 Social Security Benefit Statement" as proof of premiums paid. https://www.local94.com/media/212603/8_11_26_final_medicare_b_d_premium_reimbursement_form.pdf (accessed 2026-10-07)
5. **verified.** Commercial Division: "The Medicare Retiree Plan—is currently $475 for individual or $565 for family coverage month. The Fund will reimburse you for the annual deductible and the 20% co-insurance amounts applicable to expenses approved under Medicare Part "B"." https://www.local94.com/affiliated-funds/health-and-benefit-trust-fund/commercial-retirees.aspx (accessed 2026-10-07)
6. **inferred.** The reimbursement is for premiums "paid". If MSP pays a retiree's Part B, that retiree has no Part B premium to claim, so the fund's outlay falls by up to $2,434.80 per person per year (2026). The rest of the $7,000 cap is still available for Part D, Medigap or Medicare Advantage premiums, so the actual saving depends on how much of the cap each couple already uses. The number of School Division Medicare retirees was **not found**. The health fund has 6,631 participants (5500), and the Central Pension Fund is national, so it is not in our NY pension list.
