# SSDI for fund members: incidence, how funds treat disabled members, competitors

Research for a possible second Mycelium product: helping disabled members of multiemployer (Taft-Hartley) funds apply for SSDI, paid by the fund out of what it saves. Accessed 2026-10-07 unless noted. Labels:
**verified** means read in the primary source. **secondary** means a vendor, a search summary or a third party. **inferred** means our own arithmetic, shown in the Arithmetic section.

## Summary in plain English

1. **Cases per 10,000 members per year:** expect roughly **30–55 new SSDI awards per 10,000 working-age members**. About 45 is a reasonable midpoint for a fund whose members skew older. About **95 per 10,000 apply** each year (inferred, A1–A3).
2. **Age matters most.** The rate is about 1 per 1,000 in the 20s and **13–17 per 1,000 at ages 55–64**. Most awards go to people aged 50 and over: 55.4% of 2024 awards were at ages 55–FRA (28.0 + 12.6 + 14.8) (full retirement age), and 16.2% were at ages 50–54 (F2, F3).
3. **Union trades are higher-risk.** Construction trades, transportation, machine operators and food preparation workers have **more than double** the hazard of DI receipt compared with managers (F6). No SSA table gives award rates by industry.
4. **Most applications are denied at first.** Final award rates are about 31–34% of disabled-worker applications (2019–2021 cohorts). Applications run about 3× awards. **26% of potentially eligible work-disabled adults in one survey had never applied** (secondary, F7).
5. **How funds treat a disabled member:** a short-term benefit comes first (NY DBL at $170/week for 26 weeks, CA SDI for up to 52 weeks, or a fund's own weekly benefit). Next comes a **fund-paid health extension of about 26 weeks to 30 months** (hour-bank credits or a "disability extension"). After that, coverage ends or COBRA applies (F9–F14).
6. **Many NY, CA and IL funds use the SSDI award as the trigger** for their own benefits. Examples are disability pensions (1199SEIU, Local 3 JIBEI, SoCal IBEW-NECA, Chicago Painters), long-term disability beyond 12 months (32BJ), and lifetime or retiree medical (1199, JIBEI). So many funds already require members to obtain an SSDI award (F10–F16).
7. **How the fund saves on health costs:** under Medicare Secondary Payer rules, a disabled member who receives SSDI and is not in "current employment status" (retiree, COBRA, or more than 6 months on employer disability) has **Medicare as primary payer**. Medicare starts after 24 months of SSDI, and SSDI itself has a 5-month wait (F17–F19). 32BJ's SPD already makes Medicare primary "after six months" on STD (short-term disability) or workers' compensation (F11).
8. **Watch out on the pension side:** for pension funds an SSDI award usually *creates* a cost, because it unlocks a disability pension. The saving lands on the health fund (and on any LTD or weekly-disability plan that offsets SSDI), not the pension fund (F10, F13–F16; inferred).
9. **Competitors:** SSDC Services sells "Medicare Maximization" to "employers & trusts" and claims "$15,000 per-member per-year" savings. Allsup sells "Medicare Coordination" to self-insured employers. Genex/Enlyte and Brown & Brown (Advocator) sell to LTD carriers and employers. All of these claims are vendor claims (F21–F26). **No vendor we found markets to Taft-Hartley funds by name.**
10. **How competitors charge:** the usual model is a contingency fee from the claimant's back pay, capped by SSA at **25% / $9,200**. SSA also lets a *third-party entity* (which could be a fund) pay the representative from its own funds with no fee authorization, provided the claimant owes nothing (F27, F28). None of the vendors publish employer-side prices. We found no IFEBP or other survey data on how many funds already offer SSDI help (F29).

## Arithmetic (inferred)

- **A1. Ultimate rates applied to an age mix.** Average the men's and women's rates from F2 for each band:
  - 20–24: 1.05; 25–29: 1.35; 30–34: 1.8; 35–39: 2.5; 40–44: 3.5; 45–49: 4.9; 50–54: 8.1; 55–59: 13.55; 60–64: 16.1.
  - **Even age mix (20–64):** the average is 5.87 per 1,000, so **≈59 awards per 10,000 per year**.
  - **Older-skewed mix** (weights 4/8/10/11/12/13/15/15/12%): 6.84 per 1,000, so **≈68 per 10,000**.
  - Adjusted to recent experience (3.6 ÷ 4.6 = 0.78): **≈46 (even mix) to ≈54 (older mix) per 10,000**.
- **A2. Population basis.**
  - 2025 awards ÷ US population aged 18–64: 665,749 ÷ 205,798,350 = **32 per 10,000**. For 2024: 594,749 ÷ 205.8M = 29.
  - This understates the rate for insured workers in heavy trades. It also overstates it for actively working groups, because the rate includes people who were already out of work.
- **A3. Applications.** 1,930,414 ÷ 205.8M = **94 per 10,000 per year**. Initial state-agency (DDS) receipts are 61 per 10,000.
- **A4. Stock already on SSDI.** At 30.7 per 1,000 insured (F2), there are **≈307 per 10,000 insured**. Using population, 7.23M ÷ 205.8M = 351 per 10,000. These are people already awarded, not a pipeline.
- **A5. Benefit size.** The average 2024 award is $1,760.34/month, or about **$21,100 per year in SSDI income per awardee**.
- **A6. Typical fund.** 5,000,000 ÷ 1,436 = **≈3,500 participants**, a floor because IFEBP says "more than." That implies about **11–24 new awards per year** at 32–68 per 10,000. For 1199SEIU or 32BJ-scale funds, with roughly 100k+ members (not verified here), it would be hundreds per year.
- **A7. Rough "not yet awarded" pipeline.** Applications are about 3× awards, and about 11% of the 2023 application cohort was still pending in mid-2024 (F5b). If 26% of potentially eligible people never apply (F7, weak), then for every ~3 awards a year, about 1 eligible member never files. This is a low-confidence order of magnitude.
- **A8. Timeline against fund extensions.**
  - SSDI is paid from month 6 after onset, and Medicare starts after 24 more months, so about **month 30 after onset**.
  - 32BJ's extension lasts 30 months (F11). Without a prompt SSDI filing, Medicare is not in place when the extension ends. But the fund becomes secondary only once the member is on SSDI *and* has Medicare (F17), so delays in filing push the fund's primary exposure further out.

## Open questions and conflicts

1. **Ultimate vs. actual incidence.** The 4.6 per 1,000 rate is a long-range assumption. Actual 2016–2025 incidence averaged 3.6, and 2022 was an all-time low (F1). We used a 0.78 scaling factor, which is approximate because the adjusted rates are standardized to the 2000 population. We could not get year-by-year actual crude rates by age; the chart values were not extractable.
2. **Industry rates.** No SSA table gives awards by industry. The NIOSH figure (F6) is a hazard ratio for people aged 51+ in HRS data, not a rate per insured worker. Building services and healthcare are not broken out in the abstract.
3. **Who saves, the health fund or the pension fund?** For health funds an SSDI award means savings from Medicare-primary status (F11, F17). For pension funds it means a cost, because it unlocks unreduced disability pensions (F10, F13–F16). SoCal IBEW-NECA has stopped paying disability pensions for new pensions under its rehabilitation plan (F13). A "paid from what the fund saves" model has to name which fund saves.
4. **Medicare start date when the award comes late.** We did not verify whether Medicare entitlement is retroactive when SSDI is awarded on appeal. One secondary source says "some beneficiaries are eligible for Medicare soon after they win their appeal," but we did not open it. Verify in SSA POMS HI 00801 before building savings models.
5. **The $15,000 per-member saving** (SSDC) and the 96–97% award rates (SSDC, Allsup, Genex) are vendor claims with no published method. SSA's own final award rate for disabled-worker applications is about 31–32% (F5b). Vendors' rates are conditioned on the cases they accept or that complete the process.
6. **Retiree health vs. active plans.** 74% of multiemployer health plans cover retirees (F8). The Medicare-primary saving is largest where a disabled member keeps fund coverage after leaving work (disability pensioners, retiree plans, long extensions). It is small where coverage ends at 6–30 months anyway.
7. **Not verified:**
   - the size of 1199SEIU or 32BJ membership
   - whether the 32BJ Pension Fund's "Total Disability Benefit" requires SSDI (court opinion only: Lardo v. 32BJ Pension Fund, S.D.N.Y. 2021)
