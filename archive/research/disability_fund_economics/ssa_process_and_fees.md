# SSA process, fees and outcomes: what they mean for a fund-paid SSDI service

Research date: 2026-10-07. Every finding below has a source URL, an exact short quote, and a label:
**verified** (read in the primary source), **secondary** (a non-primary source), or **inferred** (my
reasoning from verified material). Some ssa.gov pages returned HTTP 403 to this environment. For those I
used web.archive.org copies captured in September and October 2026; the capture URL is given each time.
The eCFR text comes from the eCFR versioner API for 2026-09-01. Federal Register notices come from govinfo.gov.

## Summary in plain English (10 lines)

1. The fund can pay. SSA does not need to approve the fee when an entity pays from its own money and the member owes nothing, directly or indirectly. The representative must also file a written waiver of any fee from the member (20 CFR 404.1720(e), 416.1520(e)).
2. With that waiver, SSA's fee cap does not apply to what the fund pays. The cap is the lesser of 25% of back pay or $9,200, in effect since Nov 30, 2024 and still current. No 2025 or 2026 notice raises it. Mycelium also cannot take any cut of the member's back pay.
3. Paid help counts even without an appointment. SSA's manual says fee rules apply even if the helper "was never recognized ... as a claimant's representative". A paid helper who prepares applications should be treated as subject to SSA's fee and conduct rules.
4. Only individuals can be appointed, not companies. Mycelium staff would each register and be appointed by name. Mycelium as an entity can only register and receive assigned fees, and only for SSA-authorized fees paid out of back pay. That does not fit the fund-pays model.
5. Non-attorneys may represent. Direct payment from SSA to a non-attorney (EDPNA) requires a degree or 4 years' experience, a background check, a passed exam, malpractice insurance and continuing education. A fund-paid model does not need EDPNA status.
6. The member must sign their own application unless they are incapable (20 CFR 404.612). Helpers can prepare it but cannot sign for a competent adult.
7. Average initial decision time was 188 days in August 2026. Reconsideration was 212 days and hearing 276 days. Each appeal has a 60-day filing window, so a case that goes to hearing can take roughly 2+ years from filing (inferred).
8. Allowance rates for SSDI-only claims at state agencies (DDS) were 38.5% initial and 18.5% reconsideration in FY2025, and 38.1% and 17.3% in FY2026 through July. At ALJ hearings, 57.7% of decisions in FY2026 to date were awards. Only about 29–32% of all disabled-worker applications ever end in an award.
9. Back pay goes back at most 12 months before the filing month, after a 5-month waiting period. Medicare starts after 24 months of entitlement, so early filing matters. Every month of delay can cost a month of back pay and a month of Medicare.
10. The main legal risks: (a) any arrangement where the member ends up paying, (b) misleading or SSA-branded solicitation (42 U.S.C. 1320b-10), and (c) a possible "free from SSA" disclosure for fee-based help. No primary-source age-specific allowance rate was found.

## Numbered findings

### A. Who may represent, appointment, entities

**1. Who may represent (attorneys and non-attorneys).** *Verified.*
> "You may appoint any person who is not an attorney to be your representative in dealings with us if the person— (1) Is capable of giving valuable help to you in connection with your claim; (2) Is not disqualified or suspended ...; (3) Is not prohibited by any law ...; and (4) Is generally known to have a good character and reputation." ... "(c) Your representative(s) must be registered with us in the manner we prescribe."

20 CFR 404.1705(b)–(c). Source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-404/subpart-R/section-404.1705 (text via eCFR API `https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-09-01/title-20.xml?part=404&subpart=R`). Accessed 2026-10-07. The SSI parallel is 20 CFR 416.1505.

**2. Only individuals can be appointed; companies cannot.** *Verified.*
> "We will only recognize individuals as appointed representatives. We do not recognize organizations, firms, partnerships, or other entities as representatives."

POMS GN 03910.020B, https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0203910020. Accessed 2026-10-07. The 2024 final rule confirms this:
> "we chose to maintain our rule that only individuals, not entities, may be appointed as representatives."

89 FR 67542 (Aug 21, 2024), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-21/html/2024-18497.htm. Effective Sept 20, 2024. Phase 2 (appointment, fees, entity payment) was implemented Dec 9, 2024.

**3. Entities can receive fees only by assignment, not by appointment.** *Verified.*
> "An entity is eligible for direct payment of an authorized fee if the entity: (a) Has an Employer Identification Number; (b) Has registered with us ...; (d) Designates and maintains an employee who is registered as a representative ... as a point of contact ...; (e) Accepts payment via electronic funds transfer; and (f) Conforms to our rules."

20 CFR 404.1735. Same eCFR source as #1.

> "A representative may assign direct payment of the fee we authorize to an eligible entity if the representative: (i) Is eligible for direct payment; (ii) Has not waived the fee or direct payment; ..."

20 CFR 404.1730(e)(1).

*Inferred:* Assignment only works for SSA-authorized fees paid out of the member's past-due benefits. The fund-pays route requires waiving the fee from the claimant, and 404.1730(e)(1)(ii) bars assignment once a fee is waived. So under the fund-pays model, Mycelium is paid by the fund under contract, not by SSA.

**4. What a non-attorney needs for SSA direct payment (EDPNA).** *Verified.*
> "A non-attorney representative is eligible to receive direct payment of his or her fee out of your past-due benefits if he or she: (1) Completes and submits to us an application ...; (2) Pays the application fee ...; (3) Demonstrates that he or she possesses: (i) A bachelor's degree ...; or (ii) At least four years of relevant professional experience and either a high school diploma or a General Educational Development certificate; (4) Passes our criminal background investigation ...; (5) Takes and passes a written examination we administer; (6) Provides proof of and maintains continuous liability insurance coverage ...; and (7) Completes ... all continuing education courses that we prescribe."

20 CFR 404.1717(a). Same eCFR source. Also: "An individual who is a licensed attorney ... may not be an eligible non-attorney."

*Inferred:* EDPNA matters only if Mycelium staff, or partner representatives handling appeals, will be paid from back pay. It is not needed for the fund-pays initial-claim model.

**5. Appointment mechanics: Form SSA-1696, registration first.** *Verified.*
> "Before a representative may be appointed, they must register with us." ... "Effective December 9, 2024, representatives must use the current version of Form SSA-1696"

POMS GN 03910.040 (effective 10/02/2026, TN 52), https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0203910040. Accessed 2026-10-07.

**6. A representative cannot sign the member's application.** *Verified.*
> "A representative may not sign an application on behalf of a claimant for rights or benefits under title II of the Act unless authorized to do so under § 404.612."

20 CFR 404.1710(b). Under 404.612(a):
> "A claimant who is 18 years old or over, mentally competent, and physically able to do so, must sign his or her own application."

Source: eCFR API, section 404.612. Accessed 2026-10-07.

### B. Fees: when SSA must authorize and when it need not

**7. Default rule: SSA must authorize any fee.** *Verified.*
> "Subject to paragraph (e) of this section, a representative must not charge or receive any fee unless we have authorized it, and a representative must not charge or receive any fee that is more than the amount we authorize."

20 CFR 404.1720(b)(3). Same eCFR source.

**8. The exception for an entity or third-party payer (the fund-pays route).** *Verified.*
> "(e) When we do not need to authorize a fee. We do not need to authorize a fee when: (1) An entity or a Federal, State, county, or city government agency pays from its funds the representative fees and expenses and both of the following conditions apply: (i) You and your auxiliary beneficiaries, if any, are not liable to pay a fee or any expenses, or any part thereof, directly or indirectly, to the representative or someone else; and (ii) The representative submits to us a writing in the form and manner that we prescribe waiving the right to charge and collect a fee and any expenses from you and your auxiliary beneficiaries, if any, directly or indirectly, in whole or in part"

20 CFR 404.1720(e)(1). The SSI version is identical in substance at 20 CFR 416.1520(e)(1): "You are not liable to pay a fee or any expenses ... directly or indirectly". Same eCFR sources.

"Entity" is defined broadly in 404.1703:
> "Entity means any business, firm, or other association, including but not limited to partnerships, corporations, for-profit organizations, and not-for-profit organizations."

*Inferred:* A Taft-Hartley fund or its board of trustees fits "business, firm, or other association".

**9. How SSA's manual applies the third-party rule.** *Verified.*
> "when a third-party entity pays for the representative's services, the risk of claimants' liability for unreasonable fees is eliminated. Therefore, when a third-party entity pays the representative's fees and certain conditions are met, we do not need to authorize the representative's fee."

It lists three conditions:
> "The claimant and any auxiliary beneficiaries are free of direct or indirect financial liability ...; A third-party entity, or a government agency from its own funds, pays the fee ...; The representative submits to SSA a form SSA-1696-U4 (or a written statement) waiving the right to charge and collect a fee and expenses from the claimant"

POMS GN 03920.010B (effective 09/30/2011), https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0203920010. Accessed 2026-10-07.

**10. The current waiver mechanism is a checkbox on the SSA-1696, plus a claimant notice.** *Verified.*
> "Waives the right to receive a fee from the claimant, any auxiliary beneficiaries, or any other individual, but will collect a fee from a third-party entity."

> "If a representative wishes to collect a fee from a third-party entity, they must waive their right to charge and collect a fee under our fee authorization process. This will relieve the claimant ... of any obligation to pay for the representative's services in their case, and we will not authorize a fee to the representative."

SSA's notice to the claimant must say:
> "Your representative has informed us that a third-party entity is paying your representative's fee. ... If you or any other individual is paying any part of your representative's fee, we must first authorize that fee before your representative charges or collects it."

POMS GN 03920.020C.3 and F.3 (effective 12/12/2024), https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0203920020. Accessed 2026-10-07.

**11. Fee rules reach paid helpers who were never appointed.** *Verified.*
> "A representative, attorney or non-attorney, must obtain SSA's authorization to charge and collect a fee for services provided in proceedings before SSA irrespective of whether ...: ... the attorney/non-attorney was ever recognized by SSA as a claimant's representative, or the individual did not deal directly with or actually contact SSA; or the fee is charged to or collected from the claimant or a third party (e.g., an insurance company), unless the requirements in GN 03920.010B. are met."

Proceedings include "an initial ... determination ... by a field office". POMS GN 03920.005B–C, https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0203920005. Accessed 2026-10-07.

Related regulatory definitions in 20 CFR 404.1703:
> "Representational services means services performed for a claimant in connection with any claim the claimant has before us, any asserted right the claimant may have for an initial or reconsidered determination"

> For the conduct rules, "Representative also includes an individual who provides representational services"

**12. Unpaid general help does not need an appointment.** *Verified.*
> "Family members, friends, or other individuals do not need to be appointed or registered as a representative when they provide only general assistance, such as interpreting, filing forms, or transporting claimants to and from our offices."

POMS GN 03910.040A (effective 10/02/2026), https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0203910040.

*Inferred (see Conflicts):* This carve-out is framed around family and friends. Together with #11, the safe reading is that a paid company helping with initial applications is providing "representational services". Its staff should be registered and appointed, and file the third-party waiver, so that 404.1720(e) clearly applies.

**13. Statutory basis and criminal penalty for excess fees.** *Verified.*
> "The Commissioner of Social Security may, by rule and regulation, prescribe the maximum fees which may be charged for services performed in connection with any claim before the Commissioner of Social Security under this subchapter, and any agreement in violation of such rules and regulations shall be void."

> "[A]ny person who shall ... knowingly charge or collect directly or indirectly any fee in excess of the maximum fee, or make any agreement directly or indirectly to charge or collect any fee in excess of the maximum fee ... shall be deemed guilty of a misdemeanor and, upon conviction thereof, shall for each offense be punished by a fine not exceeding $500 or by imprisonment not exceeding one year, or both."

42 U.S.C. 406(a)(1) and (a)(5), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/406. Accessed 2026-10-07.

**14. The fee-agreement cap is $9,200, effective Nov 30, 2024, and still current.** *Verified.*
> "We are increasing the maximum dollar amount limit for fee agreements approved under the Social Security Act (the Act) to $9,200. Effective November 30, 2024, we may approve fee agreements up to the new dollar limit"

89 FR 40523 (May 10, 2024), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-05-10/html/2024-10248.htm.

> "With the publication of this Notice the $9,200 fee cap will remain in effect, and we will only publish a notice in the Federal Register when increasing the fee cap"

90 FR 19241 (May 6, 2025), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-05-06/html/2025-07813.htm. The statute defines the cap as the lesser of "25 percent of the total amount of such past-due benefits" or the dollar amount, originally $4,000 (42 U.S.C. 406(a)(2)(A)).

*Verified (no later increase):* A federalregister.gov API search on 2026-10-07 found no notice after 2025-05-06. The query was SSA documents matching "fee agreement" or "Maximum Dollar Limit" since 2025-01-01. API URL: `https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents.json?conditions[term]="Maximum Dollar Limit"&conditions[agencies][]=social-security-administration`.

**15. Assessment on fees SSA pays directly: 6.3%, capped at $123, for 2026.** *Verified.*
> "We are announcing the assessment percentage rate under the Social Security Act (Act) is 6.3 percent for 2026." ... "The maximum dollar limit for the assessment currently is $123"

90 FR 57516 (Dec 11, 2025), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-12-11/html/2025-22534.htm. This applies only when SSA pays from back pay, which matters for partner appeal representatives, not for the fund-pays route.

**16. The member cannot be charged indirectly; conduct rules apply to "any source".** *Verified.*
> A representative must not "Knowingly charge, collect or retain, or make any arrangement to charge, collect or retain, from any source, directly or indirectly, any fee for representational services in violation of applicable law or regulation. This prohibition includes soliciting any gift or any other item of value, other than what is authorized by law."

Also prohibited:
> "In any manner or by any means threaten, coerce, intimidate, deceive or knowingly mislead a claimant, or prospective claimant or beneficiary ... This prohibition includes misleading a claimant, or prospective claimant or beneficiary, about the representative's services and qualifications."

20 CFR 404.1740(c)(1)–(2). Same eCFR source.

Affirmative duties include supervising staff:
> "Ensure that all of the representative's employees, assistants, partners, contractors, or any person assisting the representative ... comply with these rules"

20 CFR 404.1740(b)(10). Representatives must also disclose in writing if their employee drafted a medical opinion or they referred the claimant to the opinion source (404.1740(b)(5)).

*Inferred:* Two fund-side features could break "not liable ... directly or indirectly": (a) the fund deducting Mycelium's fee from the member's plan benefits, or (b) requiring the member to repay the fund from SSDI back pay.

### C. Solicitation, use of SSA names, "free from SSA" notice

**17. No misleading use of SSA or Medicare names or emblems in solicitations.** *Verified.*
> "No person may use, in connection with any item constituting an advertisement, solicitation, circular, ... (including any Internet or other electronic communication) ... the words 'Social Security', ... 'Medicare', ... the letters 'SSA', ... in a manner which ... reasonably could be interpreted or construed as conveying, the false impression that such item is approved, endorsed, or authorized by the Social Security Administration"

A disclaimer does not cure a violation: the determination "shall be made without regard to any inclusion in such item ... of a disclaimer of affiliation". Civil money penalty "not to exceed— (1) except as provided in paragraph (2), $5,000".

42 U.S.C. 1320b-10(a)(1), (a)(3), (b), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/1320b-10. Accessed 2026-10-07.

**18. Fee-based offers must disclose that the help is free from SSA, with an exemption for claimant representatives.** *Verified.*
> "No person shall offer, for a fee, to assist an individual to obtain a product or service that the person knows or should know is provided free of charge by the Social Security Administration unless, at the time the offer is made, the person provides ... a notice that— (i) explains that the product or service is available free of charge from the Social Security Administration"

> "(B) Subparagraph (A) shall not apply to any offer— (i) to serve as a claimant representative in connection with a claim arising under subchapter II, subchapter VIII, or subchapter XVI"

42 U.S.C. 1320b-10(a)(4). Same source. The statutory note says the amendment applies "to offers of assistance made after the sixth month ending after the Commissioner ... promulgates final regulations prescribing the standards applicable to the notice". See Open questions.

**19. No SSA rule found that bars using plan-member lists or paying members to apply.** *Inferred, from absence.* I read all of 20 CFR 404.1700–404.1799 and 42 U.S.C. 406 and 1320b-10. None of them prohibits soliciting claimants, using an employer's or fund's member list, or paying referral fees. The limits that do exist are no deception or misleading statements (404.1740(c)(1); 406(a)(5)), no fee from the member (404.1720), and no SSA-endorsement impression (1320b-10). Payment or gifts to SSA staff, adjudicators or witnesses are barred (404.1740(c)(6)). No rule was found on payments to claimants. HIPAA marketing rules, ERISA fiduciary rules and state bar rules may still apply; they are outside this file.

### F. Back pay, waiting period, Medicare

**27. Five-month waiting period, which can start no earlier than 17 months before the application.** *Verified.*
> "You have been disabled for 5 full consecutive months or no waiting period is required. The 5-month waiting period begins with a month in which you were both insured for disability and disabled. Your waiting period can begin no earlier than the 17th month before the month you apply"

There are exceptions for a prior entitlement within 5 years and for ALS. 20 CFR 404.315(a)(4), eCFR API (2026-09-01 version), accessed 2026-10-07.

**28. Retroactivity is at most 12 months before the filing month.** *Verified.*
> "If you file an application for disability benefits ... after the first month you could have been entitled to them, you may receive benefits for up to 12 months immediately before the month in which your application is filed."

20 CFR 404.621(a)(1). The filing date can be protected by an earlier written statement of intent to claim, if an application follows within 6 months of SSA's notice (20 CFR 404.630(c)).

**29. Medicare starts after 24 months of entitlement.** *Verified.*
> An individual under 65 who "is entitled to, and has for 24 calendar months been entitled to, (i) disability insurance benefits under section 423 of this title" is entitled to hospital insurance.

42 U.S.C. 426(b), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/426. Accessed 2026-10-07.

*Inferred:* Entitlement can start up to 12 months before the filing month (#28). So the earliest Medicare month can be about 12 months after the filing month if onset is old enough. It is about 29 months after onset when the waiting period is fully served before entitlement: 5 waiting months plus 24 entitlement months.

Because decisions take 6–27 months (#20–21), Medicare entitlement is often already reached, or close, when the award arrives. That makes the filing date, not the decision date, the main driver for the fund. Each month of delay in filing a member whose onset is more than 17 months back loses one month of cash benefits permanently and pushes Medicare back one month.

**30. Fee mechanics for partner appeal representatives (back-pay model).** *Verified.* SSA pays an attorney or EDPNA "the lesser of" "Twenty-five percent of the total of the past-due benefits" or "The amount of the fee that we set", minus the assessment (20 CFR 404.1730(b)). To get direct payment, the representative should file within 60 days of the favorable notice (404.1730(c)).

*Inferred:* If Mycelium refers denied claims to partner representatives who are paid from back pay, those cases fall under the regular authorization and fee-cap regime. If Mycelium staff stay appointed on the same case under a third-party waiver, both arrangements must be shown on the SSA-1696. GN 03910.040 (multiple-representative example) says that "if either representative waives their fee, the other's original fee agreement will remain valid". So in principle, one waived (fund-paid) representative and one fee-agreement representative can be on the same case.

## Open questions / conflicts

1. **Paid non-appointed helpers vs. the "general assistance" carve-out.** GN 03910.040 says "other individuals" giving "general assistance, such as ... filing forms" need not be appointed. GN 03920.005B says fee authorization applies even if the helper "was never recognized by SSA as a claimant's representative". The 404.1720(e) exception requires "the representative" to file a waiver, which is hard to do without being registered and appointed. Conservative path: register and appoint each Mycelium staffer who works a claim, and check the third-party-entity waiver box. This needs counsel or an SSA Office of General Counsel view.
2. **Waiver form.** GN 03920.010 (2011) refers to "form SSA-1696-U4", while GN 03920.020 (Dec 2024) uses a checkbox on the current SSA-1696. The 2024 manual is newer, so assume the SSA-1696 checkbox. Confirm with SSA.
3. **Is 42 U.S.C. 1320b-10(a)(4) in force?** The statutory note makes it effective only six months after SSA issues final notice-content regulations. I found no such final rule (the search was not exhaustive). If it is in force, its exemption for offers "to serve as a claimant representative" likely covers appointed staff. Whether an offer made to a fund, rather than "to an individual", counts is untested.
4. **"Indirect" member liability through plan design.** No SSA ruling was found on whether a fund recouping costs from the member (benefit offsets, reimbursement from back pay, higher contributions) breaks 404.1720(e)(1)(i). Contracts should say explicitly that the member owes nothing.
5. **Age-specific allowance rates (50–64).** No current primary source found. Possible routes: older ASR editions with tables by age, SSA Disability Research File extracts, or a FOIA or data request. Do not use the law-firm figures.
6. **FY2025 annual averages.** The secondary claim of 226 days initial and 284 days hearing in FY2025 is unverified. SSA's archived chart gives monthly values of 209 days initial and 278 days hearing for Sep 2025. The FY2025 Annual Performance Report could not be retrieved; ssa.gov returned 403.
7. **ALJ figures cover all programs.** The 57.7% figure is SSDI+SSI combined and includes non-disability hearings, if any. The ASR cohort data (#25) shows SSDI-only hearing allowance runs about 17 points above concurrent claims (60.2% vs 43.0%, 2019 cohort). So for fund members, who are mostly SSDI-only, hearing outcomes are likely above the blended rate (inferred).
8. **Processing-time metric.** SSA's charts show monthly averages for cases cleared in that month, not times for a cohort of applications. Real elapsed time for a given filing can differ, especially while pending counts rise (initial pending rose from Feb to Aug 2026).
9. **Outside SSA's rules (not researched here).** HIPAA marketing and use of member data by a covered-entity health fund, ERISA fiduciary rules on using plan assets to pay for SSDI help, and state unauthorized-practice-of-law or bar solicitation rules for attorney partners.
