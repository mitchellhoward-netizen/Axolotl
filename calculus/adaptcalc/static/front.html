§1

How it works

  1. Tell us your child’s first name and grade.That’s the whole setup. Their book starts writing itself straight away, beginning with a few short pages that find exactly where they are.
  2. Print the pages. Your child works right on them.Paper and pencil, the way people have always learned mathematics: a real textbook’s explanations and worked examples, then practice made for your child.
  3. When they’re done, point your phone at the code on the page.No app, no login, nothing to type. Take a photo of each page and every line of the work is checked in about a minute.
  4. The book writes back, in the margins.The checked pages come back into your child’s book with notes beside their own handwriting, and the next pages arrive in your email, made from what the work showed.
§2

Notes in the margin, not a chatbot

When your child is stuck, they can ask about a line of their own work. The answer is written like a note in the margin of a good textbook: it points to the step and the worked example that explains it, and it leaves the last step to your child unless they ask outright for the answer.

Before a note is shown, every equation in it is checked, its notation is checked against what the book has taught so far, and it is tested for giving the answer away or bringing in ideas the book hasn’t covered. If a note fails, your child sees the book’s own worked example instead.

§3

Why it works

The design uses the practices with the strongest evidence in the research on how people learn mathematics.

Start from what the student knows. Gaps come from missing earlier skills, not from ability. Koedinger et al., PNAS 2023 (1.3 million practice attempts)
Feedback on every step. Step-by-step feedback nearly matches a human tutor; answer-only feedback does far less. VanLehn, Educational Psychologist 2011
Worked examples, then practice. The first practice problem of a new skill has its first steps done; the student finishes it. Sweller; Renkl, fading worked examples
Mixed review. Earlier skills are mixed into practice. In a randomized trial, mixed practice raised test scores from 38% to 61%. Rohrer et al., Journal of Educational Psychology 2020
Learn before moving on. New material waits until the skills under it hold. Mastery learning, Kulik, Kulik & Bangert-Drowns 1990
AI that guides, never answers. Unrestricted AI made students worse on exams; guided help did not. Bastani et al., PNAS 2025
§4

For parents

  • You hold the account. Children don’t sign up or have email, and there is no open chat: they can only ask about a line of their own work, and every answer is checked before they see it.
  • A progress report you can print: what is learned, what is next, and which mistakes keep coming back.
  • Every packet comes with an answer key and worked solutions.
  • Your data is yours: download it or delete it from your account page at any time. Read the privacy notice.
