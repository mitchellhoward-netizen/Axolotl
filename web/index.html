Being entitled to something doesn't mean you get it.

A parent can know their child is supposed to receive support and still be left with the hard part: who's responsible, which process applies, what paperwork is missing, what got skipped, and what happens when nobody answers.

Axolotl figures out which rule applies, then does what it actually takes to get the support in place.

Staying at a cousin's house counts

Under McKinney-Vento, that student keeps their school and gets a ride to it. Axolotl makes the claim and chases the route.

An evaluation has a clock on it

Ask under IDEA and the district has a legal deadline. Axolotl puts the request in writing and tracks the date.

An interpreter is not a favor

Title VI requires the school to communicate with you in a language you understand. Axolotl asks for it before the meeting, not during.

Same for meals, accommodations, and school stability. You describe the situation; Axolotl handles which rule applies.

Most districts can't see who actually needs help.

School data systems are usually stateless. An absence or a missed meal fires a notification, but nothing connects it to what's actually going on, and nobody closes the loop to make sure the support reaches the family. When a family tells Axolotl what's happening, the need becomes clear — and Axolotl does the work to actually resolve it.
